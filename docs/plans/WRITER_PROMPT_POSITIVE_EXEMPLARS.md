# Positive exemplars mined for the writer prompt (Session 371, item (a))

Candidate pool for **arm B** of the Opus-5 A/B/C test. Every sentence below is a real
in-house sentence, verified present in the **copy-edited** text of the guide named
(not just the writer output). Sources are the last month of production runs:
Ps 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, 71.

Independently-judged candidates come from the beta reader's §3 "Moments that landed"
(AHA / FELT / WIT tags) and §4 affective landing, across seven beta-read files —
preferred over my own taste, per the agenda's sourcing note.

**Guard (must ship with any exemplar block, copied from RULE 13):** these calibrate
the *move*, never the wording. Never quote them, echo their phrasing, or rebuild
their sentence-shapes on new material.

---

## RULE 7 — the blurry photograph: an abstract idea made concrete

**1. Ps 68 (beta: AHA)** — an abstract theme tracked as a physical circuit.
> The warrior's power, withheld until the poem is ready to hand it over, circulates
> from God to worshiper and home again. The victory anthem ends by arming the congregation.

*Why it works:* "עֹז as a theme" is exactly the blurry noun RULE 7 forbids. Instead the
power has a route — God → congregation → back — and the last sentence gives it a hand
to be put into.

**2. Ps 66 (beta: WIT)** — one abstraction ("divine omniscience") split into two felt
situations.
> Same eye, opposite emotional charge — the difference between being watched as a
> suspect and being watched over as a child.

**3. Ps 69** — the psalm's whole reversal, stated as two physical states.
> No foothold becomes a homeland.

## RULE 7b — the aphorism that EARNS its shape

RULE 7b's danger is the *balanced sentence*. So the exemplar cannot be the aphorism
alone — it must show the demonstration that pays for it. Each is quoted with its setup.

**1. Ps 70 (beta: AHA — "the thesis at its sharpest")**
> Psalm 40 places these lines after its earlier thanksgiving; Psalm 70 presents them
> independently and lets the last line hang. **The theology lives in the cut, not the wording.**

*Why it earns it:* the antithesis names a difference the previous sentence has just
put on the page — identical words, different boundary. Strip the rhythm and the
content survives.

**2. Ps 70 (beta: AHA — "I stopped and reread it")**
> The enemies seek my life, delight in my ruin, and say "Aha! Aha!" The faithful seek
> You, love Your salvation, and say "Extolled be God!" Neither group is described
> through a concrete deed. **Character here is appetite plus voice.**

*Why it earns it:* six enumerated data points precede the four-word compression. The
aphorism is a *summary of a completed count*, not a substitute for one.

**3. Ps 67 (beta: FELT)**
> Where the psalm treats the yield as theological evidence, Ibn Ezra lingers in the
> sensory delight of it… **The same earth, in the psalmist's hands a witness and in the
> poet's a courtier.**

*Why it earns it:* the two readings were both quoted in full immediately above; the
antithesis only labels a contrast the reader has already seen.

## RULE 8 — a routine fact handled in one honest clause

**1. Ps 67** — the purest option-(c) deferral in the corpus. One clause, zero inflation.
> On the lone סֶלָה here and at v.5 — and conspicuously *not* at the repeated refrain
> of v.6 — see v.6 below.

**2. Ps 71 v.5** — a word that genuinely does modest work here, said so plainly.
> מִנְּעוּרָי, "from my youth," is the first temporal anchor of the life-span argument.
> It returns at verse 17 in exactly the same form, and between the two occurrences the
> psalm will place old age. The word does modest work here and heavy work there.

**3. Ps 71 v.6** — a count, a link, and a stop.
> The final clause, בְּךָ תְהִלָּתִי תָמִיד, "in You is my praise continually," is the
> second of three occurrences of תָּמִיד in the psalm and quietly links the biography
> to the vow: the same "always" that describes the dependence describes the praise.

## RULE 8b — a commentator quotation that TRANSFORMS the verse

**1. Ps 71 v.4 — Malbim.** The admission test passed at full strength: an apparent
synonymous parallelism becomes a narrated event.
> יָד is the hand generally; כַּף is the hollow of the palm, the cupped part that closes.
> Malbim notices the escalation and reads it as a worsening situation rather than
> poetic variation: שכף הוא יותר מן היד, הגם שכבר תפסני בכפו, "for the palm is more than
> the hand — even though he has already seized me in his palm." The second line does not
> repeat the first; it reports that the grip has tightened while the sentence was being spoken.

*Why it passes:* thirty seconds ago the reader saw restatement. Now they see a clock.

**2. Ps 67 v.2 — Rashi (beta: WIT).** Compression: an abstract blessing formula turned
into weather in four Hebrew words.
> Rashi, characteristically concrete, refuses to let the image float: a shining face
> means לתת טל ומטר, "to give dew and rain" — the favor lands as weather.

**3. Ps 71 v.4 — Torah Temimah / *Kiddushin* 53a.** The protected category (rabbinic
afterlife), and a scene, not a gloss.
> מעשה באחד שחטף חלקו וחלק חברו והיו קוראין אותו בן חמצן עד יום מותו, "there was an
> incident concerning one who snatched his own portion and his fellow's portion, and
> they called him *ben ḥamtzan* — 'son of the grabber' — until the day of his death."
> … The psalm's rare enemy-word gets stripped of its cosmic scale and pinned onto one
> greedy priest.

**4. Ps 71 v.4 (RULE 8b run 1) — the IDEAL outcome: insight kept, citation dropped, room
NOT refilled.** Same observation as exemplar 1, one sentence, no name attached:
> מִ**כַּ**ף, with the מ of "from" prefixed to כַּף, is the hollow of the palm — the
> part that closes.

## RULE 3b-2 — grammar shown, never named

**1. Ps 71 v.7** — technique 1 (bold the letter) + technique 2 (say what it does), the
exact pair the rule asks for.
> The single letter **וְ** at the head of **וְ**אַתָּה is the "but" that swings the
> sentence around: whatever the many are reading in him, the reading he gives is different.

**2. Ps 71 v.9** — an aspect distinction with no aspectual vocabulary anywhere.
> כִּכְלוֹת כֹּחִי is a process, not an event: the little **כְּ** at the head of the
> word means "as," "in the course of" — as my strength is being used up.

**3. Ps 71 v.6** — technique 3 (demonstrate by contrast). A passive taught without the
word "passive."
> The psalmist does not say he chose to trust; he says he was *propped*, before he
> could choose anything.

**4. Ps 69 (beta: AHA — "you can't un-hear it")** — bolding used across a whole chain,
so the morpheme becomes audible.
> הַצִּילֵ**נִי**, עֲנֵ**נִי**, פְּדֵ**נִי** … then שֻׁלְחָ**נָם**, עֵינֵי**הֶם**,
> עֲלֵי**הֶם**. The ear registers the reversal — *me* becoming *them* — as a change of
> ending, before the mind has parsed a single curse. The suffix is the turn.

## Affective landing — three the beta reader rated `LANDING: found`

**1. Ps 69 v.20** (beta §4; "precise in a way that most biblical commentary never gets near")
> He is not asking, at this moment, to be rescued from the water or vindicated before
> the court. He is asking for one person to sit near him and move their head — to
> signal, wordlessly, *I see that this is terrible.* And he looks up, and the room is
> empty. No one who might have come does.

**2. Ps 70 v.6** (beta §3 FELT; §4 `LANDING: found`) — the landing as *undressing*, after
the apparatus.
> And here the analysis can rest. Strip away the doublet, the name-count, the frozen
> idiom, and what remains is the oldest prayer there is: *I have nothing, and I cannot
> wait. Come now.*

**3. Ps 68 introduction** (beta §4; "the one moment the guide stepped out from behind
its apparatus")
> If you have ever been the person without a place at anyone's table, this verse is
> doing something the storm cannot: it is turning the whole apparatus of cosmic power
> toward the one who has no one.

**Runner-up, Ps 71 v.9** — the shortest landing in the corpus, one flat sentence:
> There is a fear here that is not the fear of death, and the psalm is honest enough
> not to dress it up.
