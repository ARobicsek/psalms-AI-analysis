CONTRADICTED (12):
1. Who Holds the Verbs: "He rises (קוּם) and saves (לְהוֹשִׁיעַ, v. 10)."
   Claim: Verse 10 uses the cited verbal forms
   Finding: The verse has בְּקוּם, an infinitive construction, not the bare form קוּם printed in the parenthesis.
   Evidence — Psalms 76:10: "בְּקוּם־לַמִּשְׁפָּט אֱלֹהִים לְהוֹשִׁיעַ כׇּל־עַנְוֵי־אֶרֶץ"
   Suggested fix: He rises (בְּקוּם) and saves (לְהוֹשִׁיעַ, v. 10).

2. The Song of the Sea, Run Backwards: "Through v. 10 the poem speaks in completed past actions: He shattered, You made heard, God rose."
   Claim: God's rising in verse 10 is past-tense
   Finding: Verse 10 says 'when God rises' using an infinitive construction, not 'God rose'; verse 8 also contains a non-past verb. The description of everything through verse 10 as completed past action is too absolute.
   Evidence — Psalms 76:10: "בְּקוּם־לַמִּשְׁפָּט אֱלֹהִים לְהוֹשִׁיעַ כׇּל־עַנְוֵי־אֶרֶץ"
   Evidence — Psalms 76:8: "וּמִי־יַעֲמֹד לְפָנֶיךָ"
   Suggested fix: Through v. 10 the poem recalls completed acts—He shattered, You made judgment heard—alongside the question of who can stand and God's rising to judge.

3. Practical Kabbalah: "Almost everywhere else, that word names God's rebuke of the sea."
   Claim: גערה elsewhere almost always rebukes sea
   Finding: The noun גְּעָרָה repeatedly denotes interpersonal reproof, and rebukes of people occur elsewhere too. Sea-rebuke passages exist, but 'almost everywhere else' is not accurate.
   Evidence — Proverbs 13:1: "וְ֝לֵ֗ץ לֹא־שָׁמַ֥ע גְּעָרָֽה"
   Evidence — Proverbs 13:8: "וְ֝רָ֗שׁ לֹא־שָׁמַ֥ע גְּעָרָֽה"
   Evidence — Proverbs 17:10: "תֵּ֣חַת גְּעָרָ֣ה בְמֵבִ֑ין"
   Evidence — Psalms 119:21: "גָּ֭עַרְתָּ זֵדִ֣ים אֲרוּרִ֑ים"
   Suggested fix: Elsewhere, related language can name God's rebuke of the waters.
