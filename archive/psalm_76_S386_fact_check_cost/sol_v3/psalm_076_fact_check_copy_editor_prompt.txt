CONTRADICTED (20):
1. The Song of the Sea, Run Backwards: "Through v. 10 the poem speaks in completed past actions: He shattered, You made heard, God rose."
   Claim: Through verse 10 Psalm 76 speaks in completed past actions, including a completed past-tense verb meaning “God rose.”
   Finding: Verse 8 includes the imperfect יַעֲמֹד, and בְּקוּם in verse 10 is an infinitive construction, not a completed past-tense verb. The preceding past actions can still frame the scene.
   Evidence — Psalm 76:8, 10: "וּמִֽי־יַעֲמֹ֥ד לְפָנֶ֗יךָ ... בְּקוּם־לַמִּשְׁפָּ֥ט אֱלֹהִ֑ים לְהוֹשִׁ֖יעַ כׇּל־עַנְוֵי־אֶ֣רֶץ"
   Suggested fix: Through v. 10 the poem chiefly recalls completed actions—He shattered, You made judgment heard—before describing God rising to judge.

2. Practical Kabbalah: "Almost everywhere else, that word names God's rebuke of the sea."
   Claim: Elsewhere the noun גערה refers almost exclusively to God’s rebuke of the sea.
   Finding: The noun also describes human reproof and divine rebukes directed at people or associated with fire. Sea-rebuke is an attested use, not almost every other use.
   Evidence — Proverbs 13:1: "וְ֝לֵ֗ץ לֹא־שָׁמַ֥ע גְּעָרָֽה"
   Evidence — Isaiah 51:20: "בָּנַ֜יִךְ עֻלְּפ֥וּ ... הַֽמְלֵאִ֥ים חֲמַת־יְהֹוָ֖ה גַּעֲרַ֥ת אֱלֹהָֽיִךְ"
   Evidence — Isaiah 66:15: "וְגַעֲרָת֖וֹ בְּלַהֲבֵי־אֵֽשׁ"
   Suggested fix: Elsewhere, the word can name God's rebuke of the sea.

5. Verse 3: "*Salem* is Jerusalem's oldest name, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, "and Melchizedek, king of Salem" (Gen 14:18)."
   Claim: Salem is Jerusalem's oldest name.
   Finding: The earliest cited attestation uses a longer form of Jerusalem’s name, not simply Salem. The identification of Melchizedek’s Salem with Jerusalem is traditional, but it does not establish that Salem was the city’s oldest name.
   Evidence — Robert R. Cargill, “From Shalem to Jerusalem,” Biblical Archaeology Society (https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/first-person-from-shalem-to-jerusalem/): "An Egyptian execration (curse) text, dating to around 1800 B.C.E., lists Jerusalem’s name as Ru-ša-li-mum."
   Suggested fix: *Salem* is traditionally identified with Jerusalem, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, "and Melchizedek, king of Salem" (Gen 14:18).

   Claim: The Talmud attributes this question to King Hezekiah.
   Finding: Shabbat names Ḥizkiya without calling him king. The Ḥizkiya who gives Talmudic interpretations is a rabbinic sage, also encountered in a halakhic dispute with Rabbi Abbahu, not Judah's biblical king.
   Evidence — Shabbat 88a (https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat_88a): "אָמַר חִזְקִיָּה, מַאי דִּכְתִיב: ״מִשָּׁמַיִם הִשְׁמַעְתָּ דִּין אֶרֶץ יָרְאָה וְשָׁקָטָה״, אִם יָרְאָה — לָמָּה שָׁקְטָה?"
   Evidence — Pesachim 21b (https://www.sefaria.org/Pesachim_21b): "אָמַר חִזְקִיָּה: מִנַּיִן לְחָמֵץ בַּפֶּסַח שֶׁאָסוּר בַּהֲנָאָה? ... וּפְלִיגָא דְרַבִּי אֲבָהוּ."
   Suggested fix: Ḥizkiya, a Talmudic sage, asks: אם יראה למה שקטה ואם שקטה למה יראה, "if it feared, why was it still? And if it was still, why did it fear?"
