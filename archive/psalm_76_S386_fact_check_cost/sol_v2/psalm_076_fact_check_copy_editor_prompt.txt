CONTRADICTED (25):
1. The Song of the Sea, Run Backwards: "Through v. 10 the poem speaks in completed past actions: He shattered, You made heard, God rose."
   Claim: Through verse 10 the poem speaks in completed past actions, including a past-tense 'God rose' in verse 10.
   Finding: Verse 8 is a present-tense description, and verse 10's בְּקוּם is an infinitive construction ('when God rises'), not a completed-past verb.
   Evidence — Psalm 76:8, 10: "אַתָּ֤ה ׀ נ֥וֹרָא אַ֗תָּה ... בְּקוּם־לַמִּשְׁפָּ֥ט אֱלֹהִ֑ים"
   Suggested fix: Through v. 10 the poem chiefly recounts completed actions: He shattered, You made judgment heard; then God rises to judge.

2. Full psalm: "This is the custom in many Mizrachi and Sephardic (Edot HaMizrach) communities and in the custom of the Vilna Gaon."
   Claim: The stated communities and the Vilna Gaon's rite use Psalm 76 on that day.
   Finding: The documented Edot HaMizrach Sukkot practice names Psalms 42 and 43, whereas the Vilna Gaon's rite names Psalm 76. The sentence conflates those rites.
   Evidence — Shir Shel Yom, separate descriptions of the Vilna Gaon's and Edot HaMizrach rites (https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%9C_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9D): "בחג הסוכות (וחול המועד) אומרים את מזמורים מ״ב ו מ״ג בתהילים"
   Evidence — Shir Shel Yom for Sukkot, Vilna Gaon's rite (https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%9C_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9D_%D7%9C%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA): "יום טוב ראשון (פרק ע״ו)"
   Suggested fix: This is the custom in the rite of the Vilna Gaon.

3. Full psalm: "There God breaks Gog's bow and arrows "from your hand" and Israel burns the abandoned shields (Ezek 39:3, 9), the same bow, hand, and shield that vv. 4–6 of the psalm dispose of."
   Claim: Ezekiel 39:3 says God breaks Gog's bow and arrows.

8. Verse 3: "*Salem* is Jerusalem's oldest name, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, "and Melchizedek, king of Salem" (Gen 14:18)."
   Claim: Salem is Jerusalem's oldest name.
   Finding: The earliest cited attestations use a longer form of *Jerusalem*, not *Salem*. Scholars dispute whether the biblical Salem originally designated Jerusalem at all; calling it the city’s **oldest name** presents a disputed identification as established history.    Evidence — Torat Har Etzion, “Names of Jerusalem in the Tanakh” (https://etzion.org.il/en/tanakh/studies-tanakh/core-studies-tanakh/bechukotai-yom-yerushalayim-names-jerusalem-tanakh): "It seems, then, that Yerushalem is the original form."
   Evidence — Robert Cargill, “From Shalem to Jerusalem” (https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/first-person-from-shalem-to-jerusalem/): "I suggest that Shalem was never an early name for Jerusalem."
   Suggested fix: *Salem* is a biblical name traditionally identified with Jerusalem, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, "and Melchizedek, king of Salem" (Gen 14:18).

9. Verse 3: "At v. 9 the exact consonants י-ר-א-ה reappear, though there they mean "feared," not Abraham's "will see.""

   Finding: יָרְאָה has the consonants י־ר־א־ה, but Abraham's יִרְאֶה also has י־ר־א־ה. The forms do match consonantally; however, the proposed contrast between 'feared' and 'will see' is grammatically sound. Accordingly, the quoted texts do not contradict the claim.
