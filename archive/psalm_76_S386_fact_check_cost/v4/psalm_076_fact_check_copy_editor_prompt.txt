CONTRADICTED (18):
1. The Song of the Sea, Run Backwards: "Through v. 10 the poem speaks in completed past actions: He shattered, You made heard, God rose."
   Claim: All actions through verse 10 have completed-past verbal forms.
   Finding: Verse 8 describes God in direct present address; בְּקוּם in verse 10 is an infinitive construction, not a completed-past verb. The three examples do not establish an exclusively past-tense section.
   Evidence — Psalms 76:8, 10, Masoretic text: "אַתָּה ׀ נוֹרָא אַתָּה ... בְּקוּם־לַמִּשְׁפָּט אֱלֹהִים לְהוֹשִׁיעַ"
   Suggested fix: Through v. 10 the poem recounts a deliverance with past actions, present address, and God's rising to judge.

2. Practical Kabbalah: "Almost everywhere else, that word names God's rebuke of the sea."
   Claim: Elsewhere גְּעָרָה almost always refers to God rebuking sea.
   Finding: The noun also denotes human reproof, an enemy’s shout, and rebuke directed at Jerusalem’s people. Sea-rebuke is a significant parallel, not almost the whole usage.
   Evidence — Proverbs 13:1; 17:10: "וְלֵץ לֹא־שָׁמַע גְּעָרָה׃ ... תֵּחַת גְּעָרָה בְמֵבִין"
   Evidence — Isaiah 30:17; 51:20: "אֶלֶף אֶחָד מִפְּנֵי גַּעֲרַת אֶחָד ... בָּנַיִךְ עֻלְּפוּ ... גַּעֲרַת אֱלֹהָיִךְ׃"
   Suggested fix: Elsewhere, the word can name God's rebuke of the sea.
