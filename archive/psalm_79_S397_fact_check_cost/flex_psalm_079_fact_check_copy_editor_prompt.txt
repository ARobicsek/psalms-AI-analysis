CONTRADICTED (25):
1. Blood left uncovered: "When the sixty Hasideans were murdered in 161 BCE, 1 Maccabees 7:17 quoted the psalm as fulfilled scripture, but only from its second half: "the flesh of Your *ḥăsîdîm*.""
   Claim: The author quotes only the flesh phrase.
   Finding: The quotation continues through Psalm 79:3, including the blood and the absence of burial.
   Evidence — The Book of Maccabees I 7:16–17 (https://www.sefaria.org/The_Book_of_Maccabees_I_7:16-17): "ככתוב בשר חסידיך לחיתו ארץ, שפכו דמם כמים סביבות ירושלים ואין קובר."
   Suggested fix: Change “but only from its second half: ‘the flesh of Your ḥăsîdîm’” to “beginning with ‘the flesh of Your ḥăsîdîm’ and continuing through verse 3.”

2. The bargain at the center: "The rabbis explained the question with Lamentations 4:11, כִּלָּה ה׳ אֶת־חֲמָתוֹ, "the Lord has spent His fury," which goes on to say He poured out His anger and kindled a fire in Zion."
   Claim: Rabbis used Lamentations to explain verse 5’s question.
   Finding: The rabbinic question is why the heading says “psalm” rather than “lament,” not the “How long?” of verse 5.
   Evidence — Lamentations Rabbah 4:14 (https://www.sefaria.org/Lamentations_Rabbah_4:14): "לא הוה קרא צריך למימר אלא בכי לאסף, נהי לאסף, קינה לאסף, ומה אומר מזמור לאסף ... מזמר אני ששפך הקדוש ברוך הוא חמתו על העצים ועל האבנים"
   Suggested fix: Change “The rabbis explained the question” to “The rabbis answered a different question about the psalm’s heading.”

3. The psalm in other mouths: "The psalm is full of the Exodus: "inheritance" and "pasture" come from the Song of the Sea, and so does "the greatness of Your arm.""
   Claim: The Song of the Sea supplies “pasture.”
   Finding: The Song has inheritance and the greatness of God’s arm, but not the psalm’s pasture term or flock-of-pasture image.
   Evidence — Exodus 15:13, 16–17: "נֵהַ֥לְתָּ בְעׇזְּךָ֖ אֶל־נְוֵ֥ה קׇדְשֶֽׁךָ ... בִּגְדֹ֥ל זְרוֹעֲךָ֖ יִדְּמ֣וּ כָּאָ֑בֶן ... בְּהַ֣ר נַחֲלָֽתְךָ֔"
   Evidence — Psalms 79:13: "וְצֹ֥אן מַרְעִיתֶךָ֮"
   Suggested fix: Change “ ‘inheritance’ and ‘pasture’ come from the Song of the Sea, and so does ‘the greatness of Your arm’” to “ ‘inheritance’ and ‘the greatness of Your arm’ recall the Song of the Sea.”

4. Key verses: "The same pair ends every day of the Lithuanian and Polish Ashkenaz selichot and the Edot HaMizrach selichot."
   Claim: Pair ends Edot HaMizrach selichot.
   Finding: Unlike the Lithuanian and Polish texts, the Edot HaMizrach text continues with Shomer Yisrael after verse 9.
   Evidence — Selichot Edot HaMizrach (https://www.sefaria.org/Selichot_Edot_HaMizrach): "עזרנו אלהי ישענו על דבר כבוד שמך. והצילנו וכפר על חטאתינו למען שמך: שומר ישראל. שמר שארית ישראל. ואל יאבד ישראל"
   Suggested fix: The same pair ends every day of the Lithuanian and Polish Ashkenaz selichot; the Edot HaMizrach selichot also include it, but continue afterward.

12. Verse 4: "In later Hebrew a word spelled the same way, קִלּוּס, came to mean the opposite: praise, laudation."
   Claim: קֶלֶס and קִלּוּס are spelled identically
   Finding: קלס and קילוס differ in written vowel letters. Here spelling itself, rather than merely their shared consonantal root, is the claim.
   Evidence — Psalm 79:4, Masoretic text: "לַ֥עַג וָ֝קֶ֗לֶס"
   Evidence — Klein Dictionary, entries on קלס: "Derivatives: קֶלֶס ᴵᴵ, קִלּוּס ᴵᴵ"
   Suggested fix: Change “a word spelled the same way” to “a similarly spelled word.”

   Claim: Meiri reads only the Babylonian exile
   Finding: Meiri first proposes the long exile; he offers the first exile’s seeming length as an alternative. The guide presents his alternative as his sole reading.
   Evidence — Meiri on Psalm 79:5: "נראה שרמז על הגלות הארוך או שמא אף הראשון היה ארוך בעיניו וכמו שמצינו שאמ׳ על אותו הגלות ג״כ עד מתי אתה לא תרחם את ירושלם"
   Suggested fix: Change the opening to “Radak hears the ‘how long’ against the Babylonian exile; Meiri first suggests the long exile, or alternatively that even the Babylonian exile felt endless.”
