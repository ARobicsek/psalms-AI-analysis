The existing material on Psalm 27 is already rich: it traces the structural tension between confidence and lament, does careful lexical work on key verbs (לַחֲז֥וֹת / “to gaze,” וּלְבַקֵּ֥ר / “to inquire”), and uses ANE parallels (Baal’s *sukkah*, “seeing the face” of the god) to shift the psalm from vague piety into a courtroom-and-temple world. Verses 1–12 are well covered.

What’s missing is mainly at the edges. Verses 13–14—especially the unique dotted לוּלֵא (“lulei,” “unless”) and the double קַוֵּה אֶל־יְהוָה (“Wait for YHWH”)—need full treatment; they are where the psalm’s structure and theology come into focus. Some “aha” material from the deeper research has not yet been exploited: Qumran and Septuagint variants (e.g., the LXX past tense in v. 6, the LXX’s omission of לוּלֵא), the Ugaritic lexeme behind וִיפֵ֥חַ חָמָֽס (“a witness of violence”), and the close kinship with Psalm 102’s cry אַל־תַּסְתֵּר פָּנֶיךָ מִמֶּנִּי (“Do not hide Your face from me”) are especially promising. 

The liturgical section is informative but needs to be organized under the requested headings and sharpened to show how liturgical use *reinterprets* the psalm. Hebrew is generally paired with English, but care will be needed to maintain this rigor throughout, especially with added cross-references and prayer texts. Technical terms like *aposiopesis*, *inclusio*, and *form criticism* should be briefly defined for non-specialists. Overall, the task is less correction than integration and completion: extending the same level of insight to the psalm’s ending and tying in the broader textual and reception history.
