**Governing idea**
Psalm 76 pictures God as a predator in residence at Zion whose already-completed violence, done on behalf of the powerless, now demands a response: the same dread that froze the enemy's hands can instead fill a worshipper's hands with tribute.

**What it says the poem does and evokes**
The essay reads the dwelling-words of v. 3 as a lion's lair and treats the psalm's geography as a coiled spring. It argues that the structure is grammatical: completed perfects before the second Selah, then imperatives and imperfects. It reframes the battle as a heavenly lawsuit for "the lowly," culminating in fear-vocabulary and the covenant name arriving together.

**Strongest insights**
- "Both of the psalm's dwelling-words belong to the same bestiary." [Bible]
- Hughes's jaguar: "confinement can be the least interesting fact about a dangerous animal." [beyond the Bible]
- "the hinge is audible in the liturgical pause." [poetic craft]
- "The cosmic tribunal is doing what the local one was supposed to do and did not." [Bible]
- "unable to lift a hand, or carrying something." [poetic craft]

**Best writing**
"The psalm's answer to imperial power is not a stronger empire. It is a court that finally sits."

**Weak spots**
- "Two panels of roughly equal weight, each closed by סֶלָה" is followed by four units. The last unit (vv. 11–13) has no Selah, so the floor plan is hard to follow.
- The second-person appeal ("If you have ever watched a case go to someone who could afford it…") shifts into sermon register.
- "The deep unconsciousness of surgery and storm" alludes to Genesis 2 and Jonah 1 without naming them, which may leave readers puzzled.
- "Which brings us back to the hands" is odd because hands have not been discussed before.
- "God roars" overtranslates גַּעֲרָה, which means rebuke or blast.

**Claims worth checking**
- **Sukkot liturgy.** The claim fits the Vilna Gaon's custom. Whether it holds generally for "Sephardic rites" is worth confirming.
- **מְעוֹנָה as a big cat's den.** "Specifically" overstates the case. Job 37:8 uses the word for beasts' dens generally.
- **Jeremiah 25:38.** The subject is God likened to a lion, not "a lion." This actually strengthens the essay's point.
- **"Every finite verb… is a perfect."** Verse 3 opens with a wayyiqtol. Verse 8's יַעֲמֹד is an imperfect and falls before the second Selah. Verse 5 has no finite verb.
- **"Everywhere else" דִּין // מִשְׁפָּט targets the poor.** Counterexamples include Job 36:17 (the wicked) and Psalm 9:5 (the speaker's own case).
- **"Four times the same sound closes the line."** Only v. 12's לַמּוֹרָא actually ends a line. The other three occurrences fall mid-line.
- **Hughes, "The Jaguar."** The 1957 date and the quotation match *The Hawk in the Rain*.
