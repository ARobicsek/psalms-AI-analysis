**Governing idea**
Psalm 76 is a victory hymn with no human victors. God alone acts, war itself is broken, and the salvation is claimed for "all the lowly of the earth" rather than for any "us."

**What it says the poem does and evokes**
The essay traces a four-panel movement outward from Judah to Zion to heaven to the whole earth. It argues that the verbs themselves strip human agency until it returns only as worship. It hears the Song of the Sea and Hosea 2 being revised, and reads Zion as a lion's lair whose lion hunts predators instead of the poor.

**Strongest insights**
- "Before v. 11 the mighty get exactly one verb that takes an object, and it comes negated" [poetic craft]
- "Where Exodus calls God a warrior, here God breaks מִלְחָמָה, 'war'… the one item on the list that cannot be held in a hand" [Bible]
- "Psalm 76 gives us the one lair whose lion hunts the predators" (via Ps 10:9, Jer 25:38, Nah 2:12) [Bible]
- "Psalm 76:7 says 'God of Jacob' and stops," set against Psalm 46's "with us… for us" [Bible]
- Rashi and Radak "each supplies the possessive the poet held back" [tradition]

**Best writing**
"The only thing these heroes try to do in the entire poem is locate their own bodies. They fail."
"So a city whose name means 'peace' turns out to house a predator."

**Weak spots**
- "Every verb in the poem that reaches out and acts upon something belongs to Him" is stated absolutely. Later the essay concedes human transitive verbs in vv. 11–12 ("make vows," "bring tribute"), so the rule needs the "before v. 11" limit from the start.
- "No 'I,' no 'we,' no 'our'" is accurate, but v. 12 does address humans directly ("your God"), which slightly complicates the picture of total absence.
- "Readers often call this… and they are right. Less often noticed…" is throat-clearing.
- The *Don Giovanni* passage runs long for its payoff, which is essentially a contrast ("God does not even extend a hand").
- "Nearly every victory song ever written" is a sweeping, sentimental generalization.

**Claims worth checking**
- **Passover custom:** the claim that Psalm 76 is recited "on the seventh day of Passover." Customs for the festival psalm vary, and I cannot confirm this one.
- **Radak's self-revision:** the claim that Radak "admits on v. 13 that he once read the psalm as Assyrian and has since reassigned it to Gog" is an unusual, specific statement. Verify it against the commentary text.
- **Rashi's wording:** the exact phrase "לְהוֹשִׁיעַ חִזְקִיָּהוּ וְסִיעָתוֹ" should be confirmed. The Hezekiah reading itself is plausible.
- **Verified:** the Exodus 15, Hosea 2:20, Jer 25:38, Nah 2:12, Ps 10:9 and Ps 46 quotations, LXX ἐν εἰρήνῃ, the Da Ponte lines, and the single occurrence of יהוה all check out.
