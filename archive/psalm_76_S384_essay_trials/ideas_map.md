## What each essay is built around

- **A** — God stops armed force without a human victor, and the song withholds “us” so its saving judgment can reach the lowly.
- **B** — A heavenly court hears the powerless, while divine fear leaves attackers unable to use their hands and worshipers able to bring gifts.
- **C** — A song apparently about disarming an army moves from God’s dangerous lair and the camp’s silence to rescue and worship.
- **D** — The distribution of verbs makes God the actor, turns threatening predators into immobilized prey, and leaves humans thanks rather than combat.
- **E** — A victory without Israelite prowess becomes a judgment whose purpose is the protection of the lowly.
- **F** — Beside Assyrian claims of royal splendour and tribute, the psalm assigns effective power and the final gifts to God.
- **G** — The feared inhabitant of Zion’s covert protects the lowly, and effective human speech returns as vows rather than threats.
- **H** — The loss of a soldier’s hands gives way to a heavenly verdict and, finally, to people keeping their word.
- **I** — A song with neither battle nor “we” transfers agency from warriors to God, then returns human hands to gift-bearing.
- **J** — The apparent cage of besieged Jerusalem becomes God’s covert, from which judgment saves the lowly and calls for vows.
- **K** — Psalm 76 reworks the Song of the Sea so that the warrior God breaks war, while a victory without “we” belongs to the lowly.
