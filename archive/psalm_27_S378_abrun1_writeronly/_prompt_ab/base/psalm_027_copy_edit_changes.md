## Changes
*For exact before/after text, see [psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md](psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md).*

1. [7] **Introduction**: Changed “The verb of verse 4 … recurs in the psalm exactly once more” to “The first-person verb of verse 4 … recurs in the psalm exactly once more.” — The root בקש also appears in verse 8 in the plural imperative בַּקְּשׁוּ, so the original wording overstated the case; the first-person form אֲבַקֵּשׁ does recur only once more.

2. [7] **Introduction**: Changed “The Masoretes marked the first word” to “In the Masoretic text the first word is marked.” — The dots belong to the Masoretic text, but attributing their creation directly to “the Masoretes” overstates what can be said.

3. [9e] **Introduction**: Changed “Across the ancient Near East, the god of light was the god of justice” to “In the ancient Near East, the god of light could also be the god of justice.” — The paragraph’s evidence named Shamash specifically; the original sentence generalized too broadly from that example.

4. [7] **Verse 1**: Corrected Micah 7:8 from “the LORD will be a light to me” to “the LORD is a light to me,” and adjusted the follow-up claim accordingly. — The Hebrew אוֹר לִי is not a future-tense form; the original translation was inaccurate.

5. [7, 9b] **Verse 2**: Rewrote the Jeremiah 8:4 comparison to say that falling is ordinarily followed by rising, and that Psalm 27 pointedly omits such rising. — The original claimed the opposite of Jeremiah’s rhetorical point (“if they fall, do they not rise?”), which reversed the evidence.

6. [7] **Verse 4**: Corrected “It appears only six times” to “It appears only seven times.” — The occurrences cited in the paragraph itself total seven, not six.

7. [7, 9g] **Verses 5–6**: Softened “the altar as the one place a warrant could not reach” to “the altar as a place a warrant did not easily reach.” — Ancient asylum practice was real but not absolute; the original phrasing overstated it.

8. [10] **Verse 7**: Replaced “catena” with “chain.” — “Catena” is a technical term many educated readers would not confidently define, and the plain English equivalent carried the point without jargon.

9. [7, 9f] **Verse 8**: Removed the Job prooftext explanation about לְ meaning “in the place of” and replaced it with a direct explanation of Rashi’s reading of לְךָ as “on Your behalf.” — The original citation and translation were inaccurate and did not clearly support the conclusion, leaving the logic opaque.

10. [10] **Verse 10**: Glossed “merism” as “two poles naming a whole.” — This is a technical literary term that needed explanation on first use for non-specialist readers.

11. [7, 9b] **Verse 10**: Removed the Job “house of assembly” example from the discussion of אסף. — The cited verse does not use the root אסף and so did not actually support the lexical argument being made.

12. [10] **Verse 11**: Replaced “The vocabulary turns sapiential” with “The vocabulary turns to wisdom-instruction.” — “Sapiential” is specialized terminology; the revised wording preserves the point in accessible English.

13. [10] **Verse 12**: Replaced “is a crux” with “is difficult.” — “Crux” is technical scholarly jargon that was unnecessary here.

14. [9c] **Verse 12**: Softened “The Ugaritic reading is right” to “The Ugaritic proposal may be right, and if so…” — The Ugaritic comparison is a plausible interpretation, not a demonstrated certainty; the hedge now matches the evidence.

15. [7] **Verse 12**: Changed “every modern translation follows” to “most modern translations follow.” — The original was too absolute for a broad claim about modern translations.

16. [7] **Verse 13**: Changed “The Masoretes marked the word” to “The word is marked in the Masoretic text.” — As in the introduction, this avoids attributing the origin of the dots more specifically than the evidence warrants.

17. [9b] **Verse 14**: Recast the discussion of וְיַאֲמֵץ לִבֶּךָ to attribute the “He will strengthen your heart” reading specifically to Ibn Ezra. — The original presented a disputed parsing as if it were simply the grammar of the verse; the revision restores the interpretive basis.

18. [7] **Verse 14**: Removed the Arabic *quwwa* claim and softened the etymological sentence to “Its cognates have been connected with cord and tension.” — The Arabic comparison was not secure enough to state flatly, and the broader claim needed hedging.
