## Changes
*For exact before/after text, see [psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md](psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md).*

1. [6, 9e] **Introduction**: Changed “made the identical reduction” to “made a similar reduction” in the Rabia al-Adawiyya comparison.  
   The original overstated the parallel; the two texts share a specific move, but “identical” claimed more than the evidence supported.

2. [7, 9b] **Introduction**: Softened “The temple was where the falsely accused went, and to ‘seek the face’ of a king was to seek an audience and a verdict” to “could be” in both clauses.  
   The original stated a contested historical/legal reconstruction as an established fact; the argument works better when flagged at the level the evidence supports.

3. [7, 9b] **Introduction**: Replaced “He is not let in” with “The poem never shows him being let in.”  
   The original turned the poem’s lack of narrated resolution into a claimed event; the correction keeps the interpretive point while matching what the text actually gives.

4. [10] **Introduction**: Added a brief gloss for “Masoretes” (“the medieval transmitters of the Hebrew text”).  
   “Masoretes” is a technical textual term many educated readers may not confidently define on first encounter.

5. [7] **Liturgical — Key verses and phrases**: Revised “which had already bound light to face in verse 1” to “which had already bound God’s light to the seeking of his face.”  
   Verse 1 mentions light but not face; the original wording mislocated the connection within the psalm.

6. [10] **Liturgical — Key verses and phrases**: Replaced “incipit” with “opening words.”  
   “Incipit” is a technical literary term not needed for the point and was better expressed in plain language.

7. [7] **Liturgical — Key verses and phrases**: Removed “verbatim” from the claim that the Kol Nidrei refrain lifts the opening epithets.  
   The piyyut adapts the verse rather than reproducing it exactly, so “verbatim” was too strong.

8. [10] **Liturgical — Key verses and phrases**: Replaced “turned into a vocative” with “turned into direct address,” and glossed “piyyut” as “liturgical poem” on first use.  
   “Vocative” is a grammatical term that needed either glossing or replacement; “piyyut” also benefits from a brief first-use gloss for non-specialists.

9. [9c] **Liturgical — Practical Kabbalah**: Softened “to have drawn the two together in amulet practice” to “to have perhaps drawn the two together in amulet practice.”  
   The proposed link between אוֹרִי and אוריאל is plausible but conjectural; it needed an honest hedge.

10. [10] **Verse 1**: Replaced “The genitive מָעוֹז־חַיַּי” with “The phrase מָעוֹז־חַיַּי.”  
   “Genitive” is grammatical jargon here; the interpretive point did not depend on the label.

11. [9b] **Verse 2**: Changed “people who oppose him for three different reasons at once” to “encirclement in moral, legal, and personal terms at once.”  
   The original inferred motives the verse does not state; the corrected wording stays with the actual evidence given by the three labels.

12. [10] **Verse 4**: Replaced “three infinitives” with “three verbal phrases.”  
   The grammatical label was more technical than necessary for the audience, and the point did not depend on it.

13. [7, 9g] **Verse 5**: Corrected “an actual boy in an actual attic” to “an actual boy in an actual room.”  
   The Joash narrative does not place him in an attic; “room” preserves the concreteness without introducing a false detail.

14. [7, 9b] **Verse 6**: Changed “an offering made to a noise” to “an offering accompanied by a noise.”  
   The original phrasing misdescribed the ritual relation; the sacrifices are accompanied by *teruah*, not offered *to* it.

15. [7] **Verse 7**: Revised the word-order comment so that קוֹלִי is said to come before אֶקְרָא, not simply “before the verb.”  
   In the Hebrew line, קוֹלִי does not precede שְׁמַע; the corrected sentence identifies the actual prominence without misstating the syntax.

16. [7] **Verse 8**: Corrected the Septuagint citation and the explanation that followed it, changing the claim that the plural became “a first-person past tense” to the broader and accurate “a past tense.”  
   The original Greek quotation and its summary did not accurately reflect the LXX wording; the revision preserves the analytical point while matching the text more closely.

17. [9b] **Verse 9**: Replaced “denying each item in order” with “drawing on the standard sequence of covenantal withdrawal and denying each item.”  
   The psalm’s order does not match Deuteronomy’s sequence exactly, so “in order” overstated the correspondence.

18. [7, 9f] **Verse 10**: Simplified the discussion of אסף by removing the unsupported claim that it is “the technical term in Near Eastern law for taking in an exposed child,” and recast it as a verb for taking someone in under protection.  
   The original made a specialized legal claim without showing the evidence; the interpretation is sound without that overreach.

19. [9f] **Verse 10**: Softened “Jonathan Sacks returned to this line more often than to any other verse of the psalm” to “returned to this line repeatedly.”  
   The comparative claim was too broad and unsourced; the revision keeps the point without asserting more than can be shown.

20. [9f] **Verse 10**: Changed “this verse became the line survivors’ orphans said” to “this verse became a line that survivors’ orphans could say.”  
   The original asserted a specific postwar usage without support; the revised sentence preserves the historical poignancy without overclaiming documented practice.

21. [10] **Verse 12**: Replaced “the participle יָפִיחַ” with “the form יָפִיחַ.”  
   The technical grammatical label was not needed for the interpretive point.

22. [7] **Verse 12**: Changed “The Greek play resolves it by inventing a jury” to “by convening a jury.”  
   Aeschylus does not “invent” juries in the literal sense; the corrected verb keeps the contrast while avoiding factual overstatement.

23. [10] **Verse 13**: Changed “The Peshitta agrees” to “The Syriac Peshitta agrees.”  
   “Peshitta” is a technical term that benefits from a brief identifying gloss on first use.

24. [7, 9b] **Verse 14**: Replaced “What they are told is a quotation” with “What they are told is familiar commissioning language,” and recast the explanation around חֲזַק / אֱמָץ as formulaic courage-language.  
   Psalm 27:14 evokes the Joshua commissioning idiom but does not quote it exactly; the original wording overstated the textual relationship.

25. [7] **Verse 14**: Changed “could not publish at all” to “could not publish freely at home” for Miroslav Holub after 1968.  
   The original was too absolute; Holub’s publication history was constrained, not literally nonexistent in every context.
