## Changes
*For exact before/after text, see [psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md](psalm_027_copy_edit_diff.md).*

1. [7, 9(e)] **Introduction**: Changed "a vowed sacrifice with trumpets" to "a sacrifice with acclamation."  
   The verse being summarized (v. 6) does not mention a vow, and תְּרוּעָה is an acclamatory shout/blast rather than explicitly "trumpets" in the text.

2. [7, 9(b), 10] **The same sentence in two moods**: Replaced "identical grammatical shape" / "same patient" / "asks for exactly the same things" with clearer wording about shared verb-plus-**נִי** architecture and the same God-to-me action.  
   The original overstated the grammatical identity of third-person statements and second-person pleas, used unexplained technical jargon ("patient"), and claimed an exact sameness of content that the listed verbs do not support.

3. [3] **The same sentence in two moods**: Changed "The rare shared verb performs an equation" to "The rare shared verb draws an equation."  
   The effect comes from lexical repetition, not from form "performing" meaning in a stronger mimetic sense.

4. [9(c)] **What hiding is for**: Changed "the same poet-tradition" to "a closely related psalm" in the comparison with Psalm 13:3.  
   The original implied a specific genetic relationship the shared vocabulary does not prove; the comparison itself remains useful without overclaiming.

5. [10] **One thing**: Added a brief gloss to "three infinitives" as "three 'to' verbs."  
   "Infinitive" is a technical grammatical term; the gloss keeps the point accessible for non-specialists.

6. [2, 9(c)] **The sentence he could not finish**: Revised "The Greek translators fixed it by deleting the word" to "The Greek version smooths it by leaving out the word."  
   Later discussion correctly allows that the Greek may reflect a different Vorlage rather than an editorial deletion, so the earlier wording was too definite.

7. [7, 9(b)] **Liturgical — Practical Kabbalah**: Recast the Uriel sentence so only אוֹרִי is linked directly to אוריאל, while אוֹרִי and יִשְׁעִי together are said to suit the psalm's protective use.  
   The original claim that both epithets "yield" the angelic name אוריאל was not linguistically supportable.

8. [10, 11] **Verse 1**: Replaced "hapax in this inflection" with "nearly unique in this form," and rewrote "load-bearing words" as "the words on which Psalm 27's confidence rests."  
   "Hapax" and "inflection" were unnecessary jargon here, and "load-bearing" is a banned house-style phrase that had to be recast rather than retained.

9. [9(b)] **Verse 2**: Softened Malbim's reading from "the more likely one" to "a newly frightening possibility."  
   Verse 12 may make the conspiratorial reading evocative, but it does not prove Malbim's construal is more likely than the plain sense.

10. [10, 9(b)] **Verse 3**: Replaced "The demonstrative has no clear antecedent" with "The word זֹאת, 'this,' has no clear antecedent," and changed "ordinarily points" to "can point."  
   The first change removes unexplained grammatical terminology; the second scales back an overstatement about the normal force of זֹאת.

11. [9(b)] **Verse 5**: Softened "צפן does not simply mean conceal; it means to hide because the thing is precious" to "צפן can mean to hide something away because it is precious."  
   The examples support that nuance, but not as an absolute meaning in every instance.

12. [10] **Verse 9**: Replaced "are not a doublet" with "are not mere repetition," and replaced "The enjambment across..." with "The line break across...."  
   Both "doublet" and "enjambment" are technical literary terms not needed for the point being made.

13. [9(f), 7] **Verse 10**: Removed the opaque Boaz reference and revised "legal reception into a household" to "reception into a household," with Judges 19:15 as the explicit evidence.  
   The original sentence cited Boaz without explaining the relevance and described the usage too narrowly; the revised wording states the point the given evidence actually supports.

14. [7, 9(b)] **Verse 10**: Changed "the word for what they do — turning the face — is the word this psalm has just spent a verse begging God not to do" to "that image answers the psalm's own fear that God might hide His face."  
   The original claimed a lexical identity that is not there: Rashi's image is face-turning, while the psalm speaks of God hiding His face.

15. [7] **Verse 11**: Changed "recur in the same order" to "recur in close succession."  
   In Psalm 5:9 the same elements appear, but not in the exact order claimed.

16. [9(e)] **Verse 12**: Changed "This is a capital proceeding" to "This is a judicial proceeding."  
   False witnesses do indicate a legal setting, but the text does not establish that the case is specifically capital.

17. [2, 9(c)] **Verse 12**: Changed "Aeschylus knew this convergence" to "Aeschylus reaches a similar convergence."  
   The original wording suggested a direct relationship that the paragraph itself later denies by calling the resemblance independent.

18. [10] **Verse 13**: Added glosses for *puncta extraordinaria* and *aposiopesis*.  
   Both are technical terms that an educated non-specialist may not know; brief inline explanations preserve the insight without assuming specialist vocabulary.

19. [7] **Verse 14**: Changed "the only one addressed to a 'you'" to "the only one addressed to a human 'you'."  
   The psalm contains many second-person addresses to God; the distinctive feature of v. 14 is that the addressee is the self, not the deity.
