## Modern Jewish Liturgical Use (Psalm 78)

*Texts searched (Sefaria): the Ashkenaz, Sefard (Hasidic) and Edot HaMizrach siddurim; the Chabad weekday siddur; the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim of all three rites; the full selichot (Ashkenaz, Lithuanian and Polish; Edot HaMizrach); the Ashkenaz kinnot and the Edot HaMizrach Tisha B'Av order; two Haggadot; Ma'avar Yabbok, Seder Ma'amadot, Tikkun HaKlali, Perek Shirah, Yizkor, Akdamut, Keter Malkhut and the Azharot. Not searched: festival machzorim for Pesach, Shavuot and Sukkot beyond what the siddurim carry, and the Yemenite, Italian, Romaniote and other rites. A place missing below may still exist: nothing here shows that a verse is said ONLY somewhere, or in 'certain' services.*

### v. 13: בָּקַע יָם וַיַּעֲבִירֵם
**Where:** In *Emet VeEmunah*, the first blessing after the Shema at Ma'ariv. The catalogue finds it in the weekday and Friday-night services of the Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad siddurim. The same blessing is reprinted in the High Holiday machzorim for the evenings of Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and Motza'ei Yom Kippur. The prayer takes only the two words בּוֹקֵעַ יָם, "who split the sea."
**The text:** מִי כָמֹכָה בָּאֵלִם יְהֹוָה... נוֹרָא תְהִלֹּת עֹשֵׂה פֶלֶא: מַלְכוּתְךָ רָאוּ בָנֶיךָ בּוֹקֵעַ יָם לִפְנֵי משֶׁה זֶה אֵלִי עָנוּ וְאָמְרוּ: יְהֹוָה יִמְלֹךְ לְעֹלָם וָעֶד
"Who is like You among the mighty, O LORD… awesome in praises, working wonders! Your children beheld Your sovereignty as You split the sea before Moses; 'This is my God!' they responded, and said: 'The LORD shall reign for ever and ever.'"
**Note:** This is an allusion to the event the psalm narrates, not a quotation of the psalm. The prayer's participle with "before Moses" is equally close to Isaiah 63:12 (בּוֹקֵעַ מַיִם מִפְּנֵיהֶם). The phrase serves as the standard diction for the crossing, set inside the blessing's retelling of the Song at the Sea.

### v. 38: וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן
**Where:** The whole verse is recited, in every rite catalogued, at several fixed points.
- **In the Kedushah de-Sidra (*Uva LeTzion / Ve'atah Kadosh*).** It belongs to the chain of verses that follows the Aramaic Targum of the Kedushah. This is said:
  - at weekday Shacharit;
  - at Shabbat Mincha;
  - on Rosh Chodesh;
  - on Motza'ei Shabbat, after *Vihi Noam*;
  - in the Rosh Hashanah Mincha and Yom Kippur Neilah (Yom Kippur Mincha in Edot HaMizrach);
  - on Tisha B'Av night and day in the Edot HaMizrach order.

  It appears in the Ashkenaz, Sefard, Chabad and Edot HaMizrach texts.
- **In Pesukei Dezimra, twice.** These are found in all four rites, in the weekday and Shabbat services (and reprinted in the High Holiday machzorim):
  - in the anthology of psalm verses appended to *Hodu* (1 Chr 16:8–36), immediately after the two *Romemu* verses;
  - as the penultimate verse of *Yehi Chevod*.
- **Opening weekday Ma'ariv.** It is said before *Barchu* in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach (after the half-Kaddish in Edot HaMizrach). In Chabad it opens the service before *Shir HaMa'alot, Hineh Barchu*. The weekday Ma'ariv that ends a holy day carries it as well:
  - Motza'ei Yom Kippur (Ashkenaz, Sefard);
  - Motza'ei Rosh Hashanah (Edot HaMizrach);
  - Tisha B'Av night (Edot HaMizrach).
- **In Tachanun.** It opens the long *Vehu Rachum* said on Monday and Thursday. The rubric "לשני וחמישי" stands in the Chabad text, and the Ashkenaz and Sefard sections carry the same Monday/Thursday heading. In the Edot HaMizrach weekday Shacharit it falls within a chain of verses in the confession (Vidui) section. The same chain is repeated in that siddur's orders for the Fast of Gedalya, the Tenth of Tevet, the Fast of Esther and the Seventeenth of Tammuz.
- **In the house of mourning.** In the Edot HaMizrach siddur's order for the days of shiva, it is said after Isaiah 25:8 and 26:19 and before the comforters' "May you be comforted from heaven."
- **At the symbolic lashes (*malkot*) on the eve of Yom Kippur.** The Edot HaMizrach and Sefard Yom Kippur machzorim have the one administering the lashes recite the verse three times. Its 13 words, three times over, give one word for each of the 39 strokes.

**The text:**
- **Uva LeTzion:** יְהֹוָה אֱלֺהֵי אַבְרָהָם יִצְחָק וְיִשְׂרָאֵל אֲבוֹתֵינוּ שָׁמְרָה זֹאת לְעֹלָם לְיֵצֶר מַחְשְׁבוֹת לְבַב עַמֶּךָ וְהָכֵן לְבָבָם אֵלֶיךָ: וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן וְלֺא יַשְׁחִית... כִּי אַתָּה אֲדֹנָי טוֹב וְסַלָּח
  "LORD, God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel our fathers, keep this for ever as the inclination of the thoughts of Your people's heart, and direct their hearts to You. And He, being compassionate, atones iniquity and does not destroy… For You, Lord, are good and forgiving."
- **Hodu:** רוֹמְמוּ יְהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ וְהִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לְהַר קָדְשׁוֹ כִּי קָדוֹשׁ יְהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ: וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן... אַתָּה יְהֹוָה לֹא־תִכְלָא רַחֲמֶיךָ מִמֶּנִּי
  "Exalt the LORD our God and bow down at His holy mountain, for holy is the LORD our God. And He, being compassionate, atones iniquity… You, LORD, will not withhold Your compassion from me."
- **Yehi Chevod:** כִּי לֹא יִטּשׁ יְהֹוָה עַמּוֹ וְנַחֲלָתוֹ לֹא יַעֲזֹב: וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן... יְהֹוָה הוֹשִׁיעָה הַמֶּלֶךְ יַעֲנֵנוּ בְיוֹם־קָרְאֵנוּ
  "For the LORD will not abandon His people nor forsake His inheritance. And He, being compassionate… LORD, save! May the King answer us on the day we call."
- **Before Barchu (Edot HaMizrach):** וְאִמְרוּ אָמֵן. וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן וְלֹא־יַשְׁחִית... יְהֹוָה הוֹשִׁיעָה הַמֶּלֶךְ יַעֲנֵנוּ בְיוֹם־קָרְאֵנוּ: ואומר החזן: בָּרְכוּ אֶת יְהֹוָה הַמְּבֹרָךְ
  "…and say Amen. And He, being compassionate, atones iniquity and does not destroy… LORD, save! May the King answer us on the day we call. The cantor says: Bless the LORD, the blessed One."
- **Tachanun (Chabad):** לשני וחמישי וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן וְלֹא־יַשְׁחִית... אַתָּה יְיָ לֹא־תִכְלָא רַחֲמֶיךָ מִמֶּנּוּ, חַסְדְּךָ וַאֲמִתְּךָ תָּמִיד יִצְּרוּנוּ
  "For Monday and Thursday: And He, being compassionate… You, LORD, will not withhold Your compassion from us; may Your kindness and truth always guard us."
- **Mourning (Edot HaMizrach):** וָאָרֶץ רְפָאִים תַּפִּיל׃ וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן... יְהֹוָה הוֹשִׁיעָה הַמֶּלֶךְ יַעֲנֵנוּ בְיוֹם־קָרְאֵנוּ: ואומרים המנחמים: תְּנֻחֲמוּ מִן הַשָּׁמַיִם
  "…and the earth shall give up the shades. And He, being compassionate… LORD, save! The comforters say: May you be comforted from heaven."
- **Malkot (Sefard machzor rubric):** והמלקה אומר פסוק והוא רחום עד ולא יעיר כל חמתו ג"פ, שביחד יש בו ל"ט תיבות כנגד ל"ט מלקות
  "The one administering the lashes says the verse 'And He, being compassionate' through 'and does not arouse all His wrath' three times, which together have 39 words, corresponding to the 39 lashes."

**Note:**
- The verse is lifted out of the psalm's narrative of Israel's rebellion in the wilderness. Everywhere it serves as a free-standing plea for atonement.
- Its place at the opening of Ma'ariv is traditionally explained by the absence of an evening sacrifice to atone for sins. The Ashkenaz siddur's rubric, from Tur OC 235, ties Ma'ariv to the burning of the afternoon offering's limbs through the night.
- The Ashkenaz Tachanun rubric gives a midrashic origin for the Monday/Thursday *Vehu Rachum*: three elders cast adrift in three ships each composed part of it.

### v. 49: יְשַׁלַּח בָּם חֲרוֹן אַפּוֹ
**Where:** In the Pesach Haggadah, in the Maggid section, as the prooftext for Rabbi Eliezer's teaching that each Egyptian plague was fourfold. It appears in both Haggadot searched, in the Edot HaMizrach Haggadah and in the Sefard siddur's Haggadah. The Sefard siddur also prints it in the Haggadah read on Shabbat HaGadol.
**The text:** רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֲר אוֹמֵר: מִנַּיִן שֶׁכָּל מַכָּה וּמַכָּה... הָיְתָה שֶׁל אַרְבַּע מַכּוֹת? שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: יְשַׁלַּח בָּם חֲרוֹן אַפּוֹ, עֶבְרָה וָזַעַם וְצָרָה, מִשְׁלַחַת מַלְאֲכֵי רָעִים. עֶבְרָה - אַחַת, וָזַעַם - שְׁתַּיִם, וְצָרָה - שָׁלשׁ
"Rabbi Eliezer says: How do we know that each plague… consisted of four plagues? As it says: 'He sent against them His burning anger, wrath, fury and trouble, a band of evil messengers.' Wrath, one; fury, two; trouble, three…"
**Note:** This is a midrashic prooftext in a text read aloud at the Seder, not a verse recited as prayer. The midrash counts the psalm's nouns to multiply the plagues: forty in Egypt and two hundred at the sea.

### v. 54: וַיְבִיאֵם אֶל־גְּבוּל קָדְשׁוֹ
**Where:** In Pirkei Avot 6:10, the mishnah of the five "acquisitions" of God, as the second prooftext for the Temple. It appears in the Edot HaMizrach siddur's Shabbat Mishnah study.
**The text:** בֵּית־הַמִּקְדָּשׁ קִנְיָן אֶחָד מִנַּיִן, דִּכְתִיב: מָכוֹן לְשִׁבְתְּךָ פָּעַלְתָּ יְהֹוָה... וְאוֹמֵר: וַיְבִיאֵם אֶל־גְּבוּל קָדְשׁוֹ הַר־זֶה קָנְתָה יְמִינוֹ
"The Temple is one acquisition. Whence? As it is written: 'The place You made for Your dwelling, O LORD…'; and it says: 'He brought them to His holy border, this mountain which His right hand acquired.'"
**Note:** This is a prooftext in a studied text. The mishnah's point rests on the verb קָנְתָה, "acquired."

### Echoes in piyyut, selichot and other works
- **v. 2, Tal (the Prayer for Dew).** The opening stanza is בְּדַעְתּוֹ אַבִּיעָה חִידוֹת, "With His knowledge I will utter riddles." It appears in the festival sections of the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim, within the Musaf repetition before "Blessed… Shield of Abraham." The poet borrows the psalm's proem formula to open his own poem.
- **v. 2, Mi Khamokha for Shabbat Zachor (Edot HaMizrach siddur, Purim section).** The poem has הַיּוֹם אַבִּיעָה חִידוֹת, הָיוּ מִנִּי קֶדֶם עֲתִידוֹת, "Today I will utter riddles; from of old they were things to come." The poet turns the psalm's "riddles from of old" into the hidden foreknowledge of the Purim story.
- **v. 4, Keter Malkhut.** In Ibn Gabirol's description of the sun, the creature reveals "a trace of His mighty deeds, וֶעֱזוּזוֹ וְנִפְלְאוֹתָיו, His strength and His wonders." This is the psalm's pair of nouns.
- **v. 5, a selicha of the Ten Days of Penitence.** It appears in the Lithuanian selichot for the fourth day and the Polish for the fifth. The line עֵדוּת בְּיַעֲקֹב בִּתְעוּדָה נֶחְתָּם, "a testimony in Jacob, sealed as a covenant-witness," leads into God's promise to preserve Israel in the enemy's land.
- **v. 8, Shir HaYichud.** It is printed among the customs of Yom Kippur night in the Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim. The line is אָבוֹא וְאֶמְצָא תַּכְלִית שִׁבְחוֹ, לֹא־נֶאֶמְנָה אֶת־אֵל רוּחוֹ, "Shall I come and find the limit of His praise? His spirit was not faithful with God." The psalm's indictment becomes a verdict on anyone who claims to fathom God's praise.
- **v. 12, the list of verses for one's name.** The verse appears in an alphabetical list of verses whose first and last letters match a name, here נ…ן. This custom is said at the end of the Amidah. The list is printed in the Sefard siddur's Melava Malka section, in the Edot HaMizrach siddur's Havdalah/*Veyiten Lecha* section, and in Seder Ma'amadot. The verse is chosen for its letters, not its sense.
- **v. 13, the Yotzer for Shabbat HaGadol (Sefard siddur, Yotzerot).** The line is בָּקַע יָם וַיַּעֲבִירֵם וּכְנֵד נוֹזְלִים כִּנֵס, "He split the sea and brought them through, and gathered the flowing waters like a heap." It is a close paraphrase of the whole verse, followed by the feeding in the wilderness.
- **v. 16, a kinah for Tisha B'Av day (Ashkenaz kinnot).** The line is אַתָּה הֵינַקְתָּ דְּבַשׁ מִסֶּלַע. וַתּוֹצִיא נוֹזְלִים מִסֶּלַע, "You suckled honey from the rock and brought forth streams from the rock." In this kinah each "You did" is set against "and why…?" Here the contrast is with Israel's judges "dropped by the rock" and children "dashed against the rock."
- **v. 23, the stanza שַׁעֲרֵי שָׁמַיִם פְּתַח.** The full line is שַׁעֲרֵי שָׁמַיִם פְּתַח. וְאוֹצָרְךָ הַטּוֹב לָנוּ תִפְתַּח, "Open the gates of heaven, and open for us Your good treasure." It closes the Hoshanot of Hoshana Rabba in the Sefard siddur, before *Kol Mevaser*. The same stanza stands before *El Melekh Yoshev* in Neilah in the Ashkenaz and Sefard Yom Kippur machzorim. It is an allusion that blends the psalm's opened "doors of heaven" with Deuteronomy 28:12.
- **vv. 23–24, Seder Ma'amadot.** The baraita "the measure of good exceeds the measure of punishment" cites both verses: "Of the good measure it says: 'He commanded the skies above and opened the doors of heaven, and rained manna upon them to eat.'" It contrasts them with the opened "windows of heaven" of the Flood. This is a prooftext in a studied text.
- **v. 25, Ma'avar Yabbok (Siftei Tzedek).** In a kabbalistic discourse on the food of souls and angels, the author writes that Israel ate in the wilderness "as they expounded on the verse לחם אבירים אכל איש."
- **v. 25, Ma'avar Yabbok (Sefat Emet).** Grass is plucked at a burial to show that the deceased "has risen to eat לחם אבירים," the bread of the mighty.
- **vv. 36–37, Yesod HaTeshuva of Rabbeinu Yonah, printed in the Sefard Rosh Hashanah machzor.** The penitent confesses before eating, so as not to be among those of whom it says "They flattered Him with their mouth… and were not faithful to His covenant." This is a prooftext in an ethical treatise.
- **v. 38, the Yom Kippur Musaf Kedushah piyyut (Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim).** The line is קֶצֶף אִם קָצַף בַּאֲיֻמָּתוֹ. קָדוֹשׁ לֹא יָעִיר כָּל חֲמָתוֹ, "If He was angered at His fearsome nation, the Holy One does not arouse all His wrath."
- **v. 38, the Yom Kippur morning piyyut of "Holy…" lines with the response "Blessed be the name…".** It appears in the Ashkenaz machzor (at *Eil Adon*) and the Sefard machzor (Blessings of the Shema). The line is קָדוֹשׁ עָוֹן יְכַפֵּר וְלֹא יָעִיר כָּל חֲמָתוֹ, "Holy, He atones iniquity and does not arouse all His wrath."
- **v. 38, the Letter of Rav Shimshon of Ostropol (Sefard siddur, Nissan).** The verse is cited inside a kabbalistic gematria discourse on the names of three tannaim.
- **v. 38, Ma'avar Yabbok.**
  - In Siftei Tzedek and Siftei Renanot, the whole verse is recited within the book's prayers for the sick and dying, after a plea that sins be purged "not through suffering and evil illnesses" and before "May the words of my mouth…"
  - In Minchat Aharon it stands in a chain of psalm verses (77:2, 77:8, 78:38, 78:66, 79:9).
  - In Imrei Noam it is cited in a discussion of prayer for the sick.
  - The Introduction alludes to it in praising a society that rises at night for the Thirteen Attributes: והרבה להשיב אף וחימה, "and abundantly turned back anger and wrath."
- **v. 39, a selicha for the fifth day of the Ten Days (Lithuanian).** The line is עָלַי בְּרַחֲמִים שׁוּב. כִּי בַמֶּה חָשׁוּב. רוּחַ הוֹלֵךְ וְלֹא יָשׁוּב, "Turn to me in mercy, for of what account is a breath that goes and does not return?"
- **v. 39, Keter Malkhut.** In the confession of human frailty, the line is צֵל עוֹבֵר רוּחַ הוֹלֵךְ וְלֹא יָשׁוּב, "a passing shadow, a breath that goes and does not return."
- **v. 39, Ma'avar Yabbok (Sefat Emet).** On the soul's garments at death, the uppermost "alone הולך ולא ישוב, goes and does not return." This is a loose allusion to the psalm's departing breath.
- **v. 49, Ma'avar Yabbok.** In Siftei Tzedek, illnesses are sent by angels "called משלחת מלאכי רעים." In Siftei Renanot, the third extension of the *kelippah* is the source of עברה וזעם וצרה משלחת מלאכי רעים. Both are citations in kabbalistic discourse.
- **v. 54, the same Tisha B'Av kinah as v. 16.** The line is אַתָּה כִּוַּנְתָּ לְשֶׁבֶת הוֹדֶךָ. הַר זֶה קָנְתָה יְמִינְךָ וְיָדֶךָ, "You established a seat for Your glory, the mountain Your right hand acquired." It is answered by "and why have You turned back Your right hand?"
- **v. 60, R. Ephraim of Regensburg's Akedah piyyut.** It opens אִם אָפֵס רֹבַע הַקֵּן, אֹהֶל שִׁכֵּן אִם רִקֵּן, "If the fourth of the nest is gone, if the tent He dwelt in is emptied." The poet applies the psalm's abandoned sanctuary to the ruined Temple. It appears in:
  - the Ashkenaz selichot (Lithuanian, Fast of Gedaliah; Polish, fourth day of the Ten Days);
  - the Edot HaMizrach selichot, rubric "יש אומרים";
  - the Edot HaMizrach Rosh Hashanah shofar service, where the rubric says some say it on the second day in place of *Et Sha'arei Ratzon*.
- **v. 63, a kinah for Tisha B'Av day (Ashkenaz).** The line is אֲבוֹתֵינוּ זָרָה כְּהִכְנִיסוּ בַּחוּרָיו אָכְלָה אֵשׁ, "When our fathers brought a strange thing in, fire consumed His young men." It is woven into a stanza whose lines all end in אֵשׁ.
- **v. 66, Ma'avar Yabbok (Minchat Aharon).** The verse stands in the same chain of verses as v. 38, before "Help us, God of our salvation."
