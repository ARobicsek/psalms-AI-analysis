## Modern Jewish Liturgical Use (Psalm 27)

*Texts searched (Sefaria): the Ashkenaz, Sefard (Hasidic) and Edot HaMizrach siddurim; the Chabad weekday siddur; the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim of all three rites; the full selichot (Ashkenaz, Lithuanian and Polish; Edot HaMizrach); the Ashkenaz kinnot and the Edot HaMizrach Tisha B'Av order; two Haggadot; Ma'avar Yabbok, Seder Ma'amadot, Tikkun HaKlali, Perek Shirah, Yizkor, Akdamut, Keter Malkhut and the Azharot. Not searched: festival machzorim for Pesach, Shavuot and Sukkot beyond what the siddurim carry, and the Yemenite, Italian, Romaniote and other rites. A place missing below may still exist: nothing here shows that a verse is said ONLY somewhere, or in 'certain' services.*

### The whole psalm

**Where:** Psalm 27 (*LeDavid Hashem ori*) is the psalm of the penitential season. The siddurim and machzorim of Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad carry a rubric that sets it from the start of Elul through the end of the festival season. It is said at the close of the service and is followed by the Mourner's Kaddish.

- **Ashkenaz siddur:** weekday Shacharit (concluding prayers) and Maariv, and Shabbat Maariv and Musaf. The rubric reads "from 1 Elul until after Shemini Atzeret."
- **Sefard siddur:** weekday Shacharit and Mincha, and Shabbat (Friday Mincha, Shabbat morning, Shabbat Mincha), after the Kaddish that follows the Amidah. The rubric reads "from Rosh Chodesh Elul until after Hoshana Rabbah."
- **Chabad weekday siddur:** in Shacharit after the psalm of the day, and in Mincha immediately before Aleinu, under the same Elul–Hoshana Rabbah rubric.
- **High Holiday machzorim:** the psalm continues through the Days of Awe in the same closing position.
  - Ashkenaz Rosh Hashanah: Maariv, and at the end of Musaf on both days after the psalm of the day.
  - Ashkenaz Yom Kippur: Maariv on the eve, the morning service, and the Maariv at the close of Yom Kippur, where it comes just before Kiddush Levanah.
  - Sefard Rosh Hashanah: Mincha on the eve and on the festival, and the morning service of both days.
  - Sefard Yom Kippur: Mincha on the eve (just before the rubric for *malkot*), the morning service, and Mincha.
- **Edot HaMizrach siddur:** the psalm stands in full near the close of weekday Shacharit. It follows a string of verses (Exod 15:26; Prov 3:18, 3:17, 18:10, 9:11). The excerpt carries no seasonal rubric.

**The text:**
- Ashkenaz: מא' אלול עד אחר שמיני עצרת אומרים מזמור זה: לְדָוִד. ה' אורִי וְיִשְׁעִי מִמִּי אִירָא. ה' מָעוז חַיַּי מִמִּי אֶפְחָד
  - "From 1 Elul until after Shemini Atzeret this psalm is said: Of David. The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?"
- Chabad: מר״ח אלול עד הושענא רבא אחר שיר של יום אומרים: לְדָוִד יְיָ אוֹרִי וְיִשְׁעִי מִמִּי אִירָא
  - "From Rosh Chodesh Elul until Hoshana Rabbah, after the psalm of the day, one says: Of David. The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?"

**Note:**
- Nothing in the psalm names the season. The association rests on the midrash (Vayikra Rabbah 21:4), which reads "my light" as Rosh Hashanah and "my salvation" as Yom Kippur. The custom itself is early modern and is codified in *Mateh Ephraim*.
- The two end-points in the rubrics differ: Ashkenaz runs to Shemini Atzeret, while Sefard and Chabad run to Hoshana Rabbah.

### v. 4: אַחַת שָׁאַלְתִּי מֵאֵת יְהֹוָה

**Where:** The verse appears in two regular prayers.
- **Hashkavah (Edot HaMizrach siddur, mourning section):** the memorial prayer for the dead. The second half of the verse is woven into it.
- **Ark-opening prayer (Sefard Yom Kippur machzor, Mincha):** the whole verse is quoted in the private prayer said when the ark is opened for the Torah reading, in the section that begins *Vayehi binsoa*.

**The text:**
- Hashkavah: מֶלֶךְ מַלְכֵי הַמּלָכִים בְּרַחֲמָיו יַסְתִּירֵהוּ בְּצֵל כְּנָפָיו וּבְסֵתֶר אָהֳלוֹ לַחֲזוֹת בְּנוֹעַם יְהֹוָה וּלְבַקֵּר בְּהֵיכָלוֹ, וּלְקֵץ הַיָּמִין יַעֲמִידֵהוּ
  - "May the supreme King of kings in His mercy hide him in the shadow of His wings and in the shelter of His tent, to behold the pleasantness of the LORD and to visit in His temple, and raise him up at the end of days."
- Ark-opening prayer: וְהַחֲזִירֵנִי בִּתְשׁוּבָה שְׁלֵמָה לְפָנֶיךָ וְתַדְרִיכֵנִי לַעֲבוֹדָתֶךָ. אַחַת שָׁאַלְתִּי מֵאֵת יְהֹוָה אוֹתָהּ אֲבַקֵּשׁ שִׁבְתִּי בְּבֵית יְהֹוָה כָּל יְמֵי חַיַּי לַחֲזוֹת בְּנֹעַם יְהֹוָה וּלְבַקֵּר בְּהֵיכָלוֹ
  - "Bring me back in complete repentance before You and guide me to Your service. One thing I asked of the LORD, that I seek: to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the pleasantness of the LORD and to visit in His temple."

**Note:**
- In the Hashkavah, the psalmist's wish to dwell in the sanctuary becomes the soul's hope in the world to come.
- The phrase "in the shelter of His tent" also draws on v. 5 (בְּסֵתֶר אָהֳלוֹ).

### v. 7: שְׁמַע יְהֹוָה קוֹלִי אֶקְרָא

**Where:** The verse is recast in the plural as part of a run of "hear our prayer" verses in the selichot. The run follows the Thirteen Attributes and *Selach lanu… ki atah Adonai tov vesalach*. The catalogue shows it in:
- the Ashkenaz (Polish) selichot for the first day of Selichot and the fifth day of the Ten Days of Penitence;
- the selichot for the fast of 20 Sivan in the Sefard siddur.

**The text:** הַאֲזִינָה יְהוָה תְּפִלָּתֵנוּ וְהַקְשִׁיבָה בְּקוֹל תַּחֲנוּנֵינוּ: שְׁמַע יְהוָה קוֹלֵנוּ נִקְרָא וְחָנֵּנוּ וַעֲנֵנוּ: שִׁמְעָה יְהוָה צֶדֶק הַקְשִׁיבָה רִנָּתֵנוּ
- "Give ear, O LORD, to our prayer, and attend to the voice of our supplications. Hear, O LORD, our voice when we call; be gracious to us and answer us. Hear, O LORD, what is right; attend to our cry."

**Note:** The individual's plea is turned into the congregation's: "my voice… I call… answer me" becomes "our voice… we call… answer us."

### v. 14: קַוֵּה אֶל יְהֹוָה

**Where:** The verse is the first line of the passage that leads into *Ein Keloheinu*, said after the Amidah in every rite searched.
- **Sefard siddur:** weekday Shacharit, Musaf of Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh, festivals, and the Shabbatot of Shekalim and HaChodesh.
- **Ashkenaz siddur:** Shabbat Musaf, and weekday Shacharit (in a section labelled for Israel usage).
- **Chabad weekday siddur:** Shacharit.
- **Edot HaMizrach siddur:** weekday Shacharit and Rosh Chodesh.
- **Machzorim:** the Sefard Rosh Hashanah Musaf, and the close of the Ashkenaz Rosh Hashanah Musaf on both days.

**The text:** קַוֵּה אֶל יְהֹוָה חֲזַק וְיַאֲמֵץ לִבֶּךָ וְקַוֵּה אֶל יְהֹוָה: אֵין קָדוֹשׁ כַּיהֹוָה כִּי אֵין בִּלְתֶּךָ וְאֵין צוּר כֵּאלֹהֵינוּ… אֵין כֵּאלֹהֵינוּ
- "Hope in the LORD; be strong and let your heart take courage, and hope in the LORD. There is none holy as the LORD, for there is none besides You, and there is no rock like our God… There is none like our God."

**Note:**
- The verse is joined to 1 Sam 2:2 and Ps 18:32, which supply the "none like our God" theme of the hymn that follows.
- The Talmud (Berakhot 32b) reads the doubled "hope" as a charge to pray again if one's prayer was not answered. That reading suits its position directly after the Amidah, but no source in the catalogue gives it as the reason for the placement.

### Echoes in piyyut, selichot and other works

**v. 1**
- **Shir HaYichud, recited on Yom Kippur night (Ashkenaz and Sefard Yom Kippur machzorim):** the hymn weaves both halves of the verse. אוֹרִי וְיִשְׁעִי מָעוֹז חַיָּי, עָלָיו תְּלוּיִם כָּל־מַאֲוַיָּי: "My light and my salvation, the stronghold of my life; on Him hang all my desires."
- **Edot HaMizrach Yom Kippur Arvit, selichot:** the whole verse appears in a chain of verses beginning with the divine name. It follows the refrain-piyyut ending הִכּוֹן לִקְרַאת אֱלֹהֶיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל ("Prepare to meet your God, O Israel") and precedes Ps 30:3.
- **Ma'avar Yabbok, Epilogue:** the author closes with an invocation, בְּשֵׁם אֵל אוֹרִי וְיִשְׁעִי אֲשֶׁר לֹא יֵבוֹשׁוּ קוָֹיו: "in the name of God, my light and my salvation, whose hopers shall not be ashamed."

**v. 4**
- **Second day of Rosh Hashanah, morning piyyut in the blessings of the Shema (Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim):** לְחַיֵּי עוֹלָם יִכָּתְבוּ אֱמוּנַי. יִזְכּוּ לַחֲזוֹת בְּנֹעַם יְהֹוָה: "May my faithful ones be inscribed for eternal life; may they merit to behold the pleasantness of the LORD."
- **Prayer "upon arising in the morning" (Sefard Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim):** the worshipper asks to be מֵאוֹתָם הַנּוֹחֲלִים לַחֲזוֹת בְּנֹעַם יְהֹוָה וּלְבַקֵּר בְּהֵיכָלוֹ: "among those who inherit the beholding of the LORD's pleasantness and visiting in His temple." The vision is here understood eschatologically.
- **Tikkun HaKlali, a closing prayer:** וְאֶזְכֶּה לַחֲזוֹת בְּנֹעַם יְהֹוָה וּלְבַקֵּר בְּהֵיכָלוֹ: "may I merit to behold the pleasantness of the LORD and visit in His temple." This is the same eschatological use.
- **Ma'avar Yabbok (discursive and kabbalistic passages):**
  - *Siftei Renanot*: the soul's garments let it gaze "in the shining mirror לחזות בנועם ה'".
  - *Imrei Noam*: Naomi is Binah, "in the secret of לחזות בנועם ה'".
  - *Siftei Tzedek*: the phrase is glossed "שהיא בינה" ("which is Binah").
  - *Korban Ta'anit*: a Friday fast prepares one "לחזות בנועם ה' ולבקר בהיכלו", to receive the light of Shabbat.
  - *Introduction*: the author applies the phrase to his teacher, ולותיה אני מסתופף לחזות בנועם אמריו: "in whose shelter I dwell, to behold the pleasantness of his sayings."

**v. 7**
- **Second day of Rosh Hashanah, reader's repetition of Shacharit (Ashkenaz and Sefard), the piyyut שֻׁלַּחְתִּי בְּמַלְאֲכוּת after the blessing מחיה המתים:** a stanza closes with the verse, שָׁפַכְתִּי שִׂיחַ לְבַקֵּשׁ עֲתִירָה. שְׁמַע יְהֹוָה קוֹלִי אֶקְרָא: "I have poured out my speech to seek supplication: hear, O LORD, my voice when I call." Here the cantor speaks as the congregation's emissary.
- **Edot HaMizrach Yom Kippur Arvit, selichot (twice):** the whole verse stands in a chain of verses beginning *shema*, after Ps 30:11 and before Ps 6:10.
  - The program flags this as "wording following Deuteronomy 33:7" (שְׁמַע ה' קוֹל יְהוּדָה), but the excerpt quotes Ps 27:7 verbatim.

**v. 9**
- **Ashkenaz selichot, a strophic piyyut (Lithuanian, third day of the Ten Days; Polish, fourth day):** a stanza closes with the verse, קוֹלִי שְׁמַע כְּחַסְדֶּךָ. בְּיוֹם אֶעֱמֹד נֶגְדְּךָ. אַל־תַּט בְּאַף עַבְדֶּךָ: "Hear my voice according to Your kindness, on the day I stand before You; turn not Your servant away in anger." The opening also echoes v. 7.

**v. 11**
- **Sefard Rosh Hashanah Musaf, a piyyut in the reader's repetition (first day):** עִקְשִׁים בְּאֹרַח מִישׁוֹר מַסְלוּל יִדַּע: "He knew the course of the crooked on the level path." The phrase אֹרַח מִישׁוֹר is the psalm's.
- **Ma'avar Yabbok, Author's Introduction:** the physician-counsellor יורהו באורח מישור, "shall teach him on the level path," which echoes the verse's הוֹרֵנִי… וּנְחֵנִי בְּאֹרַח מִישׁוֹר.
- **Seder Ma'amadot, a personal prayer built from verses:** וְעָזְרֵנִי לִנְצוֹר עֵדוֹתֶיךָ בְּכָל לֵב. וּנְחֵנִי בְּאֹרַח מִישׁוֹר: "help me keep Your testimonies with all my heart, and lead me on a level path."

**v. 13**
- **Ashkenaz selichot for Erev Rosh Hashanah (Lithuanian and Polish):** a strophic piyyut whose stanzas end on "life" closes one stanza with וַאֲנַחְנוּ נְבָרֵךְ יָהּ שִׁבְחֲךָ לְהַעֲרֵץ. לִרְאוֹת בְּטוּב יְהֹוָה בְּאֶרֶץ הַחַיִּים: "and we shall bless the LORD, to extol Your praise, to see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living."

**v. 14**
- **Ashkenaz selichot (Lithuanian, second day of the Ten Days; Polish, third day):** a piyyut on hope whose stanzas end in verses with the root קוה. One closes וּמַדּוּעַ דַל כָּכָה כָּבוֹד וְלִי דִינַי. קַוֵּה אֶל־יְהֹוָה…: "and why is my glory brought so low, and my judgment…? Hope in the LORD…"

**vv. 2, 4, 9–14**
- **Ma'avar Yabbok, *Siftei Tzedek* and *Minchat Aharon*:** the psalm is read, abridged, in the verse-anthologies said at the bedside of the sick and dying.
  - *Minchat Aharon* has vv. 2, 4 and 9–10, then Ps 28:2, then v. 14; v. 10 also appears separately later.
  - *Siftei Tzedek* has v. 4, vv. 9–14 interleaved with Ps 26:9 and 26:11 and Ps 33:18–19, and v. 9 in another chain, followed by a rubric for the next order of readings.
