## Modern Jewish Liturgical Use (Psalm 130)

*Texts searched (Sefaria): the Ashkenaz, Sefard (Hasidic) and Edot HaMizrach siddurim; the Chabad weekday siddur; the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim of all three rites; the full selichot (Ashkenaz, Lithuanian and Polish; Edot HaMizrach); the Ashkenaz kinnot and the Edot HaMizrach Tisha B'Av order; two Haggadot; Ma'avar Yabbok, Seder Ma'amadot, Tikkun HaKlali, Perek Shirah, Yizkor, Akdamut, Keter Malkhut and the Azharot. Not searched: festival machzorim for Pesach, Shavuot and Sukkot beyond what the siddurim carry, and the Yemenite, Italian, Romaniote and other rites. A place missing below may still exist: nothing here shows that a verse is said ONLY somewhere, or in 'certain' services.*

### The whole psalm
**Where:** Psalm 130 is recited in full in several settings.

- **After Yishtabach during the Ten Days of Repentance.** All four rites have it. It comes after the closing blessing of Yishtabach and before the reader's Half Kaddish, at the end of Pesukei Dezimra:
  - Ashkenaz and Sefard (Hasidic) siddurim, weekday and Shabbat Shacharit. The rubric reads בעשרת ימי תשובה מוסיפין, "in the Ten Days of Repentance one adds".
  - Chabad weekday siddur.
  - Edot HaMizrach siddur, weekday and Shabbat. The Shabbat copy's rubric reads בשבת תשובה אומרים, "on Shabbat [of] Teshuvah one says".
  - Rosh Hashanah (both days) and Yom Kippur Shacharit in the machzorim of Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach.
- **In the run of Songs of Ascents at Shabbat Mincha.** The Sefard siddur has it between Psalm 129 and Psalm 131. The section is filed under "Amidah", but the excerpt shows the psalm sequence that follows the service.
- **In a fixed verse sequence.** The sequence runs Isa 54:17, Isa 11:9, Psalm 130, then Ps 119:89 said twelve times. It is found:
  - at Tashlich in the Ashkenaz and Edot HaMizrach Rosh Hashanah machzorim (the Ashkenaz copy is preceded by a kabbalistic petition to עתיקא קדישא);
  - at Yom Kippur Mincha in the Sefard machzor;
  - at Yom Kippur Mincha in the Edot HaMizrach machzor, inside the prayer of R. Chaim Yosef David Azulai.
  
  In the Edot HaMizrach Tashlich, the psalm is followed instead by the mishnah of R. Chananya ben Akashya.

**The text:** Ten Days, Sefard siddur:

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה יְהֹוָה ... הַבּוֹחֵר בְּשִׁירֵי זִמְרָה מֶֽלֶךְ יָחִיד אֵל חֵי הָעוֹלָמִים: בעשרת ימי תשובה מוסיפין: שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת מִמַּעֲמַקִּים קְרָאתִֽיךָ יְהֹוָה ... וְהוּא יִפְדֶּה אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכֹּל עֲוֹנוֹתָיו: ואומר החזן חצי קדיש

"Blessed are You, Lord … who chooses songs of praise, King, the One, Life of the worlds. *In the Ten Days of Repentance one adds:* A Song of Ascents. Out of the depths I call You, Lord … and He will redeem Israel from all its iniquities. *And the reader says Half Kaddish.*"

Yom Kippur Mincha, Sefard machzor:

כִּי מָלְאָה הָאָרֶץ דֵּעָה אֶת יְהֹוָה כַּמַּיִם לַיָּם מְכַסִּים: שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת, מִמַּעֲמַקִּים קְרָאתִיךָ יְהֹוָה ... ויאמר י"ב פעמים פסוק זה: לְעוֹלָם יְהֹוָה דְּבָרְךָ נִצָּב בַּשָּׁמָיִם: ויכוין שהגזרות רעות ישארו בשמים ולא ירדו לארץ

"For the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea. A Song of Ascents. Out of the depths I call You, Lord … *He says this verse twelve times:* Forever, Lord, Your word stands firm in heaven. *And he intends that evil decrees remain in heaven and not descend to earth.*"

**Note:** The verse sequence is a kabbalistic unit, with its intention stated in the Sefard rubric. The same unit appears both at Tashlich and at Yom Kippur Mincha.

### v. 2: אֲדֹנָי שִׁמְעָה בְקוֹלִי
**Where:** The verse is recast in the plural ("our voice … our supplications"). It forms part of the catena of verses in the Ashkenaz selichot that follows כי אתה אדני טוב וסלח and leads to כרחם אב. In the books searched, this catena appears:
- for the Fast of Gedaliah (Lithuanian and Polish selichot; Siddur Ashkenaz);
- for the first and sixth days (Lithuanian);
- for the seventh day (Polish).

**The text:** Polish selichot, Fast of Gedaliah:

יַעֲזֹב רָשָׁע דַּרְכּוֹ ... וְאֶל אֱלֹהֵינוּ כִּי יַרְבֶּה לִסְלוֹחַ: אֲדֹנָי שִׁמְעָה בְקוֹלֵנוּ תִּהְיֶינָה אָזְנֶיךָ קַשֻּׁבוֹת לְקוֹל תַּחֲנוּנֵינוּ: תִּכּוֹן תְּפִלָּתֵנוּ קְטֹרֶת לְפָנֶיךָ

"Let the wicked forsake his way … and to our God, for He will abundantly pardon. Lord, hear our voice; let Your ears be attentive to the voice of our supplications. Let our prayer be set as incense before You."

**Note:** Siddur Ashkenaz marks the line תהלים קל, ב עם שינוי, "Ps 130:2 with a change".

### vv. 3–4: אִם־עֲוֺנוֹת תִּשְׁמָר־יָהּ / כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה
**Where:**
- **Weekday Shacharit, Monday and Thursday.** The verses appear in the long Tachanun, והוא רחום, in the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim (section "For Monday and Thursday") and in the Chabad siddur.
- **Edot HaMizrach weekday Shacharit and fast days.** The same verse sequence appears in the Vidui section of the Edot HaMizrach weekday Shacharit. It is also given for the Fasts of Gedaliah, the Tenth of Tevet, Esther and the Seventeenth of Tammuz.
- **Neilah of Yom Kippur.** In the verse catena after the Thirteen Attributes, in the Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim, the order is reversed: v. 4 first, then v. 3. The words כי עמך הסליחה also occur in the Shacharit reader's repetition of both machzorim (no excerpt given).
- **Selichot verse catenas:**
  - Lithuanian selichot, third day;
  - Polish selichot, fourth day of the Ten Days;
  - Edot HaMizrach selichot, where the verses are preceded by אם עונינו ענו בנו;
  - v. 3 alone in the Polish selichot, second day;
  - v. 4 alone in the Lithuanian selichot for Erev Rosh Hashanah (twice) and the Polish selichot, fifth day of the Ten Days.

**The text:** Tachanun, Siddur Ashkenaz:

הוֹשִׁיעֵֽנוּ יְהֹוָה אֱלֺהֵֽינוּ וְקַבְּצֵֽנוּ מִן־הַגּוֹיִם לְהוֹדוֹת לְשֵׁם קָדְשֶֽׁךָ: אִם־עֲוֺנוֹת תִּשְׁמָר־יָהּ אֲדֹנָי מִי יַעֲמֹד: כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה לְמַֽעַן תִּוָּרֵא: לֺא כַחֲטָאֵֽינוּ תַּעֲשֶׂה־לָּֽנוּ

"Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations, to give thanks to Your holy name. If You, Lord, should keep account of iniquities, O Lord, who could stand? But with You is forgiveness, that You may be feared. Do not deal with us according to our sins."

Neilah, Ashkenaz machzor:

יְהֹוָה הוֹשִֽׁיעָה, הַמֶּֽלֶךְ יַעֲנֵֽנוּ בְיוֹם קָרְאֵנוּ: כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה לְמַֽעַן תִּוָּרֵא: אִם־עֲו‍ֹנוֹת תִּשְׁמָר־יָהּ אֲדֹנָי מִי יַעֲמֹד: סְלַח נָא לַעֲו‍ֹן הָעָם הַזֶּה

"Lord, save; may the King answer us on the day we call. But with You is forgiveness, that You may be feared. If You, Lord, should keep account of iniquities, who could stand? Pardon, please, the iniquity of this people."

**Note:** In the Neilah order, the assurance of forgiveness comes before the plea. This places the verse of forgiveness immediately after the Thirteen Attributes.

### v. 4: כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה
**Where:**
- In the Sefard (Hasidic) Torah service, within a chain of verses in the section that opens with Va-yehi binso'a. This appears in the Shabbat siddur and in the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim.
- The same chain of verses is printed in the Sefard Tashlich.

**The text:**

הִנֵּה עֵין יְהֹוָה אֶל יְרֵאָיו לַמְיַחֲלִים לְחַסְדּוֹ: הוֹד וְהָדָר לְפָנָיו עֹז וְחֶדְוָה בִּמְקוֹמוֹ: כִּי עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה לְמַעַן תִּוָּרֵא: חַנּוּן וְרַחוּם יְהֹוָה אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם וּגְדָל חָסֶד

"Behold, the eye of the Lord is on those who fear Him, who hope for His love. Majesty and splendour are before Him, strength and joy in His place. But with You is forgiveness, that You may be feared. Gracious and compassionate is the Lord, slow to anger and great in love."

**Note:** A verse about forgiveness placed among verses of praise and trust is not self-explanatory. The excerpt gives no rubric explaining the chain.

### vv. 7–8: יַחֵל יִשְׂרָאֵל … כִּי־עִם־יְהֹוָה הַחֶסֶד / וְהוּא יִפְדֶּה
**Where:** Selichot verse catenas:
- v. 7b, v. 8 and Ps 25:22 in the Tenth of Tevet selichot of the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim;
- vv. 7b–8 in the Lithuanian selichot, first day;
- v. 7b in the Polish selichot, fourth and fifth days of the Ten Days.

**The text:** Tenth of Tevet, Siddur Ashkenaz:

כִּי אַתָּה אֲדֹנָי טוֹב וְסַלָּח וְרַב חֶסֶד לְכָל קֹרְאֶיךָ: כִּי עִם יְהֹוָה הַחֶסֶד וְהַרְבֵּה עִמּוֹ פְדוּת: פְּדֵה אֱלֹהִים אֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכֹּל צָרוֹתָיו: וְהוּא יִפְדֶּה אֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכֹּל עֲוֹנוֹתָיו: פּוֹדֶה יְהֹוָה נֶפֶשׁ עֲבָדָיו

"For You, Lord, are good and forgiving, abounding in love to all who call You. For with the Lord is love, and with Him is plenteous redemption. Redeem Israel, O God, from all its troubles. And He will redeem Israel from all its iniquities. The Lord redeems the soul of His servants."

**Note:** The catena strings together the verses on redemption (פדה/פדות).

### v. 8: וְהוּא יִפְדֶּה אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל
**Where:** In Tachanun, at weekday Shacharit and Mincha:
- in the Chabad siddur;
- in the Edot HaMizrach siddur (Vidui section).

In both, the verse is appended to the last verse of Psalm 25, the psalm of the falling on the face.

**The text:** Chabad, Mincha:

תֹּם־וָיֹשֶׁר יִצְּרוּנִי, כִּי קִוִּיתִֽיךָ׃ פְּדֵה אֱלֹהִים אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכֹּל צָרוֹתָֽיו׃ וְהוּא יִפְדֶּה אֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכֹּל עֲו‍ֹנֹתָיו: בתענית ציבור ובעשי״ת אומרים כאן אבינו מלכנו

"Let integrity and uprightness guard me, for I hope in You. Redeem Israel, O God, from all its troubles. And He will redeem Israel from all its iniquities. *On public fasts and in the Ten Days of Repentance, Avinu Malkenu is said here.*"

**Note:** Ps 25:22 asks for redemption from troubles. The added line from Psalm 130 extends the request to redemption from iniquities.

### Echoes in piyyut, selichot and other works
- **Whole psalm, Ma'avar Yabbok, Siftei Tzedek:** recited in a run of psalms, between Ps 128 and Ps 134.
- **Whole psalm, Ma'aneh Lashon (Chabad):** recited in a run of psalms 129–131, in this collection of prayers for visiting graves.
- **v. 1, Perek Shirah** (also in the Sefard siddur's additional prayers): שִׁבֹּלֶת חִטִּים אוֹמֶרֶת שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת מִמַּעֲמַקִּים קְרָאתִיךָ יְהֹוָה, "The ear of wheat says: A Song of Ascents, out of the depths I call You." The pairing is not self-explanatory, and the text gives no reason.
- **v. 1, Ma'avar Yabbok, Korban Ta'anit:** an instruction for a penitential fast. Over each coin set aside for charity one says a verse, among them שיר המעלות ממעמקים קראתיך, "the Song of Ascents, Out of the depths".
- **v. 1, Tikkun HaKlali,** concluding prayer: וְהִנְנִי קוֹרֵא אֵלֶיךָ מִמָּקוֹם שָׁפָל כָּזֶה ... מִמַּעֲמַקִּים קְרָאתִיךָ יְהֹוָה, מֵעִמְקֵי עֲמָקִים, "I call You from so low a place … out of the depths I call You, Lord, from the deepest depths."
- **v. 2, Ushpizin prayer on entering the sukkah** (Ashkenaz, Sefard): וּמֵאוּשְׁפִּיזִין עִלָּאִין ... תִּהְיֶינָה אָזְנֶיךָ קַשּׁוּבות, "and to the exalted guests … may Your ears be attentive."
- **v. 2, Edot HaMizrach siddur, learning for Nisan:** a petition that God hear the souls in Eden: תִּהְיֶינָה אָזְנֶיךָ קַשּׁוּבוֹת אֲלֵיהֶם, "may Your ears be attentive to them."
- **v. 2, Seder Ma'amadot:** the whole verse in a personal petition, after Dan 9:19 and before Ps 69:14, in a prayer for the ruined Temple and burial in the Land.
- **v. 2, Ma'avar Yabbok, Minchat Aharon:** woven into a poem: קַשּׁוּבוֹת תִּהְיֶינָה אָזְנֶיךָ לְשֶׁפֶךְ שִׂיחֵנוּ, "let Your ears be attentive to the outpouring of our speech."
- **v. 3, Edot HaMizrach Yom Kippur Shacharit,** piyyut אלהים אלי אתה: הִנְנִי לְפָנֶיךָ בַּאֲשָׁמִי. הָעֲוֹנוֹת אִם תִּשְׁמָר־יָהּ יְהֹוָה מִי יַעֲמֹד מִי, "here I am before You in my guilt; if You keep iniquities, Lord, who could stand?" The words אם עונות תשמור also occur in two Musaf passages of that machzor (not excerpted).
- **v. 3, Yom Kippur Katan selicha** of R. Eliezer b. Isaac HaLevi (Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim): וְאִם עֲוֹנוֹת תִּשְׁמוֹר הֲלֹא רַבּוּ, "and if You keep iniquities, are they not many?"
- **v. 3, Lithuanian selichot, fifth day of the Ten Days,** a piyyut: הַאִם תַּמְנוּ לִגְמֹר. אִם עֲווֹנוֹת תִּשְׁמֹר, "are we to be utterly finished, if You keep iniquities?"
- **v. 4, Yom Kippur Musaf reader's repetition** (Ashkenaz, Sefard), a piyyut: נָאוֹר עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה. נָכוֹן מַהֵר לְסָלְחָה, "Radiant One, with You is forgiveness; be ready to forgive swiftly."
- **v. 4, Polish selichot, third day of the Ten Days,** an acrostic selicha whose stanza closes with the verse: צֶדֶק דֶּרֶךְ תְּשׁוּבָה לָנוּ תּוֹרֶה. כִּי עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה לְמַעַן תִּוָּרֵא, "teach us the right path of return, for with You is forgiveness."
- **v. 4, Edot HaMizrach Rosh Hashanah, Zohar study for the night:** a prooftext in the Zohar passage: וְעַל דָּא כְּתִיב: כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה, לְמַעַן תִּוָּרֵא, "and of this it is written: with You is forgiveness."
- **v. 5, Ma'avar Yabbok:**
  - The author's introduction weaves the phrases קותה נפשי ... ולדברו הוחלתי, "my soul hoped … and in His word I trust."
  - Minchat Aharon has the whole verse in a run of verses from the Songs of Ascents.
- **v. 6, Lithuanian selichot, Erev Rosh Hashanah,** in the verse catena, recast: נַפְשֵׁנוּ לַאדֹנָי מִשֹּׁמְרִים לַבֹּקֶר שֹׁמְרִים לַבֹּקֶר, "our soul waits for the Lord more than watchmen for the morning."
- **v. 6, Ma'avar Yabbok, Introduction:** praise of a man who devoted himself to the service of God משומרים לבוקר, "more than watchmen for the morning", in leading an early-rising prayer fellowship.
- **v. 7, Shabbat day zemer ברוך ה' יום יום** (Sefard siddur): a stanza on the bondage in Egypt closes with the verse: וְנִגְלָה לְהַעֲלוֹתָם מֵעֹמֶק בּוֹר וָדוּת. כִּי עִם יְהֹוָה הַחֶסֶד וְהַרְבֵּה עִמּוֹ פְדוּת, "He revealed Himself to raise them from the depth of the pit … for with the Lord is love and plenteous redemption."
- **v. 8, Shir HaYichud, Yom Kippur night** (Ashkenaz, Sefard machzorim): פָּדָה אֶת־אַבְרָהָם יְדִידוֹ, הוּא יִפְדֶּה אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל עַבְדּוֹ, "He redeemed Abraham His beloved; He will redeem Israel His servant." This is probably an allusion to the psalm's closing words.
