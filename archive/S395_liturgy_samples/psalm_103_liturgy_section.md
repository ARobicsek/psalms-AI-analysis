## Modern Jewish Liturgical Use (Psalm 103)

*Texts searched (Sefaria): the Ashkenaz, Sefard (Hasidic) and Edot HaMizrach siddurim; the Chabad weekday siddur; the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim of all three rites; the full selichot (Ashkenaz, Lithuanian and Polish; Edot HaMizrach); the Ashkenaz kinnot and the Edot HaMizrach Tisha B'Av order; two Haggadot; Ma'avar Yabbok, Seder Ma'amadot, Tikkun HaKlali, Perek Shirah, Yizkor, Akdamut, Keter Malkhut and the Azharot. Not searched: festival machzorim for Pesach, Shavuot and Sukkot beyond what the siddurim carry, and the Yemenite, Italian, Romaniote and other rites. A place missing below may still exist: nothing here shows that a verse is said ONLY somewhere, or in 'certain' services.*

### v. 1: לְדָוִד בָּרְכִי נַפְשִׁי אֶת־יְהֹוָה
**Where:** (a) **Nishmat**, the hymn that closes Pesukei Dezimra on Shabbat and festival mornings. It appears in the Shabbat services of the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim and in the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur morning services of both machzorim. The same passage is carried in the Hallel of the Pesach Haggadah, as it appears in the Haggadah and in the Sefard siddur's Haggadah, and is reprinted in the Chabad *Ma'aneh Lashon*. (b) The **verses accompanying the Priestly Blessing**, in the Siddur Sefard and in the Musaf of Rosh Hashanah (both days) and Yom Kippur in the Sefard machzorim. The congregation says a verse with each word the kohanim chant, and v. 1 goes with the word יְבָרֶכְךָ.
**The text:**
(a) Nishmat: נְהַלֶּלְךָ וּנְשַׁבֵּחֲךָ וּנְפָאֶרְךָ וּנְבָרֵךְ אֶת שֵׁם קָדְשֶׁךָ. כָּאָמוּר, לְדָוִד, בָּרְכִי נַפְשִׁי אֶת ה'. וְכָל קְרָבַי אֶת שֵׁם קָדְשׁו
"We will praise You, laud You, glorify You and bless Your holy name, as it is said: Of David. Bless the LORD, O my soul, and all that is within me, His holy name."
(b) Priestly Blessing: יְבָרֶכְךָ. יְבָרֶכְךָ יְהֹוָה מִצִיוֹן עוֹשֵׂה שָׁמַיִם וָאָרֶץ: בָּרְכִי נַפְשִׁי אֶת יְהֹוָה וְכָל קְרָבַי אֶת שֵׁם קָדְשׁוֹ: יְהֹוָה. יְהֹוָה אֲדוֹנֵינוּ, מָה אַדִּיר שִׁמְךָ
"*Yevarekhekha*: 'May the LORD bless you from Zion, maker of heaven and earth' (Ps 134:3); 'Bless the LORD, O my soul, and all that is within me, His holy name.' *Adonai*: 'O LORD our Lord, how majestic is Your name' (Ps 8:2)."
**Note:** In Nishmat the verse is the prooftext for "and bless Your holy name," and it is the last line before the festival cantor's *Ha-El be-ta'atzumot* (Sefard rubric). In the Priestly Blessing it is attached to *yevarekhekha* by the shared root ברך, not by its sense.

### v. 6: עֹשֵׂה צְדָקוֹת יְהֹוָה
**Where:** In ***El Melekh Yoshev al Kisse Rachamim***, the introduction to the Thirteen Attributes, in all three rites. It is said:
- **Ashkenaz:** on every day of selichot in both the Lithuanian and Polish orders, on the minor fasts (Gedaliah, 10 Tevet, Esther, 17 Tammuz), at Yom Kippur Katan, and at Maariv and Neilah of Yom Kippur.
- **Sefard:** on the fasts, the BaHaB Mondays and Thursday, 20 Sivan and Yom Kippur Katan, and at Maariv and Neilah of Yom Kippur.
- **Edot HaMizrach:** in the selichot of all five Yom Kippur services, in the selichot book, on the fasts, and daily in the weekday Shacharit Vidui.
**The text:** מַרְבֶּה מְחִילָה לְחַטָּאִים, וּסְלִיחָה לַפּוֹשְׁעִים. עוֹשֵׂה צְדָקוֹת עִם כָּל בָּשָׂר וְרוּחַ, לֹא כְרָעָתָם תִּגְמוֹל. אֵל! הוֹרֵיתָ לָנוּ לוֹמַר – שְׁלוֹשׁ־עֶשְׂרֵה
"Who abounds in pardon for sinners and forgiveness for transgressors, who performs righteous acts with all flesh and spirit, not repaying them according to their wickedness. O God, You taught us to recite the Thirteen."
**Note:** The phrase is more than an idiom here. The prayer leads straight into Exodus 34:6–7, as the psalm does in vv. 6–8, and its "not repaying them according to their wickedness" echoes v. 10.

### v. 8: רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן יְהֹוָה אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם וְרַב־חָסֶד
**Where:** These words occur constantly, but the program marks every service occurrence as following **Exodus 34:6** (or **Psalm 86:15**). The liturgy recites the Thirteen Attributes from Exodus, not Ps 103:8. The Exodus formula appears:
- **Selichot:** in every selichot service, on fasts, BaHaB and Yom Kippur Katan, in all three rites.
- **Weekday Tachanun or Vidui:** in Ashkenaz, Sefard, Chabad and Edot HaMizrach.
- **Yom Kippur:** in every Yom Kippur service, including the Neilah piyyut signed Amittai (Ashkenaz, Sefard), the Edot HaMizrach Musaf repetition and the Sefard Avodah.
- **Opening the Ark:** on festivals falling on weekdays, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and Hoshana Rabbah, by the rubrics in the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim and the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim of Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach.
- **Sefard meditations:** on the word יְהֹוָה of the Priestly Blessing, and before Malkhuyot in the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur Musaf repetition.
- **Torah readings:** in the fast-day and Chol HaMoed Pesach readings themselves (Sefard, Edot HaMizrach).
Psalm 86 is the source in the Sefard and Edot HaMizrach *Beit Yaakov* sections, the Chabad Song of the Day, and the Hodu section of the Edot HaMizrach Yom Kippur Shacharit.
**The text:** וַיַּעֲבֹר יְהֺוָה עַל פָּנָיו וַיִּקְרָא: יְהֺוָה יְהֺוָה אֵל רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן אֶֽרֶךְ אַפַּֽיִם וְרַב־חֶֽסֶד וֶאֱמֶת: נֹצֵר חֶֽסֶד לָאֲלָפִים
"And the LORD passed before him and proclaimed: The LORD, the LORD, God merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in kindness and truth, keeping kindness for thousands."
**Note:** Ps 103:8 is itself the psalmist's citation of Exodus 34:6, which is why it overlaps the Attributes. The psalm's own wording, with יְהֹוָה after וְחַנּוּן, appears in this part of the catalogue only in Ma'avar Yabbok (see below).

### v. 10: לֹא כַחֲטָאֵינוּ עָשָׂה לָנוּ
**Where:** In the chain of penitential verses in the long weekday Tachanun:
- **Ashkenaz and Sefard:** under the rubric "For Monday and Thursday."
- **Chabad:** in the Shacharit Tachanun.
- **Edot HaMizrach:** in the daily weekday Shacharit Vidui and on the fasts (Gedaliah, 10 Tevet, Esther, 17 Tammuz).
- **Ashkenaz selichot:** the same petition appears after the Thirteen Attributes (Lithuanian third day; Polish second day and Erev Rosh Hashanah).
**The text:** אִם־עֲוֺנוֹת תִּשְׁמָר־יָהּ אֲדֹנָי מִי יַעֲמֹד: כִּי־עִמְּךָ הַסְּלִיחָה לְמַעַן תִּוָּרֵא: לֺא כַחֲטָאֵינוּ תַּעֲשֶׂה־לָּנוּ וְלֹא כַעֲוֺנֹתֵינוּ תִּגְמֹל עָלֵינוּ: אִם־עֲוֺנֵינוּ עָנוּ בָנוּ יְהֹוָה עֲשֵׂה לְמַעַן שְׁמֶךָ
"If You, LORD, kept count of sins, O Lord, who could stand? For with You is forgiveness, that You may be feared. Do not deal with us according to our sins, nor repay us according to our iniquities. Though our iniquities testify against us, LORD, act for Your name's sake."
**Note:** Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad turn the psalm's statement of fact into a plea (תַּעֲשֶׂה, תִּגְמֹל). The Edot HaMizrach Vidui keeps the psalm's past tense (עָשָׂה, גָּמַל).

### v. 13: כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל־בָּנִים
**Where:** Three regular placements:
- (a) **The selichot verse cluster** that precedes "לַיהֹוָה הַיְשׁוּעָה". It is said on every selichot day in the Lithuanian and Polish orders and on the fasts and Yom Kippur Katan in the Ashkenaz and Sefard siddurim. It also appears in the Yom Kippur Maariv (*Ya'aleh*) and Neilah of Ashkenaz and Sefard.
- (b) ***Ha-yom harat olam***, said after the shofar blasts of Malkhuyot, Zikhronot and Shofarot in the Rosh Hashanah Musaf, in all three rites.
- (c) **The weekday Tachanun:** the Monday and Thursday Tachanun in Ashkenaz and Sefard, the Chabad Tachanun, and the Edot HaMizrach Vidui (weekday and fasts).
**The text:**
(a) כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בָּנִים כֵּן תְּרַחֵם יְהֹוָה עָלֵינוּ: לַיהֹוָה הַיְשׁוּעָה עַל עַמְּךָ בִרְכָתֶךָ סֶּלָה
"As a father has compassion on his children, so, LORD, have compassion on us. Deliverance is the LORD's; Your blessing be upon Your people, selah."
(b) אִם כְּבָנִים. אִם כַּעֲבָדִים. אִם כְּבָנִים רַחֲמֵנוּ כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בָּנִים. וְאִם כַּעֲבָדִים עֵינֵינוּ לְךָ תְלוּיוֹת
"Whether as children or as servants: if as children, have compassion on us as a father has compassion on his children; and if as servants, our eyes are fixed on You."
(c) כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל־בָּנִים כֵּן תְּרַחֵם יְהֹוָה עָלֵינוּ וְהוֹשִׁיעֵנוּ לְמַעַן שְׁמֶךָ
"As a father has compassion on his children, so, LORD, have compassion on us and save us for Your name's sake."
**Note:** In every placement the psalm's "on those who fear Him" becomes "on us." The Ashkenaz siddur itself marks the line "תהלים קג, יג בשינוי" ("Ps 103:13, altered").

### v. 14: כִּי־הוּא יָדַע יִצְרֵנוּ זָכוּר כִּי־עָפָר אֲנָחְנוּ
**Where:**
- (a) As a line of ***Avinu Malkeinu***, in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach. It appears in the Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim (morning, Mincha, Maariv, Neilah) and in the weekday siddurim. The Edot HaMizrach siddur prints it in its weekday and Shabbat morning sections. Avinu Malkeinu belongs to the Ten Days of Repentance, and in Ashkenaz also to fast days.
- (b) The whole verse in the **closing verses of Tachanun** (*Va'anachnu lo neda*), in weekday Shacharit and Mincha in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad, and in the Edot HaMizrach weekday Vidui. The same verses close the selichot (Lithuanian and Polish orders, Edot HaMizrach selichot).
- (c) In the verse anthology of **Yom Kippur Katan** (Ashkenaz, Sefard).
**The text:**
(a) אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ מְנַע מַגֵּפָה מִנַּחֲלָתֶךָ: אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ זָכוּר כִּי עָפָר אֲנָחְנוּ: אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ מְחוֹל וּסְלַח לְכָל עֲוֹנוֹתֵינוּ
"Our Father, our King, hold back plague from Your inheritance. Our Father, our King, remember that we are dust. Our Father, our King, pardon and forgive all our iniquities."
(b) חָנֵּנוּ יְהֹוָה חָנֵּנוּ כִּי־רַב שָׂבַעְנוּ בוּז: בְּרֺגֶז רַחֵם תִּזְכּוֹר: כִּי הוּא יָדַע יִצְרֵנוּ זָכוּר כִּי עָפָר אֲנָחְנוּ: עָזְרֵנוּ אֱלֺהֵי יִשְׁעֵנוּ
"Be gracious to us, LORD, be gracious, for we are sated with contempt. In wrath remember mercy. For He knows our nature; He remembers that we are dust. Help us, God of our salvation."
**Note:** Avinu Malkeinu reads the psalm's "He is mindful" as an appeal, "remember." The Ashkenaz text vocalises it זָכוֹר.

### v. 17: וְחֶסֶד יְהֹוָה מֵעוֹלָם וְעַד־עוֹלָם
**Where:** In the Edot HaMizrach siddur's Musaf for the Three Festivals, within a sequence of verses about divine חֶסֶד arranged so that their initial letters spell divine names.
**The text:** יוֹמָם יְצַוֶּה יְהֹוָה חַסְדּוֹ ... הַרְאֵנוּ יְהֹוָה חַסְדֶּךָ וְיֶשְׁעֲךָ תִּתֶּן־לָנוּ׃ וְחֶסֶד יְהֹוָה מֵעוֹלָם וְעַד־עוֹלָם עַל־יְרֵאָיו וְצִדְקָתוֹ לִבְנֵי בָנִים׃ הוֹדוּ לַיהֹוָה כִּי־טוֹב
"By day the LORD commands His kindness … Show us, LORD, Your kindness, and grant us Your salvation. But the LORD's kindness is from everlasting to everlasting upon those who fear Him, and His righteousness to children's children. Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good."
**Note:** The verse is chosen for its opening letter. The rubric "ר"ת הוי"ה" marks that the initials of these four verses (י־ה־ו־ה) spell the Tetragrammaton, and the following group is marked "ר"ת אהיה".

### v. 19: יְהֹוָה בַּשָּׁמַיִם הֵכִין כִּסְאוֹ

**Where:** The verse has two regular placements.

- **Yehi Chevod.** The whole verse is said in Yehi Chevod in Pesukei Dezimra of the morning service. This is shown for weekday and Shabbat in the siddurim of Ashkenaz, Sefard (Hasidic) and Edot HaMizrach, and for weekday in the Chabad siddur. The machzorim of all three rites reprint it in their Pesukei Dezimra for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.
- **Melokh paragraph.** The second half, "וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָׁלָה", closes the paragraph "Melokh al kol ha-olam kulo" ("Reign over the whole world"). That paragraph stands in the middle blessing of the Amidah, the sanctification of the day.
  - **Rosh Hashanah:** the catalogue shows it in the Rosh Hashanah Amidah in all three rites. That covers Maariv, Shacharit, Musaf and Mincha, both days, in the silent Amidah and the reader's repetition. In Musaf it falls within the Malkhuyot section.
  - **Yom Kippur:** the Edot HaMizrach machzor shows it in every Amidah of the day, from Arvit through Mussaf and Mincha to Neilah.
  - **Prayer for the State of Israel:** the same line closes this prayer in the Ashkenaz siddur's Shabbat morning communal prayers.

**The text:**

*Yehi Chevod (Siddur Ashkenaz, weekday Shacharit):*
יְהֹוָה שִׁמְךָ לְעוֹלָם יְהֹוָה זִכְרְ֒ךָ לְדֹר־וָדֹר: יְהֹוָה בַּשָּׁמַֽיִם הֵכִין כִּסְאוֹ וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָֽׁלָה: יִשְׂמְחוּ הַשָּׁמַֽיִם וְתָגֵל הָאָֽרֶץ וְיֹאמְ֒רוּ בַגּוֹיִם יְהֹוָה מָלָךְ
"O LORD, Your name endures forever, Your renown, O LORD, for all generations. The LORD has established His throne in heaven, and His kingdom rules over all. Let the heavens rejoice and the earth exult; let them say among the nations, 'The LORD is King.'"

*Melokh (Machzor Rosh Hashanah Ashkenaz, Maariv Amidah):*
וְיֵדַע כָּל פָּעוּל כִּי אַתָּה פְעַלְתּוֹ וְיָבִין כָּל יְצוּר כִּי אַתָּה יְצַרְתּוֹ וְיֹאמַר כֹּל אֲשֶׁר נְשָׁמָה בְאַפּוֹ יְהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מֶֽלֶךְ וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָֽׁלָה
"Let everything that has been made know that You made it, let every creature understand that You formed it, and let all that has breath in its nostrils say: The LORD, God of Israel, is King, and His kingship rules over all."

*Prayer for the State of Israel (Siddur Ashkenaz):*
הוֹפַע בַּהֲדַר גְּאוֹן עֻזֶּךָ עַל כׇּל יוֹשְׁבֵי תֵּבֵל אַרְצֶךָ, וְיֹאמַר כֹּל אֲשֶׁר נְשָׁמָה בְּאַפּוֹ: "יְיָ אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מֶלֶךְ, וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָׁלָה!" אָמֵן סֶלָה
"Appear in the majestic splendour of Your might over all the inhabitants of Your world, and let all that has breath in its nostrils say: 'The LORD, God of Israel, is King, and His kingship rules over all!' Amen, Selah."

**Note:**
- **Yehi Chevod.** Yehi Chevod is a chain of biblical verses that builds toward "The LORD is King, the LORD was King, the LORD shall be King forever". The verse's throne and universal kingdom fit that climax directly.
- **Melokh.** The Melokh paragraph borrows the psalm's half-verse as the acclamation it hopes all creation will one day utter.

### Echoes in piyyut, selichot and other works
- **v. 1, Judah Halevi's *Barkhi atzulah* (Yom Kippur Neilah, Sefard machzor):** an alphabetical poem, signed "יהודה הלוי בן שמואל," in which each stanza ends with the refrain בָּרְכִי נַפְשִׁי אֶת יְהֹוָה (22 occurrences). Its stanzas take up the psalm's "אֶת שֵׁם": "בָּרְכִי אֲצוּלָה מֵרוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ. אֶת שֵׁם אַדִּיר נֶאְדָר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ" ("Bless, O soul drawn from the holy spirit, the name of the One mighty in holiness").
- **v. 1, Shir HaYichud (Yom Kippur night, Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim):** the poet weaves the verse into his own line, "תְּהַלֵּל נַפְשִׁי כֹּחַ מַעֲשֶׂיךָ, וְכָל קְרָבַי אֶת שֵׁם קָדְשֶׁךָ" ("My soul shall praise the power of Your works, and all within me Your holy name").
- **v. 1, halakhic instruction "Upon Arising in the Morning" (Sefard Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur machzorim):** the verse is cited as the reason not to say a blessing before relieving oneself: "וכיון שצריך האדם לברך את השם בכל קרביו, אסור לברך עד שינקה את הקרבים" ("since one must bless the Name with all one's innards, one may not bless until the innards are clean").
- **vv. 11–12, piyyut in the Yom Kippur Shacharit repetition (Ashkenaz, Sefard):** "וְכִגְבֹהַּ שָׁמַיִם חַסְדְּךָ לָמוֹ הִגְבַּרְתָּ. וְכִרְחוֹק מִזְרָח מִמַּעֲרָב פִּשְׁעָם הִרְחַקְתָּ" ("As high as heaven You made Your kindness prevail over them; as far as east from west You removed their transgression").
- **v. 12, piyyut of "אֱמֶת" stanzas (Lithuanian selichot, Erev Rosh Hashanah):** "כִּרְחֹק מִזְרָח מִמַּעֲרָב פְּשָׁעִים הַעֲבֵר" ("As far as east from west, remove transgressions").
- **v. 13, refrain of *Lekha Adonai ha-tzedakah* (Edot HaMizrach selichot):** "הַמְּרַחֵם, הוּא יְרַחֵם, עָלֵינוּ כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בָּנִים" ("The Merciful One, may He have mercy on us as a father on his children").
- **v. 13, Selichot for Children's Illness (Siddur Sefard; one stanza with the rubric "only on Ta'anit Esther"):** "תְּרַחֵם עַל בָּנֶיךָ כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בָּנִים" ("Have mercy on Your children as a father has mercy on his children").
- **v. 13, private prayer before Kol Nidrei, printed with candle lighting (Ashkenaz and Sefard Yom Kippur machzorim):** the verse is expanded into a parable: "רַחֵם עָלֵינוּ כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בְּנוֹ שֶׁמָּרַד בְּאָבִיו וְיָצָא מִבֵּיתוֹ" ("have mercy on us as a father has mercy on his son who rebelled and left his house").
- **v. 14, alphabetical petition of the Yom Kippur confession (every Yom Kippur service, Ashkenaz and Sefard):** "יוֹצְרֵנוּ וְעוֹשֵׂנוּ יוֹדֵעַ יִצְרֵנוּ. יֶהֱמוּ רַחֲמֶיךָ" ("Our Former and Maker, who knows our nature, let Your mercy stir"). The preceding line, "חֲמוֹל עַל חֹמֶר מַעֲשֵׂה יָדֶיךָ," strengthens the echo.
- **v. 14, Ma'avar Yabbok (Siftei Renanot; Imrei Noam):** describes the custom of plucking grass at the cemetery while saying the verse, "ותולשין עשבים עם עפרן ואומרים זכור כי עפר אנחנו למען יזכור יום מיתתו" ("they pluck grass with its earth and say 'remember that we are dust,' so as to recall the day of death").
- **vv. 8–11 and 13, Ma'avar Yabbok (Minchat Aharon):** the psalm's own text of vv. 8–13 is read continuously as part of a string of scriptural verses, after verses from Psalm 102.
- **v. 15, Lithuanian selichot (fifth day of the Ten Days):** the whole verse, "אֱנוֹשׁ כֶּחָצִיר יָמָיו", appears in the verse chain before the Thirteen Attributes are recalled.
- **v. 17, Yotzer piyyut of the second day of Rosh Hashanah (Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim):** the half-verse ends a stanza: "זְכֹר נְדִיבֵי עַמִּים אֲבוֹת הָעוֹלָם. חֲשׂוֹךְ עֲבָדֶיךָ מִזָּדוֹן וְנֶעְלָם. וְחֶסֶד יְהֹוָה מֵעוֹלָם וְעַד עוֹלָם" ("Remember the nobles of the peoples, the fathers of the world…").
- **v. 17, Lithuanian selichot (third, fourth and fifth days of the Ten Days):** the whole verse appears in the verse chain beside "תִּתֵּן אֱמֶת לְיַעֲקֹב חֶסֶד לְאַבְרָהָם".
- **v. 17, Ma'avar Yabbok (Minchat Aharon):** a talmudic prooftext (Sanhedrin), "כל אדם שיש עליו חן בידוע שהוא ירא שמים שנאמר וחסד ה' מעולם ועד עולם על יראיו" ("whoever has grace is surely God-fearing, as it is said…").
**v. 19**
- **v. 19, Yotzer for the first day of Rosh Hashanah (Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim).** This is an alphabetical piyyut whose stanzas open "Melekh" and close on biblical phrases. One stanza pairs the psalm's throne with its kingdom: "מֶֽלֶךְ תַּקִּיף בְּמֶמְשָׁלָה. וְכִסְאוֹ תָּלָה לְמַֽעְלָה. וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָֽׁלָה" ("King mighty in dominion, who hung His throne on high, and His kingship rules over all").
- **v. 19, "Imru le-Elohim" in the reader's repetition of Yom Kippur Shacharit (Ashkenaz and Sefard machzorim).** Each strophe of this piyyut ends "לָכֵן יִתְגָּאֶה" plus a biblical phrase. Here the phrase is "לָכֵן יִתְגָּאֶה וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָֽׁלָה" ("therefore let Him be exalted, whose kingship rules over all"). The same piyyut quotes v. 20 (below).
- **v. 19, Yom Kippur Arvit selichot (Edot HaMizrach machzor).** The whole verse is recited within a chain of verses that open with the divine name: "יְהֹוָה בֹּקֶר תִּשְׁמַע קוֹלִי בֹּקֶר אֶעֱרׇךְ־לְךָ וַאֲצַפֶּה׃ יְהֹוָה בַּשָּׁמַיִם הֵכִין כִּסְאוֹ..." ("O LORD, at dawn You hear my voice… The LORD has established His throne in heaven…").
- **v. 19, Hakafot for Simchat Torah (Siddur Sefard).** The full Melokh paragraph is recited during the hakafot, ending "יְהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מֶלֶךְ, וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָׁלָה". Kabbalistic glosses in the text assign its clauses to the four worlds: עשיה, יצירה, בריאה, אצילות.
- **v. 19, Prayer of R. Chaim Yosef David Azulai (the Ḥida), Yom Kippur Mincha (Edot HaMizrach machzor).** His prayer takes over the Melokh paragraph and its close: "...יְהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מָלָךְ, וּמַלְכוּתוֹ בַּכֹּל מָשָֽׁלָה".
- **v. 19, Ma'avar Yabbok, Siftei Renanot.** This is a kabbalistic discourse on why one should not gaze at the face of the dead. The half-verse is read as the sefirah Malkhut descending into the realm of death "to subdue the husks": "לאכנעא לאינון קליפין כדכתיב ומלכותו בכל משלה".
- **v. 19, Ma'avar Yabbok, Imrei Noam.** In a passage on the merit of teaching children Scripture, the same reading recurs: "ובזה ומלכותו בכל משלה לאכנעא לאינון קליפין".
**v. 20**
- **v. 20, "Imru le-Elohim," Yom Kippur Shacharit repetition (Ashkenaz and Sefard).** A strophe on God's dealings with His blessed people ends with the opening of the verse: "לָכֵן יִתְגָּאֶה בָּרְכוּ יְהוָֹה מַלְאָכָיו" ("therefore let Him be exalted: Bless the LORD, His angels").
- **v. 20, alphabetical Kedushah piyyut, Rosh Hashanah Shacharit repetition, both days (Ashkenaz and Sefard).** The angels are named with the psalm's epithet: "גִּבּֽוֹרֵי כֹֽחַ גְּדֻלָּה. דַּהֲרֽוּהוּ בְּבֵית דְּגִילָה" ("the mighty in strength [ascribe] greatness, they rushed to Him in the house of rejoicing").
- **v. 20, Kedushah piyyut, Yom Kippur Shacharit and Musaf repetitions (Ashkenaz and Sefard).** The piyyut lists the angelic ranks: "גֹּֽבַהּ לָהֶם. וְיִרְאָה לָהֶם. אוֹמְרִים קָדוֹשׁ: גִּבּֽוֹרֵי כֹֽחַ. בְּמִשְׁכְּנוֹת אָהֳלֵיהֶם. אוֹמְרִים בָּרוּךְ" ("height is theirs and awe is theirs, they say 'Holy'; mighty in strength in their dwelling-tents, they say 'Blessed'").
- **v. 20, Neilah, sanctification of the day (Ashkenaz) and repetition (Sefard).** This is a reverse-alphabet piyyut of plea: "גָּדוֹל וְקָטוֹן רֽוּחַ שֵֽׂכֶל הַחֲלִים. גִּבּֽוֹרֵי כֹֽחַ רְצוֹנְךָ חַזֵּק וְהַאֲלִים" ("restore understanding to great and small; strengthen and make mighty the mighty in strength who do Your will"). The phrase here seems to be applied to the human doers of God's will rather than to angels, and "רְצוֹנְךָ" appears to echo "עֹשֵׂי רְצוֹנוֹ" of v. 21.
- **v. 20, Kallir-style yotzer for Parashat Shekalim (Siddur Sefard).** "כַּאֲשֶׁר נִקֵיתָ חֵטְא גִּבּוֹרֵי כֹּחַ" ("as You cleansed the sin of the mighty in strength"). Here the epithet names Israel, whose sin of the calf was atoned by the half-shekel. The Talmud (Shabbat 88a) applies this verse to Israel at Sinai, who put "doing" before "hearing" as the angels do.
- **v. 20, Selichot for Erev Rosh Hashanah (Ashkenaz: Lithuanian and Polish).** The selicha is signed, per its rubric, "יוסף בר יצחק מאורלייאנש". It mourns the vanished righteous: "גָּזּוּ אֱמוּנִים, גִּבּוֹרֵי כֹחַ בְּמֶרֶץ" ("the faithful are cut off, the mighty in strength in their vigour"), leading to "אָבַד חָסִיד מִן־הָאָרֶץ" (Micah 7:2).
- **v. 20, Kinnot for Tisha B'Av day (Ashkenaz).** This is a kinah for the martyrs of the Rhineland communities (אַשְׁפִּירָה Speyer, וָרְמַיְזָא Worms). They are given the angels' title: "עַל יִחוּד שֵׁם מְיֻחָד יִחֲדוּ שֵׁם בִּגְבוּרָה, גִּבּוֹרֵי כֹּחַ עוֹשֵׂי דְּבָרוֹ לְמַהֲרָה" ("for the unity of the One Name they proclaimed the Name in might, mighty in strength, swift to do His word").
- **v. 20, Shabbat-evening zemer (Siddur Sefard, Zemirot; the catalogue's label "Shema" is a mislabel).** In the zemer with the refrain "יְעִידוּן יַגִּידוּן כֻּלָּם כְּאֶחָד": "הָאֵל הַבּוֹרֵא שְׁחָקִים בְאָמְרוֹ. גִּבּוֹרֵי כֹחַ עוֹשֵׂי דְבָרוֹ" ("God who created the skies by His word, [and] the mighty in strength who do His word").
- **v. 20, Ma'avar Yabbok, Sefat Emet.** A kabbalistic passage on the soul of the deceased speaks of the strengthening of "כח עושי דברו" of God in this world.
- **v. 20, Ma'avar Yabbok, Siftei Renanot.** The angels formed from a person's Torah and deeds are called "גבורי כחו של האדם" ("the mighty-in-strength of the man"). The passage draws on the Sinai tradition of נעשה ונשמע.
**vv. 20–22**
- **vv. 20–22, piyyut on the angelic and earthly dominions, Yom Kippur Shacharit repetition (Edot HaMizrach).** The piyyut describes the heavenly hosts and caps its sections with the psalm's closing verses.
  - **v. 20:** "הַכֹּל עֲבָדֶיךָ מְשָׁרְתֵי פָנֶיךָ. גִּבֹּרֵי כֹחַ עֹשֵׂי רְצוֹנֶךָ" ("all are Your servants who minister before You, mighty in strength, doing Your will"). This blends vv. 20 and 21.
  - **v. 21:** "וְלָהֶם קָרָא מְשׁוֹרֵר בְּמִקְרָאָיו. בָּרְכוּ יְהֹוָה כָּל־צְבָאָיו" ("and to them the Psalmist called in his verses: Bless the LORD, all His hosts").
  - **v. 22:** "וּבְכֵן זִמֵּר נְעִים זְמִירוֹת לְעֹשָׂיו. בָּרְכוּ יְהֹוָה כָּל־מַעֲשָׂיו", followed by "בָּרְכוּ יְהֹוָה בְּכָל־מְקוֹמוֹת מֶמְשַׁלְתּוֹ" ("and so the sweet singer sang to his Maker: Bless the LORD, all His works… in all places of His dominion").
  - The piyyut thus presents the psalm's closing summons as David's address to the angels and to all creation.
- **vv. 20–22, Ma'avar Yabbok, Minchat Aharon.** The closing verses are recited whole within a chain of verses. The chain runs from earlier verses of the same psalm (vv. 8–13, "רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן יְהוָה…") through "בָּרֲכוּ יְיָ מַלְאָכָיו… בָּרֲכִי נַפְשִׁי אֶת יְהוָה" and on to "יְהִי כְבוֹד יְיָ לְעוֹלָם".
