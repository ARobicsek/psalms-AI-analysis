### P6 (flagged)

**Before:**

When that Voice hits land in verses 5 through 7, its verbs and objects dismantle the prestige of earthly kings and rival gods alike. The poet uses staircase parallelism: a clause starts an action, pauses to name the LORD, and repeats the verb with a sharper geographical focus. At the same time, the line crackles with hard *b*’s, *sh*’s, and trilled *r*’s: קוֹל ה׳ שֹׁבֵר אֲרָזִים, וַיְשַׁבֵּר ה׳ אֶת־אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן (*QŌL yə-hō-WĀH shō-VĒR 'a-rā-ZIYM, way-shab-BĒR yə-hō-WĀH 'eth-'ar-ZĒY hal-lə-vā-NŌN*), "the voice of the LORD breaks cedars; the LORD shatters the cedars of Lebanon" (v. 5). Two verses later, in a lone three-beat line that flashes between longer couplets, the Voice "hews out flames of fire": קוֹל־ה׳ חֹצֵב לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ (v. 7). Why pair the shattering of Lebanon’s cedars with חֹצֵב (*ḥō-TSĒV*), "hews"? In its other four biblical appearances, that exact participial form always describes construction workers hewing stone or rock-vats. Three of those passages describe Solomon’s eighty thousand חֹצֵב בָּהָר, "hewers in the mountain" (1 Kings 5:29; 2 Chronicles 2:1, 17), who worked alongside the cutters of Lebanon’s cedars to build the Jerusalem Temple (1 Kings 5:20; Ezra 3:7).

### P8 (flagged)

**Before:**

As the storm sweeps south into the wilderness of Kadesh in verses 8 and 9, its force turns from shattering to labor pains. The verb repeated in verse 8, יָחִיל (*yā-ḤIYL*), "convulses" or "makes writhe," intensifies in verse 9a into יְחוֹלֵל אַיָּלוֹת (*yə-ḥō-LĒL 'ay-yā-LŌTH*), "causes hinds to calve," before the Voice "strips forests bare" (וַיֶּחֱשֹׂף יְעָרוֹת).
