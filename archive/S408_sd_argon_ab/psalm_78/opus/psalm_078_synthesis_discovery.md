## OBSERVATION 1

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 3, 11, 32, 37, 42, 57, 65–66)

**Claim:** Psalm 78 reads like a point-by-point answer to Psalm 44, the Korahite lament that protests the community's innocence and asks why the Lord sleeps. Psalm 44 opens with the fathers' telling (44:2) and claims "we have not forgotten You, nor been false to Your covenant; our heart has not turned back" (44:18–19). Psalm 78 opens with the same telling (v.3) and then documents that the fathers did forget (v.11), were not faithful to His covenant (v.37) and turned back (v.57 וַיִּסֹּגוּ; v.9 הָפְכוּ). It grants Psalm 44's plea all the same: the Lord wakes (v.65) and drives the foe "backward" (v.66). Perhaps Psalm 78 was composed, or placed, as Asaph's reply to that lament: the Lord did wake, but the innocence Psalm 44 claimed is denied.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the lexical network, CONJECTURE for deliberate reply — the shared phrases are dense and rare, but which text answers which cannot be proven.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- "ספרו לנו" with the fathers occurs only three times in Tanakh: Ps 78:3, Ps 44:2 ("אבותינו ספרו־לנו") and Judg 6:13 (computed rare-pair table; micro note v.3). The Judges verse is Gideon's complaint: "ואיה כל נפלאתיו אשר ספרו לנו אבותינו". So the phrase's other two uses are accusations against an absent God, while Ps 78 turns it into an indictment of Israel.
- Ps 44:24 "עורה למה תישן אדני" (micro v.65); Ps 78:65 "וַיִּקַץ כְּיָשֵׁן אֲדֹנָי". This is the only אדני in Ps 78 (divine-name notes), the same name with the same sleep vocabulary.
- Ps 44:11 "תשיבנו אחור מני צר" (micro v.66); Ps 78:66 "וַיַּךְ־צָרָיו אָחוֹר" and Ps 78:42 "מִנִּי־צָר".
- Ps 44:15 "תשימנו משל בגוים" (figurative section, proverb vehicle); Ps 78:2 "אֶפְתְּחָה בְמָשָׁל פִּי".
- Ps 44:18–19 "כל זאת באתנו ולא שכחנוך ולא שקרנו בבריתך לא נסוג אחור לבנו" (general scholarship, text certain), set against 78:32 "בְּכׇל־זֹאת", 78:11 "וַיִּשְׁכְּחוּ", 78:37 "וְלֹא נֶאֶמְנוּ בִּבְרִיתוֹ", 78:57 "וַיִּסֹּגוּ".
- The Jerusalem Talmud (Maaser Sheni 5:5) already pairs Ps 44:24 with Ps 78:65 (rabbinic reception section).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The opening formula, the Lord's sudden awakening, the backward blow, the "mashal" and the insistence on forgetting and covenant-breach all turn out to belong to one argument with a single lament psalm. It also explains why v.65 needs no human cry to precede it: the cry is already on record in Psalm 44.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The sleep verse is a known pairing, but the full network has not been assembled: the bracket of "fathers told us" at the opening, the innocence protest of 44:18–19 answered term by term, and the parallel Gideon complaint.

## OBSERVATION 2

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2, 72 (with v. 39)

**Claim:** One other psalm opens by announcing a riddle with "open", "mashal" and "ḥidah": Ps 49:5 "אטה למשל אזני אפתח בכנור חידתי". The two riddle-psalms resolve on the same consonantal verb-form, ירעם. Ps 49:15 gives its answer: "like sheep they are appointed for Sheol; Death shepherds them (מָוֶת יִרְעֵם)". Ps 78 ends "וַיִּרְעֵם כְּתֹם לְבָבוֹ": David shepherded them. Read as a pair, the riddle of flesh and passing breath (78:39; cf. 49:13, 21) is answered in Ps 49 with Death as shepherd and in Ps 78 with God's chosen shepherd.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the shared form and frame, CONJECTURE for intended dialogue — the forms are consonantally identical but differently vocalized (yiqtol vs. wayyiqtol).

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Ps 49:5 "אַטֶּה לְמָשָׁל אׇזְנִי אֶפְתַּח בְּכִנּוֹר חִידָתִי" (shared-vocabulary parallels list, which matches Ps 78:1–2 on חידה, משל, נטה, אזן, פתח).
- Ps 49:15 "כַּצֹּאן לִשְׁאוֹל שַׁתּוּ מָוֶת יִרְעֵם" (figurative section, sheep vehicle, example 5).
- Ps 78:72 "וַיִּרְעֵם" (MT; phonetic way-yir-ʿĒM); Ps 78:39 "בָשָׂר הֵמָּה רוּחַ הוֹלֵךְ וְלֹא יָשׁוּב".
- Caution: Ps 18:14 וַיַּרְעֵם ("thundered") is a homograph from another root, so the claim rests on the context of sheep and shepherding, not on the letters alone.

**Payoff (what clicks):** This gives a reading of what the "riddle" (v.2) is answered by. The puzzle of mortal, unreliable flesh closes on a shepherd's verb, and the shepherd is not Death. It also makes the psalm's last-but-one word feel like a solution rather than an appendix.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators cite Ps 49:5 for the opening formula, but no one tracks the two riddle-psalms to their shared shepherd verb at the point where each resolves.

## OBSERVATION 3

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3–6, 33, 38

**Claim:** Verse 38 recasts the Exod 34:6–7 / Num 14:18 creed as verbs: רחום is kept, "רב" becomes הִרְבָּה, "ארך אפים" becomes לְהָשִׁיב אַפּוֹ, and "נשא עון" becomes יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן. The creed's closing clause, "visiting the iniquity of fathers on children... to the third and fourth generation", is entirely absent. Instead, vv.3–6 build a chain of about four generations (fathers → us → children → children yet unborn → their children) along which the fathers' *story* passes, not their guilt. The omission is pointed. Num 14 is the spies episode, which the commentators see in vv.32–33, and there the children explicitly "bear your whoredoms" for forty years. The psalm, by contrast, aims at children who will "not be like their fathers".

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the reworking of the creed is clear and the absent clause is certain; reading the omission as purposeful is interpretive but well motivated.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Micro note v.38: "From Exod 34:6 (רחום, truncated; no חנון)"; the macro open question already links v.38 to Exod 34:6–7 and Num 14:18–20.
- Exod 34:7 / Num 14:18 "פקד עון אבות על בנים... על שלשים ועל רבעים" (general scholarship, text certain).
- Macro structure: vv.1–8 "four-generation chain of transmission"; staircase device (vv.3–6).
- Malbim on v.33 quotes Num 14:33 "בניכם יהיו רועים ארבעים שנה... ונשאו את זנותיכם"; Ibn Ezra, Radak and Malbim tie v.32 to the spies (commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** It explains why the psalm's mercy formula drops exactly the part of the creed that would undercut its program of teaching. Generational transmission is redirected from punishment to narrative, and the catechism of vv.1–8 and the mercy of v.38 become one design.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The truncation of Exod 34 is noted, but the missing visitation clause has not been set against the psalm's own four-generation chain.

## OBSERVATION 4

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 5 (with vv. 67, 71)

**Claim:** Verse 5 "וַיָּקֶם עֵדוּת בְּיַעֲקֹב וְתוֹרָה שָׂם בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל" closely echoes the formula of the sister Asaph psalm, Ps 81:6 "עֵדוּת בִּיהוֹסֵף שָׂמוֹ": עדות, then ב + a patriarchal name, then the verb שׂים. In Ps 78 the testimony is lodged in "Jacob", not "Joseph". This fits a psalm that will reject "the tent of Joseph" (v.67) yet has David shepherd "Jacob His people" (v.71). Joseph keeps his place among the people who hold the testimony, but he is no longer its address.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the echo, CONJECTURE for deliberate re-addressing.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Micro note v.5: "עֵדוּת = testimony (cf. Ps 81:6 'עדות ביהוסף שמו', a Joseph connection)".
- Ps 81:6 continues "בצאתו על ארץ מצרים" (general scholarship); compare 78:12 "בארץ מצרים".
- Macro context: the Asaph corpus has Joseph affinities (Pss 80–81); micro note v.71: "'Jacob' here includes Joseph/Ephraim, so rejection of a tribe is not expulsion".

**Payoff (what clicks):** The tension within the Asaph collection (pro-Joseph Pss 80–81 against anti-Ephraim Ps 78) becomes legible in a single word-swap at the start. It also explains why the ending says "Jacob" rather than "Judah".

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier cites Ps 81:6 only as a parallel for the word עדות, without the matching שׂים slot or the substitution of Jacob for Joseph.

## OBSERVATION 5

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 8, 72

**Claim:** The diagnosis of v.8, "דּוֹר לֹא־הֵכִין לִבּוֹ", appears elsewhere in this exact wording for Rehoboam, David's grandson, the king under whom Ephraim broke away: 2 Chr 12:14 "וַיַּעַשׂ הָרָע כִּי לֹא הֵכִין לִבּוֹ". The cure of v.72, "בְּתׇם־לֵבָב", is used of David at 1 Kgs 9:4, but there it is a condition laid on Solomon ("if you walk as David your father walked"). The idiom's other uses therefore move the "unprepared heart" into the house of Judah. This supports the reading that the abrupt ending with David is an implicit warning, not a guarantee.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the concordance facts, CONJECTURE for any claim that Chronicles echoes the psalm; the observation stands on what the idiom's distribution shows.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Concordance "כון לב": 2 Chr 12:14 "כִּ֣י לֹ֤א הֵכִין֙ לִבּ֔וֹ לִדְר֖וֹשׁ אֶת־יְהֹוָֽה".
- Concordance "תם לבב": 1 Kgs 9:4 "וְאַתָּ֞ה אִם־תֵּלֵ֣ךְ לְפָנַ֗י כַּאֲשֶׁ֨ר הָלַ֜ךְ דָּוִ֤ד אָבִ֙יךָ֙ בְּתׇם־לֵבָ֣ב".
- Analyst notes: "Abrupt ending with David, no doxology: implicit warning that Judah too could follow Ephraim".
- General scholarship: the schism occurs under Rehoboam (1 Kgs 12).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The heart vocabulary that runs from v.8 to v.72 does not close cleanly. Outside the psalm, the failing heart of v.8 belongs to David's heir, and the whole heart of v.72 is a conditional demand on that heir. This gives the "riddle" an unsettling sequel.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier lists 2 Chr 12:14 only as a parallel for the idiom; no one notes that it belongs to the Davidide of the schism.

## OBSERVATION 6

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 17, 30, 32 (contrast v. 38)

**Claim:** The adverb עוֹד ("still, again") occurs three times, all in cycle I, and always with Israel: "they went on sinning *still*" (v.17), "their food *still* in their mouths" (v.30), "they sinned *still*" (v.32). The persistence of appetite sits between two statements of persistent sin. God's persistence is never expressed with עוד; it takes a different verb, "וְהִרְבָּה לְהָשִׁיב אַפּוֹ" (v.38). This is minor texture, but it gives Israel and God two separate grammars of "keeping on".

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED for the distribution; the interpretive gloss is light.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Computed repeated forms: עוד 3× (vv.17, 30, 32).
- Micro notes v.17 ("עוֹד recurs vv.30, 32") and v.38 ("idiomatic הִרְבָּה + inf. = 'did so often'").

**Payoff (what clicks):** It shows "the food was still in their mouths" as the hinge of a small three-part chain, with appetite literally framed by sin.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The repetition is listed, but no one has noticed that it is confined to Israel and set against v.38's verb.

## OBSERVATION 7

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 17, 35, 54, 56, 69

**Claim:** The title עֶלְיוֹן appears three times, each time at a moment of rebellion or hollow remembrance: "defying Elyon" (v.17), "they remembered... El Elyon their redeemer" (v.35, in the same breath as the lie of v.36), "they tested and defied God Elyon" (v.56). In Gen 14:18–19, Melchizedek of Salem serves "אל עליון קנה שמים וארץ". Ps 78 applies that creator-verb קנה to Zion ("הַר־זֶה קָנְתָה יְמִינוֹ", v.54) and founds the sanctuary "like the heights... like the earth" (v.69). Rabbinic tradition already sets Gen 14:19 and Ps 78:54 side by side in its list of God's "acquisitions" (Pesachim 87b). Perhaps the Elyon whom Israel defies throughout is, in the end, the Most High whose mountain is Jerusalem.

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE — the distribution and the Pesachim pairing are certain, but authorial intent toward the title connected with Salem is unprovable.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Computed: עליון 3× (vv.17, 35, 56); micro note v.17 "עליון... paired with rebellion at vv.17, 35, 56".
- Micro note v.54 "קנה also 'create' (Gen 14:19 Elyon)".
- Rabbinic reception: Pesachim 87b lists "קֹנֵה שָׁמַיִם וָאָרֶץ" and "הַר זֶה קָנְתָה יְמִינוֹ"; Avot 6:10 likewise.
- General scholarship: Gen 14:18 "מלך שלם... כהן לאל עליון"; Ps 76:3 places Salem in parallel with Zion.

**Payoff (what clicks):** It explains why a psalm of the Elohistic Psalter keeps returning to this particular title in its rebellion scenes: the name they defy anticipates the place they are finally given.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The Elyon pattern and the קנה link are noted separately; joining them through the Zion/Salem title to the climax in vv.54/69 is new.

## OBSERVATION 8

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 24–25 (with vv. 54–55)

**Claim:** In the Gra's custom, Ps 78 is the Psalm of the Day for the second day of Passover. The usual rationale, the plagues and the Exodus, fits any day of the festival. In the diaspora the second day is 16 Nisan, the day of the Omer, when new grain from the land becomes permitted (Lev 23:14). By the biblical narrative this is also when Israel ate the produce of the land and the manna ceased (Josh 5:11–12). Perhaps that is why the psalm of "דְּגַן־שָׁמַיִם" (v.24), heavenly grain, ending with entry into the land (vv.54–55), suits that particular day: the morning heavenly grain gives way to grain of the earth.

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE — the custom and the calendar facts are solid, but no source gives this reason.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Deep research §4: Ps 78 as Shir shel Yom on the second day of Pesach (Ma'aseh Rav, Gra) [corrected by check].
- Ps 78:24 "וּדְגַן־שָׁמַיִם נָתַן לָמוֹ"; vv.54–55 entry and allotment.
- General scholarship: Lev 23:10–14 (Omer; new grain forbidden until that day); Josh 5:11–12 "ויאכלו מעבור הארץ ממחרת הפסח... וישבת המן ממחרת".

**Payoff (what clicks):** It offers a reason for *second day* in place of the generic "Exodus" explanation, and it ties the custom to the psalm's most quoted image (John 6:31's "bread from heaven").

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The custom is usually explained by the plague section alone.

## OBSERVATION 9

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 35, 48, 50, 62

**Claim:** Deut 32:30 joins two clauses: "כִּי־צוּרָם מְכָרָם וַיהוָה הִסְגִּירָם", "their Rock sold them, YHWH delivered them up". Ps 78 splits the verse in two. Israel takes up the first noun as a comfort: "they remembered that God was צוּרָם" (v.35). The psalm keeps the second verb for judgment, first against Egypt (הִסְגִּיר, vv.48, 50) and then against Israel: "וַיַּסְגֵּר לַחֶרֶב עַמּוֹ" (v.62). The Rock they invoked is, in their own source text, the Rock who hands them over.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — both halves come from the same short verse and the Deut 32 dependence of the psalm is well established; deliberate splitting is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Concordance סגר: Deut 32:30, flagged as the confirmation target; micro note v.48 "Hiphil סגר... (Deut 32:30 'ויהוה הסגירם')".
- Micro note v.35 "צורם... Rock title (Deut 32:4, 15, 18, 30)"; figurative rock vehicle example 4 quotes Deut 32:30.
- Ps 78:62 "וַיַּסְגֵּר לַחֶרֶב עַמּוֹ".

**Payoff (what clicks):** It sharpens the "shallow memory" of v.35: Israel remembered the title from a verse whose other half is a sentence of being handed over. The move of Israel into Egypt's place (v.62 = v.48) is then the fulfilment of the remainder of that verse.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** צורם and הסגיר are each tied to Deut 32 separately; no one has noticed that they are the two halves of one verse, divided between Israel's memory and God's verdict.

## OBSERVATION 10

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 9, 18, 24, 34–37, 41–42, 57, 64

**Claim:** The "treacherous bow" of v.57 is the visible tip of a much larger shared cluster with Hosea 7:11–16, Hosea's indictment of Ephraim. Hosea has "Ephraim like a dove, פוֹתָה, without heart" (7:11), set beside Ps 78:36–37 "וַיְפַתּוּהוּ... וְלִבָּם לֹא־נָכוֹן". Hosea has "I would redeem them (אֶפְדֵּם) but they spoke lies (כְּזָבִים) against me" (7:13), set beside 78:42 "פָּדָם" and 78:36 "יְכַזְּבוּ". Hosea has "they did not cry to me בְּלִבָּם... over grain (דָּגָן)" (7:14), set beside 78:18 "בִּלְבָבָם" and 78:24 "דְגַן". Then comes Hos 7:16, with "they return, not upward" (beside 78:34, 41, where turning goes wrong), "like a treacherous bow" (78:57), "they will fall by the sword" (78:64 "בַּחֶרֶב נָפָלוּ") and "their mockery in the land of Egypt". The unexplained Ephraim notice of vv.9–11 belongs to the same prophetic dossier on Ephraim.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the cluster of shared vocabulary, CONJECTURE for the direction of dependence — the density across four consecutive verses is well beyond chance, but which text borrows is open.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Figurative bow vehicle, example 3: full text of Hos 7:16 "יָשׁ֣וּבוּ ׀ לֹ֣א עָ֗ל הָיוּ֙ כְּקֶ֣שֶׁת רְמִיָּ֔ה יִפְּל֥וּ בַחֶ֛רֶב... ז֥וֹ לַעְגָּ֖ם בְּאֶ֥רֶץ מִצְרָֽיִם".
- Micro note v.57 "Hos 7:16 'היו כקשת רמיה'"; Ps 78:64 "כֹּהֲנָיו בַּחֶרֶב נָפָלוּ".
- General scholarship (texts certain): Hos 7:11 "ויהי אפרים כיונה פותה אין לב"; Hos 7:13 "ואנכי אפדם והמה דברו עלי כזבים"; Hos 7:14 "ולא זעקו אלי בלבם... על דגן ותירוש".
- Concordance פתה (Ps 78:36); Ps 78:42 "פָּדָם".

**Payoff (what clicks):** It gives the strongest external footing yet for the enigmatic v.9: "Ephraim" and the "bow" come as a package with Hosea's Ephraim. It accounts for the cluster of mouth, lies and heart in vv.36–37, and it links the Israel-as-Egypt reversal to Hosea's "mockery in the land of Egypt". It is also relevant to the dating debate (the north after 722).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Hos 7:16 is routinely cited for the bow alone; the surrounding verses' פתה, פדה, כזב, לב, דגן and the sword have not been collected.

## OBSERVATION 11

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 38 (with vv. 60–61)

**Claim:** The Tur's reason for opening weekday Maariv with v.38 is that the morning and afternoon services have the daily offerings to atone, while the evening has none. That reason fits the verse's grammar. In "וְהוּא רַחוּם יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן", God Himself is the subject of כפר, with no altar, priest or offering mentioned. Across the whole psalm, no offering is ever named, the wilderness tabernacle never appears, and the only shrine on stage, Shiloh, is abandoned (v.60). The verse that stands in for the missing evening sacrifice comes from a psalm whose atonement is, start to finish, cult-free.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the Tur's reason and the verse's syntax are both in the dossier; the link between them is an inference, not a cited rationale.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Deep research §4: Tur OC 237, "at Shacharit and Minchah the daily offerings atone, but at Ma'ariv there is none, so Ve-hu rachum was instituted" [corrected by check].
- Micro note v.38: "God as subject of כפר (Ps 65:4; 79:9; Deut 21:8); no sacrificial apparatus".
- Ps 78:60 "וַיִּטֹּשׁ מִשְׁכַּן שִׁלוֹ"; the psalm contains no offering vocabulary (it can be checked across vv.1–72).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The liturgical placement turns out to rest on more than a gap in the schedule. The psalm itself is a theology of atonement without sacrifice, and it was chosen for the one hour of the day that has no sacrifice.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The Tur's reason is known; that the psalm as a whole is free of cult, which is what fits it to that reason, is not observed.

## OBSERVATION 12

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 38 (with vv. 43–51, 59–62)

**Claim:** In the rite of court flogging (Mishnah Makkot 3:14), the reader recites Deut 28:58–59, "וְהִפְלָא ה׳ אֶת מַכֹּתְךָ וְאֵת מַכּוֹת זַרְעֲךָ", and closes with Ps 78:38. Deut 28:59 turns פלא, the psalm's word for wonders against Egypt (vv.4, 11, 12), into extraordinary plagues on Israel. The next verse, Deut 28:60, threatens "all the diseases of Egypt". The liturgy thus repeats in miniature the arc of the psalm: Egypt's plagues turned on Israel (vv.59–62 echoing vv.48–50), then restrained wrath ("לֹא יָעִיר כׇּל־חֲמָתוֹ"), recited over a punishment that is itself deliberately capped (thirty-nine lashes, not forty).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the Mishnah's verses are in the dossier and Deut 28:60 is certain; the reading of the rite as a reenactment is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Rabbinic reception: Mishnah Makkot 3:14 (verses recited; v.38 as the close).
- Ps 78 פלא vocabulary (micro vv.4, 11, 12); Israel-as-Egypt reversal (macro device; micro v.62).
- General scholarship: Deut 28:60 "והשיב בך את כל מדוה מצרים"; Deut 25:3 limits the count.

**Payoff (what clicks):** It explains why *this* verse closes a scourging. Its context is God turning Egypt's plagues on His own people and then holding back, which is exactly the posture the court is asked to imitate.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The standard explanation is the arithmetic of 13 words × 3 = 39; the thematic fit of "Egypt's plagues on Israel" is not connected.

## OBSERVATION 13

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 38–39

**Claim:** Pss 78 and 106 both turn on a remembering God, but they ground mercy differently. Ps 106:45 has "וַיִּזְכֹּר לָהֶם בְּרִיתוֹ וַיִּנָּחֵם כְּרֹב חֲסָדָו"; Ps 78:39 has "וַיִּזְכֹּר כִּי־בָשָׂר הֵמָּה". In Ps 78, ברית appears only as something Israel failed to keep (vv.10, 37), and the word חֶסֶד appears nowhere in all 72 verses. Its sister recitals (106, 136) rest on that word. Ps 78's mercy is רחום grounded in creaturely frailty, not in covenant loyalty.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the absence of חסד and the two remembrance verses can be checked; the theological contrast is the plain reading of that distribution.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Related psalms: Ps 106:45 "וַיִּזְכֹּ֣ר לָהֶ֣ם בְּרִית֑וֹ וַ֝יִּנָּחֵ֗ם".
- Ps 78:39 "וַיִּזְכֹּר כִּי־בָשָׂר הֵמָּה"; micro note v.10 "ברית... here and v.37... absent elsewhere"; v.38 truncation of Exod 34 (drops חנון and רב חסד).
- General scholarship: Ps 136's refrain כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ.

**Payoff (what clicks):** It explains why v.38 shed exactly the חסד terms of the creed (see Observation 3), and why mercy here needs no human merit or covenant footing at all. Mercy rests on God's view of what humans are.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The sister psalms are compared for content, but no one has noted the total absence of חסד or the contrast between "remembered covenant" and "remembered flesh".

## OBSERVATION 14

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 39

**Claim:** The verse that grounds divine mercy, "a breath that goes and does not return", provoked rabbinic unease in two separate places. Rashi refuses to read רוּחַ הוֹלֵךְ וְלֹא יָשׁוּב as the life-breath, because "if you say so, you have denied the resurrection", and makes it the evil inclination. Rosh Hashanah 32b bars the verse from the Zikhronot as a remembrance tied to punishment. The tradition sensed that v.39's mercy is bought with an admission of mortal finality too stark to let stand unmodified.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — both receptions are explicit in the dossier; linking them as a single reaction is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Rashi on v.38/39: "ולא יתכן לפרש... רוחם של חיים... כפרת בתחיית המתים" (commentaries); Midrash Tehillim 78:5 likewise reads it as the yetzer.
- Rosh Hashanah 32b: "verses of remembrance that speak of a punishment may not be used... 'So He remembered that they were but flesh'" (rabbinic reception).
- Radak: "ולא ישוב — מדרך הטבע".

**Payoff (what clicks):** It shows the double edge of v.39. The same clause works as compassion inside the psalm and reads as death-sentence outside it, which is why a verse so central to mercy was kept out of the High Holiday liturgy of remembrance.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Each ruling is known in its own setting; reading them together as a response to the verse's finality is not.

## OBSERVATION 15

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 42, 52, 54

**Claim:** Psalm 77 ends "נָחִיתָ כַצֹּאן עַמֶּךָ בְּיַד־מֹשֶׁה וְאַהֲרֹן". Psalm 78, immediately after, retells the image ("וַיַּסַּע כַּצֹּאן עַמּוֹ", v.52) without the leaders. It moves the "hand" back to God: "לֹא־זָכְרוּ אֶת־יָדוֹ" (v.42), "קָנְתָה יְמִינוֹ" (v.54). The only other hand in the psalm is the enemy's: "בְיַד־צָר" (v.61). The editorial sequence of 77:21 and 78:52 makes the erasure of Moses visible as a deliberate rewording of the line just read.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the adjacency and the wording are certain; reading it as editorial intent is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Micro note v.52: "Cf. Ps 77:21 'נחית כצאן עמך'"; macro: Moses and Aaron are never named.
- Liturgical: the Melaveh Malka anthology chains 78:12 directly with 77:21 "נָחִיתָ כַצֹּאן עַמֶּךָ בְּיַד מֹשֶׁה וְאַהֲרֹן", rejoining what the psalm pulls apart.
- Ps 78:42, 54, 61 (יד/ימין).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The much-noted absence of Moses gets a precise mechanism: the reader has just heard "by the hand of Moses and Aaron", and Ps 78 answers "His hand", saving a human shepherd for David alone (vv.70–72).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The 77:21 parallel is cited, but not the relocation of יד as the instrument of the erasure.

## OBSERVATION 16

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 44–51 (esp. v. 49)

**Claim:** The psalm's plague list is short (seven, not ten). The one verse in it that is not a plague, the stacked abstractions of v.49, is where two separate rabbinic readers find the missing numbers. Meiri says its five wrath-terms stand for the five plagues not named. The Haggadah, following R. Eliezer and R. Akiva, counts its terms as four or five components of *each* plague. Both treat v.49 as the psalm's place of counting, because the list around it falls conspicuously short of the canonical ten. This is minor texture.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — both readings are in the dossier; the shared motive is an inference.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Meiri on v.49: "והחמש הנשארות שלא הזכירם כללם באלו החמש התיבות" (commentaries).
- Haggadah (liturgical section) and Midrash Tehillim 78:10 (rabbinic reception).
- Macro open question: seven plagues, gnats, boils and darkness omitted.

**Payoff (what clicks):** It explains why the Haggadah's arithmetic reaches for a psalm verse rather than Exodus: the psalm's own shortfall turns v.49 into a counting device.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Meiri's gap-filling and the Haggadah's multiplication are not usually read as responses to the same anomaly.

## OBSERVATION 17

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 60, 67, 69

**Claim:** 1 Samuel calls Shiloh "בֵּית ה׳" (1:24) and even "הֵיכַל ה׳" (1:9; 3:3). Ps 78 consistently downgrades it to מִשְׁכָּן and אֹהֶל ("a tent He had set among men", v.60; "the tent of Joseph", v.67). The verbs of building, ויבן, and the noun מקדש are kept for Zion (v.69). The Talmud's harmonization, that Shiloh was stone below with curtains above (Zevachim 118a; Yerushalmi Megillah 1:12), exists because it must reconcile Samuel's "house" with this psalm's "tent". The halakhic compromise is a response to the polemic of the psalm's vocabulary.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the vocabulary in both books and the Talmudic sugya are certain; calling the downgrade deliberate is interpretive but in keeping with the macro analysis.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Zevachim 118a and Yerushalmi Megillah 1:12: "כתוב אחד אומר ותביאהו בית ה׳ שילה וכתוב אחד אומר ויטש משכן שילה... בית של אבנים מלמטן ויריעות מלמעלן" (rabbinic reception).
- Macro open question 11: the אהל/משכן trajectory as a possible polemic against Shiloh; micro note v.69 "מקדש vs משכן/אהל".
- General scholarship: 1 Sam 1:9 "מזוזת היכל ה׳"; 3:3 "בהיכל ה׳".

**Payoff (what clicks):** The shift from tent to mountain becomes a matter of contested terms, not only images, and an obscure halakhic detail turns out to record the friction between Ps 78 and Samuel.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The Talmudic sugya is read as architectural harmonization, not as evidence of the psalm's deliberate shift in vocabulary away from "house" and "temple" for Shiloh.
