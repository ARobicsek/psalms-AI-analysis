## OBSERVATION 1

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1–8)

**Claim:** The psalm draws on two source texts, and their words fall in two different zones of the poem. The vocabulary distinctive to Lev 19:15, a law addressed to Israelite judges (עָוֶל, נשא פנים, דַּל, plus שפט/צדק), sits in the indictment and mandate of vv. 2–4. The vocabulary distinctive to Deut 32:8, where the Most High allots the nations (עֶלְיוֹן, נחל, גּוֹיִם, אָדָם, נצב/יצב), sits only in the frame and verdict, vv. 1 and 6–8. Neither set crosses into the other's zone. The two classic readings of the addressees, human judges and divine beings, each attach to one of these zones.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE. The lemma overlaps are computed in the dossier. The claim that the distribution is deliberate is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Lev 19:15 shares עול, דל, צדק, שפט, נשא with vv. 1–4 and 8. Deut 32:8 shares עליון, נצב, נחל, גוי, אדם with vv. 1, 6, 7, 8 (dossier, Shared-Vocabulary Parallels).
- Lev 19:15 text: לֹא־תַעֲשׂוּ עָוֶל בַּמִּשְׁפָּט לֹא־תִשָּׂא פְנֵי־דָל (dossier concordance).
- Rashi, Radak, Meiri and Malbim read the addressees as judges and tie the psalm to judicial law. The modern consensus reads them as patron gods on the basis of Deut 32:8 (dossier, commentaries and deep research).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The long dispute over whether the addressees are judges or gods is explained by the text itself. The charge sheet is written in Israelite court law, and the sentence is written in the language of cosmic allotment. Each tradition reads one half faithfully.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Both intertexts are well known separately. That they divide the poem between them, and that this division matches the split in its history of interpretation, has not been drawn.

## OBSERVATION 2

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 2, 5, 6–8)

**Claim:** The psalm's tie to the Song of Moses goes well beyond the famous 32:8. Deut 32:4 describes El as having no ʿāwel (אֵל אֱמוּנָה וְאֵין עָוֶל צַדִּיק), which is the exact standard the gods fail when they "judge ʿāwel" (v. 2). The Song also has מוֹסְדֵי הָרִים (32:22) and the verb mwṭ (לְעֵת תָּמוּט רַגְלָם, 32:35). Seder Ma'amadot places Ps 82 between Joel 2:26–27 (כִּי בְקֶרֶב יִשְׂרָאֵל אָנִי, matching v. 1's בְּקֶרֶב) and a Torah reading from Deut 32:13ff. The compiler of that unit appears to have preserved the psalm's two hinges.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the lexical links. CONJECTURE that the Ma'amadot compiler had them in mind.

**Anchors:**
- Deut 32:4, "of God, אין עול": BDB entry for עָוֶל (dossier lexicon).
- Deut 32:22 מוֹסְדֵי הָרִים and Deut 32:35 תָּמוּט רַגְלָם (dossier concordance, searches for מוסדי ארץ and מוט).
- Seder Ma'amadot: Joel 2:26–27, then Ps 82, then Deut 32:13ff (dossier, Liturgical "Echoes").
- General scholarship: Deut 32:17 speaks of אֱלֹהִים לֹא יְדָעוּם, gods Israel "did not know". This lies inside the Ma'amadot reading only if that reading runs on past 32:13, as the "ff" suggests.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The gods are measured against the Song's own definition of El, not only against its allotment scheme. A medieval liturgical arrangement turns out to have held the same intertexts together.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The reversal of 32:8 is standard. The further threads to 32:4, 22 and 35, and the Ma'amadot pairing as evidence of an early reader making them, are not.

## OBSERVATION 3

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm

**Claim:** The psalm contains no noun for justice or righteousness: no מִשְׁפָּט, צֶדֶק, צְדָקָה, מֵישָׁרִים or אֱמֶת. The only abstract moral noun in it is עָוֶל, injustice. Justice appears only as verbs: šp̄ṭ four times, haṣdîqû, palləṭû, haṣṣîlû. Its source texts and near neighbours use these nouns freely: Lev 19:15 has בַּמִּשְׁפָּט and בְּצֶדֶק, and Ps 72:1–2 has מִשְׁפָּטֶיךָ, צִדְקָתְךָ, בְצֶדֶק and בְמִשְׁפָּט.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED for the absence, which can be counted from the eight verses. PROBABLE that it means something.

**Anchors:**
- A full word inventory of vv. 1–8 (psalm text).
- Lev 19:15 text (dossier concordance). Ps 72 shares six lemmas with this psalm (dossier, Shared-Vocabulary).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Justice in Ps 82 is never a possession or an ideal, only something done or left undone. The one abstraction the gods actually produce is ʿāwel, the object of their "judging". The close, "judge the earth", is again a verb, never a restored state.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Commentators list the poor-terms and the špṭ Leitwort. The total absence of justice nouns next to the presence of ʿāwel is not noted.

## OBSERVATION 4

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 2, 3–4, 5, 8)

**Claim:** Within the Asaph collection, Ps 74 holds a dense cluster of Ps 82's materials, all on the human side of the complaint:
- עַד־מָתַי אֱלֹהִים (74:10)
- כׇל־מוֹעֲדֵי־אֵל בָּאָרֶץ, "the meeting-places of El in the land", burned (74:8)
- הִצַּבְתָּ כׇּל־גְּבוּלוֹת אָרֶץ (74:17)
- מָלְאוּ מַחֲשַׁכֵּי־אֶרֶץ נְאוֹת חָמָס (74:20)
- עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן (74:21)
- קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים (74:22)

מוֹעֲדֵי־אֵל and עֲדַת־אֵל are both construct + אֵל built on the root יעד. They line up with the two Ugaritic council expressions the research cites, pḫr mʿd and ʿdt ilm.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the cluster, which is six shared items. CONJECTURE that Ps 82 is composed as an answer. Ps 74:8 is ordinarily read as earthly sanctuaries.

**Anchors:**
- Ps 74 verses as quoted in the dossier, Related Psalms section (skipgram כׇל מוֹעֲדֵי אֵל בָּאָרֶץ; shared roots חשך, נצב, אביון, עני; phrases עד מתי, קום אלהים).
- עֵדָה derives from יעד (Klein, dossier lexicon).
- Ugaritic pḫr mʿd and ʿdt ilm (dossier, deep research §3).
- Radak and Meiri on v. 5 invoke the flood's חָמָס (dossier commentaries), the same noun as 74:20.

**Payoff (what clicks):** In Ps 74 the poor are left in "dark places of the earth" while the meeting-places of El burn. In Ps 82 God stands in the assembly of El and places the darkness in the gods themselves. The lament's question receives its tribunal.

**Novelty of the linkage:** McClellan relates Ps 82 to complaint psalms such as Ps 74 by genre. This item-by-item cluster, and the מועדי־אל / עדת־אל rhyme, are not drawn.

## OBSERVATION 5

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 2, 3, 5, 8)

**Claim:** In the Temple's weekly cycle, Ps 82 (Tuesday) is followed by Ps 94 (Wednesday), and Ps 94 reads like a companion piece:
- הִנָּשֵׂא שֹׁפֵט הָאָרֶץ (94:2) takes up 82:8.
- עַד־מָתַי רְשָׁעִים (94:3) takes up 82:2.
- It supplies the widow that Ps 82 leaves out (94:6, אַלְמָנָה … וִיתוֹמִים).
- It turns "not understanding" into the wicked's slander against God, וְלֹא־יָבִין אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב (94:7), which answers 82:5.
- It asks מִי־יָקוּם לִי … מִי־יִתְיַצֵּב לִי (94:16), naming both of God's postures in Ps 82: standing (v. 1) and rising (v. 8).
- It names the corrupt court, whose judges וְדָם נָקִי יַרְשִׁיעוּ (94:20–21), the forensic counterpart of הַצְדִּיקוּ.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the lexical correspondences. CONJECTURE that the weekly order was chosen for them.

**Anchors:**
- m. Tamid 7:4: third day Ps 82, fourth day Ps 94 (dossier, Rabbinic Reception).
- Ps 94:2–3 (dossier verse note on v. 2); Ps 94:16 (dossier concordance, נצב); Ps 94:8 cited in Sukkah 55a (dossier).
- General scholarship (stake-in-print texts): Ps 94:6, 94:7, 94:20–21.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The open question of the missing widow is filled by the next day's song. Ps 82's unanswered petition ("arise, judge the earth") is the next morning's opening cry. The stand/arise pair returns as a human question.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The dossier notes 94:2–3 as a pre-echo. The full diptych, and its adjacency in the liturgy, are not drawn.

## OBSERVATION 6

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 2, 5, 6–8)

**Claim:** Zeph 3:3–8 is the closest prophetic analogue to the whole scenario of Ps 82, and it is aimed at human officials. In order, it gives:
- princes and judges "in her midst" (שָׂרֶיהָ בְקִרְבָּהּ … שֹׁפְטֶיהָ זְאֵבֵי עֶרֶב, 3:3);
- YHWH "righteous in her midst", who "does no ʿawlâ" and "morning by morning gives his justice to light" (3:5);
- God's own אָמַרְתִּי … אָכֵן (3:7);
- לְיוֹם קוּמִי לְעַד … לֶאֱסֹף גּוֹיִם (3:8).

Each element lines up with Ps 82: בְּקֶרֶב (v. 1), ʿāwel (v. 2), light against darkness (v. 5), the "I said … surely" formula (vv. 6–7), the princes (v. 7) and rising to judge the nations (v. 8).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE as an analogue. Direction of dependence undetermined.

**Anchors:**
- Zeph 3:5, Zeph 3:7 (dossier concordance, עול and אכן searches); Zeph 3:8 (dossier, Figurative "court" and "arise").
- General scholarship: Zeph 3:3 text.

**Payoff (what clicks):** God's "in the midst" set against the officials' "in the midst" shows what בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים stages in v. 1. Daily light against "evening wolves" shows why the gods' failing is darkness. The analogue's human targets also give the judges-reading biblical support.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The dossier cites Zeph 3:7 for the formula and 3:8 for "arise" in separate places. The full sequence of 3:3–8 is never set beside the psalm.

## OBSERVATION 7

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1, 5, 8

**Claim:** The superscription's opening, מִזְמוֹר לְאָסָף אֱלֹהִים, occurs elsewhere only at Ps 79:1: אֱלֹהִים בָּאוּ גוֹיִם בְּנַחֲלָתֶךָ. Ps 79 also has "how long" (79:5) and nations "that do not know you" (אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יְדָעוּךָ, 79:6). Ps 82 reverses each point. The nations who invaded God's inheritance become God's inheritance (תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם). The ignorance moves upstream from the nations to their gods (לֹא יָדְעוּ, v. 5). Perhaps the puzzling בְּ of v. 8 answers 79:1's בְּנַחֲלָתֶךָ, with the roles inverted.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the cluster. CONJECTURE for the preposition.

**Anchors:**
- Computed rare pair לאסף אלהים: v. 1 and Ps 79:1 only (Computed Distributional Facts).
- General scholarship: Ps 79:1, 5, 6 texts.
- The crux of תנחל בְּ (dossier verse notes on v. 8; deep research §2).

**Payoff (what clicks):** A shared opening formula points the reader to a lament about invading nations. Ps 82 is then its cosmic sequel: the nations' crimes are charged to their patron gods.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The computed rare pair has not been used to read Ps 82 against Ps 79.

## OBSERVATION 8

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1–2, 7–8

**Claim:** The exact form תִּשְׁפְּטוּ occurs only three times: Ps 82:2, Ps 58:2 and 2 Chr 19:6. Jehoshaphat's charge to his judges in 2 Chr 19:6–7 holds a dense cluster of the psalm's terms: לֹא לְאָדָם תִּשְׁפְּטוּ ("not for a human do you judge", set against v. 7 כְּאָדָם, "like a human") and אֵין עִם־יְהוָה … עַוְלָה וּמַשֹּׂא־פָנִים (matching v. 2). This lexical cluster is the likeliest trigger for the medieval dating to Jehoshaphat (Radak, Malbim, Meiri) and for the rabbinic pairing of Ps 82:1 with 2 Chr 19:6 (Tosefta, Yerushalmi). Three further facts fit: Chronicles places an Asaphite Levite prophet in his reign (2 Chr 20:14); his prayer says וְאַתָּה מוֹשֵׁל בְּכֹל מַמְלְכוֹת הַגּוֹיִם (20:6); and his name means "YHWH judges", which is the claim of vv. 1 and 8.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the lexical basis. CONJECTURE that the name and 2 Chr 20 shaped the dating.

**Anchors:**
- Computed: תשפטו occurs in 2 Chr 19:6, Ps 58:2 and Ps 82:2 only (Computed Distributional Facts).
- 2 Chr 19:6 quoted in Tosefta Sanh. 1:4 and y. Sanh. 1:1 (dossier); Radak, Malbim and Meiri on v. 1 date the psalm to Jehoshaphat (dossier commentaries).
- General scholarship: 2 Chr 19:7 text; 2 Chr 20:6; 2 Chr 20:14 (Jahaziel, "a Levite of the sons of Asaph").

**Payoff (what clicks):** The judges-reading and its Jehoshaphat setting turn out to rest on concrete shared wording, not homiletic convenience.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The commentators cite 2 Chr 19:6 without noting the rare form, the לאדם/כאדם play, or the ʿawlâ-ūmaśśōʾ-pānîm match.

## OBSERVATION 9

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1–2, 7–8

**Claim:** Ps 58, the third bearer of תִּשְׁפְּטוּ, is a mirror image of Ps 82. It opens by asking whether ʾēlem (often read as "gods") judge בְּנֵי אָדָם uprightly (58:2). It closes אַךְ יֵשׁ־אֱלֹהִים שֹׁפְטִים בָּאָרֶץ (58:12), with ʾĕlōhîm taking a plural participle. Ps 82:1 makes ʾĕlōhîm singular through the participle נִצָּב. It ends with the gods becoming like ʾādām, the very humans they judge in Ps 58, and with God judging "the earth".

**Confidence:** PROBABLE. The texts are secure; אֵלֶם in 58:2 is disputed.

**Anchors:**
- Computed תשפטו distribution (Computed Distributional Facts); Ps 58:2–3 "sister text" (dossier, Analyst's Notes).
- BDB lists עוֹלוֹת in Ps 58:3 (dossier lexicon).
- General scholarship: Ps 58:12 text.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The number-ambiguity of ʾĕlōhîm that v. 1 exploits is used by a sister psalm in the opposite direction. This sharpens how much rides on the single participle נִצָּב.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The two psalms are known as sisters. The mirror-image participle grammar of their closing and opening is not drawn.

## OBSERVATION 10

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1, 8

**Claim:** The dossier asks why God must "arise" in v. 8 when he has stood since v. 1. A plausible answer, offered here as conjecture, comes from b. Sanhedrin 7a. That passage pairs Ps 82:1 (the Shekhinah present with judges who judge truly) with Ps 12:6 ("from the plunder of the ʿăniyyîm, the groan of the ʾebyônîm, now I will arise"), and reads God's "arising" as the Shekhinah departing from a corrupt court. On that reading, קוּמָה is the request that God rise up from the failed council and judge the earth directly. That is exactly where the object of v. 8 moves: from the council to הָאָרֶץ.

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE. It applies the Talmud's reading of קום to v. 8, which the Talmud itself does not do.

**Anchors:**
- Sanhedrin 7a as quoted by Torah Temimah on v. 1 (dossier commentaries).
- Ps 12:6 עַתָּה אָקוּם … אֶבְיוֹנִים (dossier concordance, אביון).
- The open question about נצב → קומה (dossier verse notes on v. 8).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The posture shift becomes meaningful: the sequence is presence, then withdrawal from the corrupt bench, then direct jurisdiction. The poor of Ps 12:6 are the same poor whose plight triggers the rising.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Torah Temimah records the Sanhedrin passage only as a gloss on v. 1. No one connects it to the crux in v. 8.

## OBSERVATION 11

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 1

**Claim:** Two early Jewish versions take the edge off the second divine name in v. 1 in the same way, by reading אֵל as "might". Aquila renders it ἰσχυρός, as he does routinely. The Targum turns עֲדַת־אֵל into כְּנִישַׁת צַדִּיקִין דְתַקִיפִין בְּאוֹרַיְתָא, "the congregation of the righteous who are strong in Torah". Both draw on the lexicon's אֵל "power". This is minor texture.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE that the Targum's תקיפין reflects the "might" sense; it may be free paraphrase.

**Anchors:**
- Targum v. 1 text (dossier psalm text).
- Klein: אֵל "power" (dossier lexicon). Aquila "in the assembly of the Mighty One" (deep research §2, memory).
- General scholarship: Aquila's habitual ἰσχυρός for אֵל.

**Payoff (what clicks):** It shows a shared early strategy for defusing a verse that names El inside an assembly of ʾĕlōhîm.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Aquila's rendering is known. Its convergence with the Targum's תקיפין is not remarked.

## OBSERVATION 12

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1, 7

**Claim:** The pair אֱלֹהִים נִצָּב occurs nowhere else. The participle נִצָּב is used of humanity in the related Ps 39: אַךְ כׇּל־הֶבֶל כׇּל־אָדָם נִצָּב סֶלָה (39:6), "every human, even standing firm, is mere breath". Read together, standing is real only when God does it (v. 1). The gods sentenced to die כְּאָדָם (v. 7) inherit a "standing" that Ps 39 calls hevel. This is minor texture.

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE, a thematic resonance between the two psalms.

**Anchors:**
- Computed: אלהים נצב nowhere else (Computed Distributional Facts).
- Ps 39:6 with נצב, אדם and סלה (dossier, Related Psalms).

**Payoff (what clicks):** It sharpens what the gods lose. They lose not only immortality but the capacity to stand that the psalm reserves for God.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Ps 39 appears only in the computed root list. It has not been read against v. 1.

## OBSERVATION 13

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1, 6

**Claim:** The psalm names its presiding deity by two titles, placed far apart: אֵל (v. 1, עֲדַת־אֵל) and עֶלְיוֹן (v. 6, בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן). Joined, they make אֵל עֶלְיוֹן, the god whose priest Melchizedek is in Gen 14:18. Perhaps this is part of why 11QMelchizedek could cast Melchizedek as the ʾĕlōhîm who judges in v. 1. **Confidence:** CONJECTURE. The pesher states no such reasoning.

**Anchors:**
- 11Q13 identifies the ʾĕlōhîm of v. 1 with Melchizedek (dossier, Analyst's Notes; deep research).
- BDB: אל עליון in Gen 14:18–22 (dossier lexicon, עליון).

**Payoff (what clicks):** It gives a textual hook in the psalm itself for the Qumran choice of judge.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The 11QMelch reading is famous. Its possible grounding in the psalm's split El/Elyon titles is not commonly drawn.

## OBSERVATION 14

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 2

**Claim:** The folk-magical use of Ps 82 in Shimush Tehillim, to succeed in business and win favour with an employer, looks perverse beside the psalm's indictment. There, "lifting the face" is a superior's favourable acceptance of a petitioner (Gen 19:21; Prov 16:15). Most aptly, Jacob approaches a powerful man with a gift hoping אוּלַי יִשָּׂא פָנָי (Gen 32:21).

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE. The manual gives no reason.

**Anchors:**
- Shimush Tehillim entry for Ps 82 (dossier, deep research §4).
- Gen 19:21 and Prov 16:15 as positive senses of the idiom (dossier, Figurative "face").
- General scholarship: Gen 32:21 text.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The magical use is not random. It reads the psalm's most conspicuous idiom in the sense the poem deliberately corrupts.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The deep research offers only a vague inference ("standing before authorities"). The idiom-based explanation is new.

## OBSERVATION 15

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2–3

**Claim:** Read against its source, vv. 2–3 cut one clause out of Lev 19:15. Leviticus forbids partiality in both directions: לֹא־תִשָּׂא פְנֵי־דָל and וְלֹא תֶהְדַּר פְּנֵי גָדוֹל. The psalm moves "lifting the face" onto the רְשָׁעִים. It drops the ban on favouring the dal altogether, and makes the dal the object of a positive command to vindicate. It also drops Leviticus's nouns בַּמִּשְׁפָּט and בְּצֶדֶק. Resh Lakish's puzzle in b. Hullin 134a is the residue of that surgery: what can עָנִי וָרָשׁ הַצְדִּיקוּ mean, given Exod 23:3 וְדָל לֹא תֶהְדַּר בְּרִיבוֹ? His answer, "give from your own", is echoed by Rashi's restriction ("if he is in the right") and by the charity readings of Chomat Anakh and Romemot El. All of these patch the deleted clause.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE.

**Anchors:**
- Lev 19:15 full text (dossier concordance).
- Hullin 134a (dossier, Rabbinic Reception; Torah Temimah on v. 3); Rashi, Chomat Anakh and Romemot El on v. 3 (dossier commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** A cluster of traditional commentary on v. 3 is explained as a reaction to one specific alteration of the Torah's even-handedness law.

**Novelty of the linkage:** The Lev 19:15 echo and the Hullin passage are each in the dossier. Linking the talmudic difficulty to the deleted clause is new.

## OBSERVATION 16

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2–4

## OBSERVATION 27

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 8

**Claim:** The form תִּנְחַל appears in two other telling places.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE that the medieval readings mirror Num 18:20. CONJECTURE that the Levitical irony is intended.

**Anchors:**
- Computed: תנחל in Deut 19:14, Judg 11:2, Num 18:20 (Computed Distributional Facts).
- Ibn Ezra (citing R. Judah Halevi), Radak and Meiri on v. 8 (dossier commentaries). Num 18:20 is cited for the בְּ (dossier verse notes on v. 8).
- General scholarship: Num 18:20 full text; Judg 11:24, 27.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The medieval readings that make the poor God's "inheritance" stand on the Torah's model of God as the Levites' inheritance. Judges 11 shows the theology of v. 8 replacing a once-standard framework.

**Novelty of the linkage:** Num 18:20 is cited only for its syntax. Its link to the Halevi/Radak readings and to the singer's Levitical identity is not made.
