## OBSERVATION 1

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 6–8)

**Claim:** Psalm 82 does not merely allude to Deuteronomy 32:8 conceptually in `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` (v. 6) and `תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם` (v. 8); it disassembles almost the entire lexical inventory of Deuteronomy 32:8 (`בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם בְּהַפְרִידוֹ בְּנֵי אָדָם יַצֵּב גְּבֻלֹת עַמִּים לְמִסְפַּר בְּנֵי אֵל/אֱלֹהִים`) and redistributes eight of its words/roots across vv. 1, 6, 7, and 8 (`נצב`, `אֵל`, `אֱלֹהִים`, `בְּנֵי`, `עֶלְיוֹן`, `אָדָם`, `נחל`, `גּוֹיִם`) with every grammatical and cosmic role inverted. Where Deuteronomy 32:8 has `יַצֵּב` (Hiphil of `נצב`, setting boundaries for the divine sons), separates mortal `בְּנֵי אָדָם` under immortal `בְּנֵי אֵל/אֱלֹהִים`, and has `עֶלְיוֹן` apportion the nations to others (`בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם`, Hiphil of `נחל`), Psalm 82 has God stand (`נִצָּב`, Niphal of `נצב`, v. 1) in `עֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`, collapse the `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` into mortal `כְּאָדָם` (vv. 6–7), and take possession of `כׇּל־הַגּוֹיִם` Himself (`תִנְחַל`, Qal of `נחל`, v. 8).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Computed shared-vocabulary match confirming five non-trivial shared lemmas (`עליון`, `נצב`, `נחל`, `גוי`, `אדם`) plus `בני` and `אל/אלהים` between a single verse (Deut 32:8) and the framing verses of Psalm 82.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Shared-Vocabulary Parallels (computed): Deuteronomy 32:8 (`בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם בְּהַפְרִידוֹ בְּנֵי אָדָם יַצֵּב גְּבֻלֹת עַמִּים לְמִסְפַּר בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל` [4QDeut-j `בני אלהים`]) shares `עליון`, `נצב`, `נחל`, `גוי`, `אדם` with Ps 82:1, 6, 7, 8.
- Psalm 82:1 (`אֱלֹהִים נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`), 82:6 (`וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן`), 82:7 (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`), and 82:8 (`אַתָּה תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Shows that the opening posture `נִצָּב` (v. 1) and the mortality simile `כְּאָדָם` (v. 7) belong to the same systematic grammatical reversal of Deuteronomy 32:8 as `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` (v. 6) and `תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם` (v. 8).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators routinely connect vv. 6 and 8 (`בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` and `תִנְחַל ... גּוֹיִם`) to Deuteronomy 32:8, but overlook that `נִצָּב` (v. 1) and `כְּאָדָם` (v. 7) also come directly from Deuteronomy 32:8 (`יַצֵּב` and `בְּנֵי אָדָם`), completing an eight-word lexical inversion of that single verse.

## OBSERVATION 2

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 2–8)

**Claim:** A structural puzzle of Psalm 82 is why God issues four positive imperatives in vv. 3–4 commanding the `אֱלֹהִים` to carry out their judicial office (`שִׁפְטוּ ... הַצְדִּיקוּ / פַּלְּטוּ ... הַצִּילוּ`) if He is about to abolish their office and sentence them to death in vv. 6–7 (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`). Verse 5—where the poem abruptly drops its 2nd-person plural address (`אַתֶּם`) for 3rd-person plural verbs (`לֹא יָדְעוּ וְלֹא יָבִינוּ בַּחֲשֵׁכָה יִתְהַלָּכוּ יִמּוֹטוּ...`)—supplies the missing dramatic beat: as Ibn Ezra observed on v. 5 (`שהוכיחם ולא ידעו ולא הבינו, או טעמו, שתקו כאילו לא ידעו`), vv. 2–4 function as a final ultimatum to which the accused gods respond with mute incomprehension. The four 2mp imperatives in `-û` of vv. 3–4 (`שִׁפְטוּ`, `הַצְדִּיקוּ`, `פַּלְּטוּ`, `הַצִּילוּ`) are answered in v. 5 by four 3mp indicative verbs in `-û` (`יָדְעוּ`, `יָבִינוּ`, `יִתְהַלָּכוּ`, `יִמּוֹטוּ`), as the Judge turns away from the unresponsive council to record that their incorrigibility is collapsing the earth's foundations before turning back to them in vv. 6–7 (`אֲנִי־אָמַרְתִּי ... אַתֶּם`) to pronounce sentence, and before v. 8 replaces the unheeded plural imperative `שִׁפְטוּ` (v. 3) with the singular imperative `שׇׁפְטָה` addressed to God.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — Grounded in the person-shift (2mp → 3mp → 2mp → 2ms), the 4-for-4 `-û` verb sequence across vv. 3–5, and Ibn Ezra's explicit reading of v. 5 as the council's silent non-response to the rebuke of vv. 2–4.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Psalm 82:2–7 person shifts: 2mp (`תִּשְׁפְּטוּ`, `תִּשְׂאוּ`, `שִׁפְטוּ`, `הַצְדִּיקוּ`, `פַּלְּטוּ`, `הַצִּילוּ`, vv. 2–4) → 3mp (`יָדְעוּ`, `יָבִינוּ`, `יִתְהַלָּכוּ`, `יִמּוֹטוּ`, v. 5) → 2mp (`אַתֶּם`, `כֻּלְּכֶם`, `תְּמוּתוּן`, `תִּפֹּלוּ`, vv. 6–7) → 2ms (`קוּמָה`, `שׇׁפְטָה`, `אַתָּה תִנְחַל`, v. 8).
- Traditional Commentaries, Ibn Ezra on 82:5: `דברי המשורר שהוכיחם ולא ידעו ולא הבינו, או טעמו, שתקו כאילו לא ידעו` ("The words of the poet, that He rebuked them and they neither knew nor understood; or its meaning is: they remained silent as though they did not know").

**Payoff (what clicks):** Resolves both why God gives a job description to beings He executes three verses later and why v. 5 shifts to the 3rd person right between two 2nd-person speeches (vv. 2–4 and 6–7).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Form-critical discussions treat v. 5's 3rd-person shift and vv. 3–4's imperatives as separate anomalies rather than seeing v. 5 (with Ibn Ezra) as the dramatic pause in which the ultimatum of vv. 3–4 goes unheeded, triggering the capital sentence of vv. 6–7.

## OBSERVATION 3

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1–8, with Ps 74:10, 20–22 and Ps 83:19)

**Claim:** Within the Asaph collection (Pss 50, 73–83), the exact invocation `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים` occurs only twice—at the close of Psalm 74 (74:22, `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים רִיבָה רִיבֶךָ`) and at the close of Psalm 82 (82:8, `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`)—and the two psalms share the exact cry `עַד־מָתַי` (74:10; 82:2), the pairing of `עָנִי` and `אֶבְיוֹן` (74:21; 82:3–4), and the darkness of `אֶרֶץ` (74:20 `מַחֲשַׁכֵּי־אֶרֶץ`; 82:5 `בַּחֲשֵׁכָה ... מוֹסְדֵי אָרֶץ`). Psalm 82 answers Psalm 74 by inverting its speaker and advancing its legal stage: the `עַד־מָתַי` that Israel cried *to* God in 74:10 is hurled *by* God at the gods in 82:2, and because God prosecutes that `רִיב` in 82:1–7, the closing plea `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים` advances from "plead Your lawsuit (`רִיבָה רִיבֶךָ`)" in 74:22 to "judge the earth (`שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`)" in 82:8. Immediately afterward, the closing verse of the next psalm and of the entire Asaph collection (Ps 83:19, `וְיֵדְעוּ כִּי־אַתָּה שִׁמְךָ יְהוָה לְבַדֶּךָ עֶלְיוֹן עַל־כׇּל־הָאָרֶץ`) resolves every open tension of Ps 82:5–8: reversing `לֹא יָדְעוּ` (82:5) into `וְיֵדְעוּ`, supplying the personal name `יְהוָה` withheld behind `אֱלֹהִים` throughout Psalm 82, replacing `וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם` ("sons of Elyon, all of you," 82:6) with `לְבַדֶּךָ עֶלְיוֹן` ("You alone are Elyon"), and uniting `כִּי־אַתָּה` and `הָאָרֶץ` (82:8) under a single named deity.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact phrase matches across the Asaph collection documented in the Related Psalms and Concordance sections.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Related Psalms Analysis, Psalm 74: contiguous phrases `עַד־מָתַי` (Ps 74:10 // Ps 82:2) and `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים` (Ps 74:22 // Ps 82:8), plus shared roots `חשך` + `אֶרֶץ` (Ps 74:20 // Ps 82:5) and `עָנִי` + `אֶבְיוֹן` (Ps 74:21 // Ps 82:3–4).
- Concordance for `עליון`: Psalm 83:19 (`וְיֵדְעוּ כִּי־אַתָּה שִׁמְךָ יְהוָה לְבַדֶּךָ עֶלְיוֹן עַל־כׇּל־הָאָרֶץ`) matching Ps 82:5 (`לֹא יָדְעוּ ... כׇּל־מוֹסְדֵי אָרֶץ`), 82:6 (`וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם`), and 82:8 (`שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ כִּי־אַתָּה תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains how Psalm 82 functions as the dramatic hinge of the Asaph collection—turning the communal lament of Psalm 74 (`רִיבָה רִיבֶךָ`) into a cosmic verdict (`שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`) and setting up the climactic naming of `יְהוָה לְבַדֶּךָ עֶלְיוֹן עַל־כׇּל־הָאָרֶץ` at the close of Psalm 83.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Scholars note the generic similarity of `עַד־מָתַי` to communal laments, but have not traced the specific three-step progression from `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים רִיבָה רִיבֶךָ` (74:22) through `קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ` (82:8) to `וְיֵדְעוּ כִּי־אַתָּה שִׁמְךָ יְהוָה לְבַדֶּךָ עֶלְיוֹן עַל־כׇּל־הָאָרֶץ` (83:19).

## OBSERVATION 4

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1–2

**Claim:** The exact consonantal form `תשפטו` (`תִּשְׁפְּטוּ`, v. 2) occurs only three times in the entire Tanakh: in Psalm 82:2 (`עַד־מָתַי תִּשְׁפְּטוּ־עָוֶל וּפְנֵי רְשָׁעִים תִּשְׂאוּ`), its sister council-indictment Psalm 58:2 (`מֵישָׁרִים תִּשְׁפְּטוּ בְּנֵי אָדָם`), and Jehoshaphat's charge to the human judges of Judah in 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 (`כִּי לֹא לְאָדָם תִּשְׁפְּטוּ כִּי לַיהוָה וְעִמָּכֶם בִּדְבַר מִשְׁפָּט ... כִּי־אֵין עִם־יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ עַוְלָה וּמַשֹּׂא פָנִים`). In 2 Chronicles 19:6–7, four distinctive elements of Psalm 82:1–2, 7 appear together—the rare form `תִּשְׁפְּטוּ`, God's presence with the judges inside the court (`וְעִמָּכֶם בִּדְבַר מִשְׁפָּט`), the contrast with `אָדָם`, and the pairing of `עַוְלָה` (cognate to `עָוֶל`) with `מַשֹּׂא פָנִים` (cf. `פְּנֵי רְשָׁעִים תִּשְׂאוּ`)—immediately before an oracle delivered by a Levite "of the sons of Asaph" (2 Chr 20:14). This inner-biblical link explains why Tosefta Sanhedrin 1:4, y. Sanhedrin 1:1, Radak, Meiri, and Malbim all pair Psalm 82:1–2 with 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 and date the psalm to Jehoshaphat's judicial reform: Chronicler's Jehoshaphat had already adapted the language of Psalm 82:1–2 for Israel's human courts.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact distributional count of `תשפטו` (3x in Tanakh) combined with explicit citations of 2 Chr 19:6–7 in Tosefta Sanhedrin 1:4, y. Sanhedrin 1:1, Radak, Meiri, and Malbim on 82:1.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `תשפטו` occurs 3x in Tanakh (Ps 82:2; Ps 58:2; 2 Chr 19:6).
- Rabbinic Reception (Tosefta Sanhedrin 1:4; y. Sanhedrin 1:1:29) and Traditional Commentaries (Radak, Meiri, and Malbim on 82:1): all cite 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 (`ראו מה אתם עושים כי לא לאדם תשפטו כי לה' ועמכם בדבר המשפט`) to explain Ps 82:1–2 and situate the psalm in Jehoshaphat's reign.

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains the textual basis of the classical Jewish "human judges" interpretation—not merely an appeal to Exodus 21–22 or theological avoidance of myth, but an inner-biblical reuse of Psalm 82:1–2's rare vocabulary in 2 Chronicles 19:6–7.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Modern scholars discuss the rabbinic "human judges" reading solely in terms of Exodus 21:6; 22:7–8 and John 10:34, missing the fact that `תִּשְׁפְּטוּ` + `עַוְלָה` + `מַשֹּׂא פָנִים` + divine presence in court links Psalm 82:1–2 directly to 2 Chronicles 19:6–7.

## OBSERVATION 5

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2–4

**Claim:** Psalm 82:2–3 reworks five core terms from Leviticus 19:15 (`לֹא־תַעֲשׂוּ עָוֶל בַּמִּשְׁפָּט לֹא־תִשָּׂא פְנֵי־דָל וְלֹא תֶהְדַּר פְּנֵי גָדוֹל בְּצֶדֶק תִּשְׁפֹּט` — `עָוֶל`, `שׁפט`, `נשׂא פנים`, `דַּל`, `צדק`), but inverts their legal structure: where Leviticus 19:15 (like Exod 23:3) forbids showing partiality either to the `דַּל` or to the `גָּדוֹל` in an ordinary civil lawsuit, Psalm 82 detaches `דַּל` from `תִּשָּׂא פְנֵי`, replaces social rank (`גָּדוֹל`) with violent guilt (`רְשָׁעִים`, v. 2 `וּפְנֵי רְשָׁעִים תִּשְׂאוּ`; v. 4 `מִיַּד רְשָׁעִים`), and makes the poor the direct object of `שִׁפְטוּ־דַל` and of the unique unprefixed Hiphil imperative `הַצְדִּיקוּ` (`עָנִי וָרָשׁ הַצְדִּיקוּ`, v. 3, 1x in Tanakh). Because forensic Hiphil `הִצְדִּיק` elsewhere in Tanakh takes `הַצַּדִּיק` ("acquit the party in the right," Deut 25:1; 1 Kgs 8:32) as its object and never poverty terms, Resh Lakish in *b. Chullin* 134a objected that reading `עָנִי וָרָשׁ הַצְדִּיקוּ` as a courtroom verdict would violate Exodus 23:3 (`וְדָל לֹא תֶהְדַּר בְּרִיבוֹ`), while Rashi and Radak felt compelled to insert the conditional gloss "if he is in the right" (`אִם זַכַּאי הוּא בְּדִינוֹ`). In the psalm's own logic—reinforced by placing `רָשׁ` (v. 3, differing by a single final `ע`) between the two occurrences of `רְשָׁעִים` (vv. 2, 4)—the `עָנִי וָרָשׁ` require no conditional qualifier because their opponents are not neutral civil litigants (`גָּדוֹל`, which Radak on v. 2 tellingly restores in his gloss `חזקים וגדולים ועשירים`) but predatory `רְשָׁעִים` holding them in their grasp (`מִיַּד רְשָׁעִים הַצִּילוּ`), placing the destitute in the forensic slot of `הַצַּדִּיק` in Deuteronomy 25:1.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Grounded in the shared-vocabulary match with Lev 19:15 and Deut 25:1, the SQL count for `הצדיקו` (1x in Tanakh), and the explicit halakhic discussions in *b. Chullin* 134a, Rashi, and Radak on 82:2–3.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Shared-Vocabulary Parallels (computed): Leviticus 19:15 (top single-verse match, sharing `עול`, `דל`, `צדק`, `שפט`, `נשא`) and Deuteronomy 25:1 (`וְהִצְדִּיקוּ אֶת־הַצַּדִּיק וְהִרְשִׁיעוּ אֶת־הָרָשָׁע`).
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `הצדיקו` (exact form without vav) occurs 1x in Tanakh (Ps 82:3).
- Rabbinic Reception (*b. Chullin* 134a / Torah Temimah on 82:3: `מאי הצדיקו אילימא בדינים והא כתיב ודל לא תהדר בריבו אלא צדק משלך ותן לו`) and Traditional Commentaries (Rashi on 82:3: `אִם זַכַּאי הוּא בְּדִינוֹ`; Radak on 82:2: `לפי שהם חזקים וגדולים ועשירים`; Radak on 82:3: `כשהיה הצדק עמהם`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains simultaneously why Psalm 82:2–3 alters Leviticus 19:15's `גָּדוֹל` to `רְשָׁעִים`, why Radak instinctively glossed `רְשָׁעִים` back as `גדולים`, and why Resh Lakish, Rashi, and Radak stumbled over the unqualified command `עָנִי וָרָשׁ הַצְדִּיקוּ`.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Biblical scholars note the echo of Leviticus 19:15 in v. 2, and Talmudists know Resh Lakish's midrash in *Chullin* 134a on v. 3, but the two have not been joined to show that Psalm 82's substitution of `רְשָׁעִים` for Leviticus's `גָּדוֹל` is what licensed using forensic `הַצְדִּיקוּ` directly of the poor.

## OBSERVATION 6

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3–4 (and v. 6)

**Claim:** Although every biblical and Ugaritic prototype for vv. 3–4 pairs the orphan (`יָתוֹם` / Ugaritic `ytm`) with the widow (`אַלְמָנָה` / Ugaritic `almnt`; e.g., Deut 10:18; Isa 1:17 `שִׁפְטוּ יָתוֹם רִיבוּ אַלְמָנָה`; Kirta `KTU 1.16` VI 45–50), Psalm 82:3–4 omits `אַלְמָנָה` entirely and instead doubles `דַּל` in the identical post-imperative slot of both verses (`שִׁפְטוּ־דַל וְיָתוֹם` // `פַּלְּטוּ־דַל וְאֶבְיוֹן`). The phonetic transcription and rare-form counts show that sound and parallelism drive both choices: v. 3 and v. 4 form a five-slot acoustic grid in which every position of v. 4 mirrors v. 3—(1) `שִׁפְטוּ־` (`shif-tū`) // `פַּלְּטוּ־` (`pal-lə-tū`, a unique exact consonantal form in Tanakh chosen over common rescue verbs for its matching `-ṭû` imperative ending), (2) `דַל` // `דַל`, (3) `וְיָתוֹם` (`wə-yā-THŌM`) // `וְאֶבְיוֹן` (`wə-'ev-YŌN`), (4) `עָנִי וָרָשׁ` (`wā-RĀSH`) // `מִיַּד רְשָׁעִים` (`rə-shā-ʿIYM`), and (5) `הַצְדִּיקוּ` (`hats-DIY-qū`, also a unique exact consonantal form in Tanakh) // `הַצִּילוּ` (`hats-TSIY-lū`). Dropping the feminine `וְאַלְמָנָה` preserves the masculine oxytonic rhyme `וְיָתוֹם` // `וְאֶבְיוֹן` and places `אֶבְיוֹן` (`'ev-YŌN`, the lowest needy person, v. 4) in direct acoustic alignment with `עֶלְיוֹן` (`ʿel-YŌN`, the Most High, v. 6) across the pivot of v. 5, whereas the LXX translator—insensitive to the Hebrew sound-grid—reverted to Isaiah 1:17's word order (`Κρίνατε ὀρφανὸν καὶ πτωχόν`) and obscured the doubled `דַּל` by rendering it `πτωχόν` in v. 3 and `πένητα` in v. 4.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — The five-slot phonetic alignment, the uniqueness of the exact forms `פלטו` and `הצדיקו`, and the LXX divergence are established facts; that sound and the `דַּל` graft from Lev 19:15 motivated dropping `אַלְמָנָה` is the best explanation of a conspicuous omission.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Phonetic Transcriptions, vv. 3–4, 6: `shif-tū-DHAL wə-yā-THŌM ʿā-NIY wā-RĀSH hats-DIY-qū` // `pal-lə-tū-DHAL wə-'ev-YŌN miy-YADH rə-shā-ʿIYM hats-TSIY-lū` ... `ʿel-YŌN`.
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `פלטו` (v. 4; 1x in Tanakh) and `הצדיקו` (v. 3; 1x in Tanakh).
- Concordance for `יתום` (Deut 10:18; Isa 1:17; Jer 22:3; Ps 68:6) and Deep Web Research (Ugaritic Kirta `KTU 1.16` VI 45–50 `almnt` // `ytm`), plus LXX text of Ps 82:3–4 (`Κρίνατε ὀρφανὸν καὶ πτωχὸν ... Ἐξέλεσθε πένητα, καὶ πτωχὸν`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Accounts for three anomalies at once: why the widow (`אַלְמָנָה`) is missing from the ancient Near Eastern and biblical orphan formula, why `דַּל` is the only poverty term repeated, and why the LXX reordered v. 3a.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Individual sound pairs in vv. 3–4 have been noted, but not that the entire two-verse mandate is a slot-for-slot acoustic template whose rhyme scheme (`וְיָתוֹם` // `וְאֶבְיוֹן` ↔ `עֶלְיוֹן`) and Lev 19:15 graft (`שִׁפְטוּ־דַל`) displaced `אַלְמָנָה`.

Rosh Hashanah* 31a inserts `בְּחׇכְמָתוֹ` into the third-day creation rationale for Psalm 82.

Rosh Hashanah* 31a look only at `אֶרֶץ` (v. 8) and `עֲדַת־אֵל` (v. 1), missing that "in His wisdom" (`בְּחׇכְמָתוֹ`) comes from linking v. 5 (`לֹא יָדְעוּ וְלֹא יָבִינוּ ... מוֹסְדֵי אָרֶץ`) to Wisdom's third-day creation of `מוֹסְדֵי אָרֶץ` in Proverbs 8:29 (and 3:19).

## OBSERVATION 8

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 6–7

**Claim:** Verse 7's pairing of generic humanity (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`) with high-ranking princes (`וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ`) is not a mismatch between a low-status simile and a high-status simile, because each colon of v. 7 inverts its corresponding colon in v. 6 along a distinct axis: v. 7a (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`) revokes v. 6a's ontological claim (`אֱלֹהִים אַתֶּם`, deathless divine nature → mortal `אָדָם` who dies), while v. 7b (`וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ`) revokes v. 6b's claim of supreme height and collective immunity (`וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם`, exalted sons of the Most High [root `עלה`], "all of you" [`כֻּלְּכֶם`] → "any single one" [`אַחַד`] of the subordinate officials who *falls* [`נפל`]). At the same time, inserting `אַחַד` in the unique form `וּכְאַחַד` (1x in Tanakh) creates an initial phonetic chime between the two similes (`כְּאָדָם` [`kə-'ā-DHĀM`] // `וּכְאַחַד` [`ū-khə-'a-ḤADH`]).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Directly observable in the lexical, morphological, and phonetic alignment of vv. 6a–b and 7a–b and the SQL rare-form count for `וכאחד` (1x in Tanakh).

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Psalm 82:6–7: `אֱלֹהִים אַתֶּם` (v. 6a) ↔ `כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן` (v. 7a); `וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם` (v. 6b) ↔ `וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ` (v. 7b).
- Phonetic Transcriptions, v. 7: `'ā-KHĒN kə-'ā-DHĀM tə-mū-THŪN ū-khə-'a-ḤADH has-sā-RIYM tip-PŌ-lū`.
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `וכאחד` occurs 1x in Tanakh (Ps 82:7).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Resolves the debate over why v. 7 pairs lowly `אָדָם` with exalted `שָׂרִים` and why `אַחַד` is inserted before `הַשָּׂרִים` (both answering `כֻּלְּכֶם` in v. 6b and chiming with `כְּאָדָם` in v. 7a).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators treat vv. 6–7 as a general antithesis between divine and human rather than seeing the strict diagonal split between nature/mortality (6a ↔ 7a) and vertical rank/collective totality (6b `עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם` ↔ 7b `אַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ`).

## OBSERVATION 9

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 5–7

**Claim:** Threatening human judges with "you shall die like a human being (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`)" is a logical tautology if the addressees are already mortal humans, which explains why Rashi—who read vv. 1–5 as addressed to human judges (`הַדַּיָּנִים`)—abruptly pivots at v. 6 to the Sinai-immortality baraita of *b. Avodah Zarah* 5a (`מַלְאָכִים; כְּשֶׁנָּתַתִּי לָכֶם אֶת הַתּוֹרָה, נְתַתִּיהָ לָכֶם עַל מְנָת שֶׁאֵין מַלְאַךְ הַמָּוֶת שׁוֹלֵט עוֹד בָּכֶם`) and glosses `כְּאָדָם` in v. 7 as `כְּאָדָם הָרִאשׁוֹן` ("like the first Adam," while Ibn Ezra, Radak, and Meiri instead posit the death of the judges' angel-like souls). Rashi's Adamic reading is supported by a striking inversion of Genesis 3:4–5, 22 across Ps 82:5–7: where the serpent told the first humans `לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן ... וִהְיִיתֶם כֵּאלֹהִים יֹדְעֵי טוֹב וָרָע` (Gen 3:4–5) and God said `הֵן הָאָדָם הָיָה כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ לָדַעַת טוֹב וָרָע` (Gen 3:22), Psalm 82:5–7 reverses the trajectory—because the `אֱלֹהִים` *do not know* (`לֹא יָדְעוּ`, v. 5; `אֱלֹהִים אַתֶּם`, v. 6), they are told `אָכֵן כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ` (v. 7), using both the emphatic paragogic-nun form `תְּמוּתוּן` (spelled plene only here in Tanakh; cf. defective `תְּמֻתוּן` in Gen 3:3–4) and the rare form `כְּאָדָם` (only 3x in Tanakh: Hos 6:7; Job 31:33; Ps 82:7, each time in a context of transgression).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — Rashi's exegetical pivot at v. 6 and the medieval soul-death explanations in v. 7 are documented in the commentary dossier, as is the 3x distribution of `כאדם`; the lexical inversion of Genesis 3:4–5, 22 (`ידע`, `אֱלֹהִים`, `אָדָם`, `תְּמֻתוּן/תְּמוּתוּן`, `כְּאַחַד`) strongly motivates reading an Adamic resonance in v. 7.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Traditional Commentaries, Rashi on 82:1–5 (`הַדַּיָּנִים`) vs. Rashi on 82:6–7 (`מַלְאָכִים... כְּאָדָם הָרִאשׁוֹן תְּמוּתוּן, אַחֲרֵי שֶׁחִבַּלְתֶּם מַעֲשֵׂיכֶם כְּמוֹהוּ`, citing *b. Avodah Zarah* 5a), and Ibn Ezra, Radak, and Meiri on 82:7 (death of the soul with the body).
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `כאדם` occurs 3x in Tanakh (Hos 6:7; Job 31:33; Ps 82:7); `תמותון` (plene spelling) occurs 1x in Tanakh (Ps 82:7).
- General scholarship / biblical text: Genesis 3:3–5 (`לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן ... וִהְיִיתֶם כֵּאלֹהִים יֹדְעֵי טוֹב וָרָע`) and Genesis 3:22 (`הֵן הָאָדָם הָיָה כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ לָדַעַת טוֹב וָרָע`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains why Rashi abandons his "human judges" reading right at v. 6 in favor of *Avodah Zarah* 5a, why Ibn Ezra, Radak, and Meiri had to spiritualize `תְּמוּתוּן` in v. 7, and why v. 7 uses the paragogic-nun form `תְּמוּתוּן` alongside `כְּאָדָם` and `וּכְאַחַד`.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Readers note Rashi's citation of *Avodah Zarah* 5a on vv. 6–7, but have not seen that Rashi was forced off his vv. 1–5 "judges" reading by the tautology of sentencing mortals to die `כְּאָדָם`, nor traced the five-term reversal of Genesis 3:4–5, 22 (`ידע`, `אֱלֹהִים`, `כְּאָדָם`, `תְּמוּתוּן`, `וּכְאַחַד`) across vv. 5–7.

## OBSERVATION 10

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 6–8

**Claim:** Unlike other biblical judgments on exalted or heavenly figures that punish them with confinement, casting down, or humiliation (e.g., Isa 14:12–15; 24:21–22; Ezek 28:16–17), Psalm 82:7 uniquely sentences the `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` to biological death (`אָכֵן כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`), and v. 8 immediately follows that death sentence with the verb of hereditary succession (`כִּי־אַתָּה תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם`). Reading vv. 6, 7, and 8 together reveals a three-step legal chain—**sons (`בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן`, v. 6) → die (`תְּמוּתוּן`, v. 7) → estate inherited (`תִנְחַל`, v. 8)**: because the nations were allotted as patrimonial estates (`בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם`, Deut 32:8) to the divine sons, those estates could never legally pass by inheritance (`נחל`) so long as their holders remained immortal (as Malbim observes on v. 8: `הם לא ינחלו את הארץ כי הם מוכנים למות ... ואתה תנחל בכל הגוים`). Sentencing the `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` to *die* vacates their allotments, and explains why `תִנְחַל` takes the preposition `בְּ-` (`תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם`), a syntax shared by all four occurrences of the exact form `תנחל` in Tanakh (Num 18:20 `בְּאַרְצָם לֹא תִנְחָל`; Deut 19:14 `תִּנְחַל בָּאָרֶץ`; Judg 11:2 `לֹא־תִנְחַל בְּבֵית־אָבִינוּ`; Ps 82:8) for holding a proprietary estate *within* a larger domain.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Grounded in the lexical sequence `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` (v. 6) → `תְּמוּתוּן` (v. 7) → `תִנְחַל` (v. 8), the SQL count of `תנחל` (4x in Tanakh, always with `בְּ-`), and Malbim's commentary on 82:8.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Psalm 82:6–8: `וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם` (v. 6) → `כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן` (v. 7) → `כִּי־אַתָּה תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם` (v. 8).
- Computed Distributional Facts (Rare forms): `תנחל` occurs 4x in Tanakh (Num 18:20; Deut 19:14; Judg 11:2; Ps 82:8), every time constructed with `בְּ-`.
- Traditional Commentaries, Malbim on 82:8: `הם לא ינחלו את הארץ כי הם מוכנים למות, והם לא ישפטו אותה בצדק, קומה אלהים ואתה שפטה הארץ, כי אתה בראת אותה ואתה תנחל בכל הגוים`.

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains both why the penalty in v. 7 must be *death* (`תְּמוּתוּן`) rather than mere demotion or exile, and why v. 8 uses the specific legal construction `תִנְחַל בְּ-` ("hold an inheritance within") rather than a general verb of ruling.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Scholars treat the "death of the gods" in v. 7 as a mythological motif and `תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם` in v. 8 as a syntax problem, without noticing that in inheritance law only the death (`תְּמוּתוּן`) of the prior estate-holding sons (`בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן`) allows their shares across the nations to be inherited (`תִנְחַל בְּ-`).

## OBSERVATION 11

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1, 3–4, 8

**Claim:** A recurring question in Psalm 82 is why v. 8 petitions God to "arise" (`קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים`) when v. 1 already depicts Him standing (`אֱלֹהִים נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים יִשְׁפֹּט`). In *b. Sanhedrin* 7a (cited in Torah Temimah on 82:1), R. Shmuel bar Nachmani pairs Psalm 82:1 directly with Psalm 12:6 (`מִשֹּׁד עֲנִיִּים מֵאֶנְקַת אֶבְיוֹנִים עַתָּה אָקוּם יֹאמַר יְהוָה`): when judges judge truly, the Divine Presence stays stationed among them (`נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`, 82:1), whereas when they rob the `עֲנִיִּים` and `אֶבְיוֹנִים`, God withdraws from the court to intervene directly (`עַתָּה אָקוּם`, 12:6). Psalm 82 itself enacts this exact transition across its eight verses: in v. 1 God is `נִצָּב` *inside* the delegated council (`בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`); in vv. 3–4 the council betrays the `עָנִי` and `אֶבְיוֹן` (the exact pair of nouns in Ps 12:6); in v. 7 the delegated court is dissolved (`תְּמוּתוּן ... תִּפֹּלוּ`); and in v. 8 the worshiper summons God to fulfill His promise in Psalm 12:6 (`קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`) by stepping out of the abolished intermediate assembly to assume unmediated rule over `הָאָרֶץ`.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Grounded in the explicit pairing of Ps 82:1 and Ps 12:6 in *b. Sanhedrin* 7a (in the commentary dossier) and the shared lexical cluster (`נִצָּב`, `עָנִי`, `אֶבְיוֹן`, `קוּם`).

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Traditional Commentaries, Torah Temimah on 82:1 (citing *b. Sanhedrin* 7a): `כל דיין שדן דין אמת לאמיתו משרה שכינה בישראל שנאמר אלהים נצב בעדת אל בקרב אלהים ישפוט וכל דיין שאינו דן דין אמת לאמיתו גורם לשכינה שתסתלק מישראל שנאמר משוד עניים מאנקת אביונים עתה אקום יאמר ה' וגו'`.
- Concordance for `אביון`: Psalm 12:6 (`מִשֹּׁד עֲנִיִּים מֵאֶנְקַת אֶבְיוֹנִים עַתָּה אָקוּם יֹאמַר יְהוָה`).
- Psalm 82:1 (`נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`), 82:3–4 (`עָנִי ... וְאֶבְיוֹן`), and 82:8 (`קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Explains why `נִצָּב` (v. 1) and `קוּמָה` (v. 8) are not a redundant repetition of standing posture, but two opposite judicial stages—standing *within* a delegated court (`בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`) versus rising (`קוּם`, as promised in Ps 12:6 for the `עָנִי` and `אֶבְיוֹן`) to replace that court across the whole earth.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators treat *b. Sanhedrin* 7a as a homiletical warning to rabbinic judges, without seeing that its pairing of Ps 82:1 (`נִצָּב`) with Ps 12:6 (`מִשֹּׁד עֲנִיִּים מֵאֶנְקַת אֶבְיוֹנִים עַתָּה אָקוּם`) traces the internal trajectory of Psalm 82 from v. 1 through vv. 3–4 to v. 8.
