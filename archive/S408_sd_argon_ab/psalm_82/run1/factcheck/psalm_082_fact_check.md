> Verse 7's pairing of generic humanity (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`) with high-ranking princes (`וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ`) is not a mismatch between a low-status simile and a high-status simile, because each colon of v. 7 inverts its corresponding colon in v. 6 along a distinct axis: v. 7a (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`) revokes v. 6a's ontological claim (`אֱלֹהִים אַתֶּם`, deathless divine nature → mortal `אָדָם` who dies), while v. 7b (`וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ`) revokes v. 6b's claim of supreme height and collective immunity (`וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן כֻּלְּכֶם`, exalted sons of the Most High [root `עלה`], "all of you" [`כֻּלְּכֶם`] → "any single one" [`אַחַד`] of the subordinate officials who *falls* [`נפל`]).
- Claim: עליון derives from עלה.

**C49** · Observation 8 · `count_or_uniqueness`
> At the same time, inserting `אַחַד` in the unique form `וּכְאַחַד` (1x in Tanakh) creates an initial phonetic chime between the two similes (`כְּאָדָם` [`kə-'ā-DHĀM`] // `וּכְאַחַד` [`ū-khə-'a-ḤADH`]).
- Claim: Exact form וכאחד occurs once.

**C51** · Observation 9 — Claim · `commentator_reading`
> **Claim:** Threatening human judges with "you shall die like a human being (`כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`)" is a logical tautology if the addressees are already mortal humans, which explains why Rashi—who read vv. 1–5 as addressed to human judges (`הַדַּיָּנִים`)—abruptly pivots at v. 6 to the Sinai-immortality baraita of *b. **C55** · Observation 9 — Claim · `count_or_uniqueness`
> Rashi's Adamic reading is supported by a striking inversion of Genesis 3:4–5, 22 across Ps 82:5–7: where the serpent told the first humans `לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן ... וִהְיִיתֶם כֵּאלֹהִים יֹדְעֵי טוֹב וָרָע` (Gen 3:4–5) and God said `הֵן הָאָדָם הָיָה כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ לָדַעַת טוֹב וָרָע` (Gen 3:22), Psalm 82:5–7 reverses the trajectory—because the `אֱלֹהִים` *do not know* (`לֹא יָדְעוּ`, v. 5; `אֱלֹהִים אַתֶּם`, v. 6), they are told `אָכֵן כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן וּכְאַחַד הַשָּׂרִים תִּפֹּלוּ` (v. 7), using both the emphatic paragogic-nun form `תְּמוּתוּן` (spelled plene only here in Tanakh; cf. defective `תְּמֻתוּן` in Gen 3:3–4) and the rare form `כְּאָדָם` (only 3x in Tanakh: Hos 6:7; Job 31:33; Ps 82:7, each time in a context of transgression).
- Claim: Plene תמותון occurs only at Psalm 82:7

**C59** · Observation 10 — Claim · `grammar_or_textual`
> **Claim:** Unlike other biblical judgments on exalted or heavenly figures that punish them with confinement, casting down, or humiliation (e.g., Isa 14:12–15; 24:21–22; Ezek 28:16–17), Psalm 82:7 uniquely sentences the `בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן` to biological death (`אָכֵן כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן`), and v. 8 immediately follows that death sentence with the verb of hereditary succession (`כִּי־אַתָּה תִנְחַל בְּכׇל־הַגּוֹיִם`).

**C65** · Observation 11 — Claim · `rabbinic_or_liturgical`
> In *b. Sanhedrin* 7a (cited in Torah Temimah on 82:1), R. Shmuel bar Nachmani pairs Psalm 82:1 directly with Psalm 12:6 (`מִשֹּׁד עֲנִיִּים מֵאֶנְקַת אֶבְיוֹנִים עַתָּה אָקוּם יֹאמַר יְהוָה`): when judges judge truly, the Divine Presence stays stationed among them (`נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`, 82:1), whereas when they rob the `עֲנִיִּים` and `אֶבְיוֹנִים`, God withdraws from the court to intervene directly (`עַתָּה אָקוּם`, 12:6).
- Claim: Sanhedrin 7a pairs Psalms 82:1 and 12:6

**C68** · Observation 11 — Claim · `grammar_or_textual`
> Psalm 82 itself enacts this exact transition across its eight verses: in v. 1 God is `נִצָּב` *inside* the delegated council (`בַּעֲדַת־אֵל בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים`); in vv. 3–4 the council betrays the `עָנִי` and `אֶבְיוֹן` (the exact pair of nouns in Ps 12:6); in v. 7 the delegated court is dissolved (`תְּמוּתוּן ... תִּפֹּלוּ`); and in v. 8 the worshiper summons God to fulfill His promise in Psalm 12:6 (`קוּמָה אֱלֹהִים שׇׁפְטָה הָאָרֶץ`) by stepping out of the abolished intermediate assembly to assume unmediated rule over `הָאָרֶץ`.
- Claim: Both psalms mention poor and needy
