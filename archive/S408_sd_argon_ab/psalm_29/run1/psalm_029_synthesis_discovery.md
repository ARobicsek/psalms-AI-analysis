## OBSERVATION 1

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1–11)

**Claim:** Across the psalm's 4 + 10 + 4 arrangement of the eighteen occurrences of `יְהוָה`, the grammatical role of the divine name and the distribution of the preposition `לְ` invert completely between the outer frames. In vv. 1–2, all four occurrences of the name carry the dative prefix `לַיהוָה` as the indirect object of tribute imperatives (`הָבוּ`, `הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ`), while `יְהוָה` is never a verbal subject; across the storm of vv. 3–9, the prefix `לְ` disappears entirely (`0x`) as `יְהוָה` moves from construct (`קוֹל יְהוָה`, 7x) into unprefixed verbal agency in the B-cola of the three staircases (vv. 3c, 5b, 8b); and in vv. 10–11, `קוֹל` disappears (`0x`), `יְהוָה` stands four times as the unprefixed nominative subject of finite verbs (fronted at the head of the colon in 10a, 11a, 11b), and the prefix `לְ` returns three times—now attached to the cosmos, eternity, and Israel (`לַמַּבּוּל`, `לְעוֹלָם`, `לְעַמּוֹ`).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact morphological and distributional counts across the Masoretic Text of vv. 1–11.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- `לַיהוָה` occurs 4x in vv. 1–2 and 0x in vv. 3–11; unprefixed `יְהוָה` occurs 14x in vv. 3–5, 7–11 (Distributional SQL table: Repeated forms & Divine-name tally).
- Outside the superscription `לְדָוִד`, the prefix `לְ` occurs 4x in vv. 1–2 (`לַיהוָה`), 0x in vv. 3–9 (`לַהֲבוֹת` in v. 7 has a root lamed from `להב`), and 3x in vv. 10–11 (`לַמַּבּוּל` v. 10a, `לְעוֹלָם` v. 10b, `לְעַמּוֹ` v. 11a) (Psalm text, vv. 1–11).
- In v. 1, `הָבוּ` comes from the defective root `יהב` (attested in Biblical Hebrew only in Qal imperatives demanding that something be handed over), whereas v. 11 switches to `יִתֵּן` from `נתן` for YHWH's bestowal of `עֹז` upon `לְעַמּוֹ` (Verse-Level Notes, vv. 1, 11; BDB `יהב`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The circulation of power from heaven to earth is enacted not only by the `עֹז` inclusio (v. 1 ↔ v. 11) but by case grammar: the psalm begins with four datives directed *to YHWH* (`לַיהוָה`), passes through a storm with zero datives where YHWH claims sole verbal agency, and ends with YHWH as fourfold nominative subject directing the dative *to His people* (`לְעַמּוֹ`).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators note the 4 + 10 + 4 count of `יְהוָה` and the `עֹז` inclusio separately, without observing that all four `יְהוָה` in vv. 1–2 are dative `לַיהוָה`, that `לְ` vanishes in vv. 3–9, and that vv. 10–11 convert `יְהוָה` into a fronted nominative while transferring `לְ` to `לַמַּבּוּל`, `לְעוֹלָם`, and `לְעַמּוֹ`.

## OBSERVATION 2

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1, 2, 3, 4, 9c, 11)

**Claim:** The double tribute demanded of the divine beings in v. 1c—`כָּבוֹד וָעֹז` ("glory and strength")—is systematically split in two at the climax of the psalm. All four occurrences of `כָּבוֹד` remain in the heavenly and temple sphere (`כָּבוֹד וָעֹז` v. 1c, `כְּבוֹד שְׁמוֹ` v. 2a, `אֵל־הַכָּבוֹד` v. 3b), culminating when the palace assembly answers the opening summons by crying only the first word of the pair, `כָּבוֹד` (`וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד`, v. 9c); the second word, `עֹז`, is withheld from the entire body of the storm (where v. 4a substitutes `בַּכֹּחַ`) and from the temple cry of v. 9c so that YHWH can hand it down to His people on earth in v. 11a (`יְהוָה עֹז לְעַמּוֹ יִתֵּן`), paired no longer with `כָּבוֹד` but with `שָׁלוֹם`.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact lexical distribution of `כָּבוֹד`, `עֹז`, `כֹּחַ`, and `הָדָר` across vv. 1–11.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- `כָּבוֹד` / `הַכָּבוֹד` occurs 4x (vv. 1, 2, 3, 9c) and 0x in vv. 10–11; `עֹז` occurs 2x (v. 1c and v. 11a) and 0x in vv. 2–10 (Psalm text; Structural Overview).
- Every other noun demanded of the worshippers in vv. 1–2 (`כָּבוֹד` in vv. 1–2, `הַדְרַת` in v. 2b, `קֹדֶשׁ` in v. 2b) reappears inside the storm of vv. 3–9 (`אֵל־הַכָּבוֹד` v. 3b, `בֶּהָדָר` v. 4b, `קָדֵשׁ` v. 8b, `כָּבוֹד` v. 9c), whereas v. 4a uses `בַּכֹּחַ` rather than `בָּעֹז` (Verse-Level Notes, vv. 2, 4, 8, 9).
- In v. 9c, the worshippers fulfill the summons `הָבוּ לַיהוָה כָּבוֹד וָעֹז` by saying only `כָּבוֹד` (`אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד`), while `עֹז` reappears only in v. 11a (`עֹז לְעַמּוֹ יִתֵּן`) alongside `בַשָּׁלוֹם` (v. 11b) (Psalm text, vv. 1, 9c, 11).

**Payoff (what clicks):** This explains two small anomalies at once: why v. 4a qualifies the storm-voice with `בַּכֹּחַ` instead of `בָּעֹז`, and why the temple choir in v. 9c answers the command to give `כָּבוֹד וָעֹז` by shouting only `כָּבוֹד`—because in the psalm's economy, `כָּבוֹד` stays in the palace with the King while `עֹז` is passed through the throne down to Israel.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Standard analyses treat `כָּבוֹד` (vv. 1–2 ↔ v. 9c) and `עֹז` (v. 1 ↔ v. 11) as two separate inclusios, missing that they originate as a single coordinate pair (`כָּבוֹד וָעֹז`) in v. 1 that is deliberately pried apart between heaven (`כָּבוֹד`, v. 9c) and earth (`עֹז`, v. 11).

## OBSERVATION 3

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 5, 11)

**Claim:** The prose direct-object marker `אֶת־` and the Piel verb stem each occur only twice in Psalm 29, and they land on the exact same two clauses: `וַיְשַׁבֵּר יְהוָה אֶת־אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן` (v. 5b) and `יְהוָה יְבָרֵךְ אֶת־עַמּוֹ בַשָּׁלוֹם` (v. 11b). Both verses open in colon A with a Qal verb lacking `אֶת־` (`שֹׁבֵר אֲרָזִים` // `עֹז לְעַמּוֹ יִתֵּן`) and escalate in colon B to `יְהוָה` + Piel verb + `אֶת־` + definite object, with a phonetic echo between the two Piel forms (`way-shab-BĒR` from `שׁבר` and `yə-vā-RĒKH` from `ברך`).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exhaustive check of verb stems and `אֶת־` tokens in vv. 1–11.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- `אֶת־` appears in v. 5b (`אֶת־אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן`) and v. 11b (`אֶת־עַמּוֹ`), and nowhere else in the psalm (Verse-Level Notes, vv. 5, 11).
- `וַיְשַׁבֵּר` (v. 5b, Piel of `שׁבר`) and `יְבָרֵךְ` (v. 11b, Piel of `ברך`) are the only two Piel verbs in the psalm (`יְחוֹלֵל` in v. 9a is Polel of hollow `חיל/חול`), and both follow a Qal verb in the A-colon (`שֹׁבֵר` in v. 5a; `יִתֵּן` in v. 11a) (Verse-Level Notes, vv. 5, 9, 11).
- Phonetic transcriptions of v. 5b (`way-shab-BĒR yə-hō-WĀH 'eth-'ar-ZĒY hal-lə-vā-NŌN`) and v. 11b (`yə-hō-WĀH yə-vā-RĒKH 'eth-ʿam-MŌ vash-shā-LŌM`) (Phonetic Transcriptions, vv. 5, 11).
- Redaction-critical proposals (Kraus, Loretz) argue that v. 11 is a secondary post-exilic/Jerusalemite addition to an older Canaanite core in vv. 3–9 (Deep Web Research, §1; Open Question 10).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Far from being a grammatically disconnected appendix tacked onto an archaic poem, v. 11b (`יְהוָה יְבָרֵךְ אֶת־עַמּוֹ`) is built on the exact Qal-to-Piel + `יְהוָה` + `אֶת־` syntactic template of v. 5b (`וַיְשַׁבֵּר יְהוָה אֶת־אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן`), setting the king's intensive Piel blessing of His people (`יְבָרֵךְ אֶת־עַמּוֹ`) in direct structural counterpoint to the storm's intensive Piel shattering of Lebanon's cedars (`וַיְשַׁבֵּר ... אֶת־אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן`).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The micro notes flag the two occurrences of `אֶת־` in v. 5 and v. 11 merely as a dating caveat about archaic poetry, without noticing that they coincide with the psalm's only two Piel verbs and identical Qal → Piel + `אֶת־` bicolon escalation.

## OBSERVATION 4

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 1–3, 9, 11)

**Claim:** Scattered across four different Talmudic tractates are separate derivations that, when held together, show the Sages read Psalm 29 not merely as a numerical prooftext for the eighteen blessings of the Amidah, but as a complete miniature blueprint of statutory Jewish prayer: its 18 occurrences of `יְהוָה` plus the single epithet `אֵל־הַכָּבוֹד` (v. 3b) give the 18 + 1 blessings of the weekday Amidah; its 7 `קוֹלוֹת` (vv. 3–9) give the 7 blessings of the Shabbat Amidah; its opening three clauses (vv. 1–2) mandate the opening triad of every Amidah (*Avot*, *Gevurot*, *Kedushat HaShem*); and its closing clause (v. 11b) mandates that the Amidah and Priestly Blessing seal with *Shalom*.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Explicitly attested across four Talmudic loci in the dossier.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- 18 `יְהוָה` = 18 blessings of the weekday Amidah, and `אֵל־הַכָּבוֹד הִרְעִים` (v. 3b) = the 19th blessing (*Birkat HaMinim*, against sectarians/arrogant) added at Yavneh (b. Berakhot 28b–29a in Torah Temimah on 29:3; y. Berakhot 4:3:4 in Rabbinic Reception; Deep Web Research).
- 7 occurrences of `קוֹל` in Ps 29 = the 7 blessings of the Shabbat Amidah (y. Berakhot 4:3:7 in Rabbinic Reception; Sacks Ref 1).
- The first three blessings of the Amidah are derived sequentially from vv. 1–2: *Avot* from `הָבוּ לַיהוָה בְּנֵי אֵלִים`, *Gevurot* from `הָבוּ לַיהוָה כָּבוֹד וָעֹז`, and *Kedushat HaShem* from `הָבוּ לַיהוָה כְּבוֹד שְׁמוֹ הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` (b. Megillah 17b:9; b. Rosh Hashanah 32a:6; Rashi on 29:1–2).
- The closing blessing of the Amidah (*Sim Shalom*) and Priestly Blessing is derived from v. 11b `יְהוָה יְבָרֵךְ אֶת־עַמּוֹ בַשָּׁלוֹם` (y. Berakhot 2:4:14 in Rabbinic Reception; b. Megillah 18a in Torah Temimah on 29:11).

**Payoff (what clicks):** In rabbinic liturgy, reciting Psalm 29 at the climax of *Kabbalat Shabbat* (just before the 7-blessing Friday night Amidah) and when returning the Torah to the Ark (just after the Shabbat *Musaf* Amidah) is an enactment of the Amidah's own archetype—the one biblical text that contains the opening triad, the weekday 18+1 count, the Shabbat 7 count, and the closing seal of `שָׁלוֹם` in a single eleven-verse arc.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentaries cite one or two of these Talmudic derivations at their individual verses (vv. 1, 2, 3, or 11), whereas assembling all four across the Bavli and Yerushalmi reveals that the Sages mapped the entire macro-architecture of both the weekday and Shabbat Amidah onto this single psalm.

## OBSERVATION 5

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 3, 7–11)

**Claim:** Although Psalm 29 adapts the conventions of a Canaanite storm-god hymn (where the climax of Baal's thundering voice in KTU 1.4 V and VII is the release of life-giving rain, `mṭr`/`gšm`, upon the earth), the psalm never once mentions rain (`מָטָר` or `גֶּשֶׁם`), clouds, or agricultural fertility. Inside the poem, this omission strips the storm of Baal's seasonal fertility function so that what descends from heaven at the end is not rain on the soil, but covenantal `עֹז` and `שָׁלוֹם` upon `עַמּוֹ` (v. 11); in liturgical reception, this same absence of rain—leaving cosmic waters (`מַיִם רַבִּים` v. 3, `מַבּוּל` v. 10) alongside dry-wilderness fire (`לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ` v. 7, `מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ` v. 8)—is what enabled Jewish tradition to assign the same psalm to two opposite poles of the calendar: the autumn rain-threshold of Sukkot/Shemini Atzeret and the dry late-spring Sinai revelation of Shavuot.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — The textual absence of rain and the dual festival assignments are established facts; reading the omission of rain as the hinge connecting the anti-Baal polemic to the dual Sukkot/Shavuot reception is a strong explanatory inference.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- In Ugaritic KTU 1.4 V 6–9 and VII 25–39, Baal opens a rift in the clouds and gives forth his voice (`ql`) specifically to bestow his rain (`mṭr`) on the earth, whereas Psalm 29 contains no word for rain (`מָטָר` / `גֶּשֶׁם`), clouds, or crop fertility (Deep Web Research, §§1, 3, 7, citing Amzallag 2021).
- Instead of watering vegetation, the storm shatters Lebanon's cedars (v. 5) and strips forests bare (v. 9b), reserving all positive bestowal for `עַמּוֹ` in v. 11 (`עֹז` and `שָׁלוֹם`) (Psalm text, vv. 5, 9, 11).
- Second Temple and rabbinic sources assign Psalm 29 both to the autumn water/rain festival of Sukkot / Shemini Atzeret (LXX superscription `ἐξοδίου σκηνῆς`; b. Sukkah 55a) and to the late-spring Sinai Torah festival of Shavuot (Tractate Soferim 18:3; Mekhilta Yitro; b. Zevachim 116a) (Deep Web Research, §4; Traditional Commentaries, Radak on v. 3).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Amzallag's observation that a "storm-god psalm" strangely lacks rain need not require a volcanic/metallurgical hypothesis: withholding rain both completes the theological transfer from nature-fertility (Baal) to covenant-blessing (YHWH) and explains how a single storm poem could anchor both the autumn Feast of Booths (via `מַיִם רַבִּים` and `מַבּוּל`) and the summer Feast of Weeks (via `לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ`, `מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ`, and `עֹז` = Torah).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Scholarship on the Canaanite background debates the missing rain in isolation from liturgical history, while liturgical scholarship notes the competing Sukkot and Shavuot assignments without seeing that the absence of explicit rain-vocabulary is what made the Sinai/Shavuot reading possible alongside the Sukkot reading.

## OBSERVATION 6

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 1–2 (with Psalm 96:7–12)

**Claim:** Psalm 96 does not merely quote Ps 29:1–2 while replacing `בְּנֵי אֵלִים` with `מִשְׁפְּחוֹת עַמִּים` (96:7–9); across 96:7–12 it systematically rewrites the entire storm-theophany of Ps 29:3–10 (`רעם`, `חיל`, `יהוה מלך`, `יער`) so that every passive or ravaged target of YHWH's storm in Psalm 29 becomes an active worshipper. Moreover, Ps 96:8b's insertion of `שְׂאוּ־מִנְחָה וּבוֹאוּ לְחַצְרוֹתָיו` ("bring an offering and come into His courts") immediately before `בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` explains all three major LXX/Peshitta divergences in Ps 29:1–2: why `הָבוּ` is rendered `Ἐνέγκατε` ("bring [an offering]"), why `בְּנֵי אֵלִים` (consonantally identical to defective `בְּנֵי אֵילִים`) is rendered as the sacrificial object "young rams" (`υἱοὺς κριῶν`), and why `בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` is rendered "in His holy court" (`ἐν αὐλῇ ἁγίᾳ αὐτοῦ`).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — The lexical parallels between Ps 29 and Ps 96:7–12 and the LXX/Peshitta readings are established; that Ps 96:8b's sacrificial Temple-court insertion catalyzed the unified sacrificial-court reading of Ps 29:1–2 in the LXX and Peshitta is highly probable.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Following the verbatim quotation of Ps 29:1–2 in Ps 96:7–9a, Ps 96:9b–12 redeploys the signature roots of Ps 29:3–10 in inverted roles: `חִילוּ מִפָּנָיו כָּל־הָאָרֶץ` (96:9b ↔ `יָחִיל` / `יְחוֹלֵל` in 29:8–9), `אִמְרוּ בַגּוֹיִם יְהוָה מָלָךְ` (96:10 ↔ `אֹמֵר` + `יְהוָה מֶלֶךְ` in 29:9c–10), `יִרְעַם הַיָּם` (96:11 ↔ `הִרְעִים` over the waters in 29:3), and `אָז יְרַנְּנוּ כָּל־עֲצֵי־יָעַר` (96:12 ↔ stripped `יְעָרוֹת` in 29:9b) (Related Psalms Analysis, Psalm 96; Concordance `רעם`).
- Between `הָבוּ לַיהוָה כְּבוֹד שְׁמוֹ` (Ps 29:2a = 96:8a) and `הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` (Ps 29:2b = 96:9a), Ps 96:8b inserts `שְׂאוּ־מִנְחָה וּבוֹאוּ לְחַצְרוֹתָיו` ("bring an offering and come into His courts") (Related Psalms Analysis; Shared-Vocabulary Parallels).
- In Ps 29:1–2 (LXX Ps 28:1–2), the LXX renders `הָבוּ` five times as `Ἐνέγκατε` ("bring/carry"), adds the doublet `ἐνέγκατε τῷ Κυρίῳ υἱοὺς κριῶν` ("bring to the Lord young rams", reading `אֵלִים` as defective `אֵילִם` = "rams", cf. Num 7:23; 29:23 in the `בני אלים` concordance, while the Peshitta translates *only* "offspring of rams"), and both LXX (`ἐν αὐλῇ ἁγίᾳ αὐτοῦ`) and Peshitta (`בדרתא דקודשה`) render `בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` as "in His holy court" (Psalm Text; Verse-Level Notes, vv. 1–2; Deep Web Research, §2).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The three classic translational cruxes of LXX and Peshitta Ps 29:1–2 (`Ἐνέγκατε`, "young rams," and "in His holy court") are not three disconnected scribal blunders; they are a single, internally consistent sacrificial reading of Ps 29:1–2 through the lens of its sister text Ps 96:8–9 ("Bring an offering and come into His courts"), turning a summons to heavenly gods into a call to bring young rams into the Temple court.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Text critics note the influence of Ps 96:8–9 on `ἐν αὐλῇ ἁγίᾳ αὐτοῦ` in v. 2, without connecting that same Temple-offering context (`שְׂאוּ־מִנְחָה`) to why `הָבוּ` became `Ἐνέγκατε` and `בְּנֵי אֵלִים` became sacrificial "young rams" in v. 1, or to how Ps 96:9–12 continues rewriting Ps 29:3–10.

## OBSERVATION 7

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2, 8 (with Psalm 96:9)

**Claim:** The exact consonantal string `קדש` appears twice in Psalm 29—both times as the final word of its verse—linking the heavenly command `הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` ("bow down to YHWH in the majesty of holiness," v. 2) to the southern climax of the storm, `יָחִיל יְהוָה מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ` ("YHWH convulses the wilderness of Kadesh," v. 8). That this consonantal pairing across vv. 2 and 8 was heard as a deliberate structural link in antiquity is demonstrated by Psalm 96:9 (`הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ חִילוּ מִפָּנָיו כָּל־הָאָרֶץ`), which quotes Ps 29:2b in its first colon and immediately supplies the verb `חיל` from Ps 29:8 (`יָחִיל מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ`) in its second colon.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Direct consonantal distribution in Ps 29:2, 8 and verbatim/lexical splicing in Ps 96:9.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Consonantal `קדש` occurs 2x in Psalm 29: vocalized `קֹדֶשׁ` ("holiness") at the end of v. 2 (`בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ`) and vocalized `קָדֵשׁ` ("Kadesh") at the end of v. 8 (`מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ`) (Distributional SQL table: Repeated forms; Verse-Level Notes, vv. 2, 8).

- In Ps 96:9, the bicolon reads `הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ חִילוּ מִפָּנָיו כָּל־הָאָרֶץ`—combining Ps 29:2b (`הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַיהוָה בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ`) with the imperative `חִילוּ` from the root `חיל` of Ps 29:8 (Shared-Vocabulary Parallels, Ps 96:9; Related Psalms Analysis).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Psalm 96:9 provides an inner-biblical witness that ancient Israelite readers heard `בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ` (v. 2) and `יָחִיל ... מִדְבַּר קָדֵשׁ` (v. 8) together: the convulsing (`יָחִיל`) of the wilderness of `קָדֵשׁ` is the earth's involuntary performance of the prostration commanded in `הַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ`.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators either note the `קֹדֶשׁ` / `קָדֵשׁ` consonantal play inside Psalm 29 or note that Psalm 96:9a quotes Psalm 29:2b, without realizing that Psalm 96:9b (`חִילוּ מִפָּנָיו`) splices the verb of Ps 29:8 (`יָחִיל ... קָדֵשׁ`) directly onto Ps 29:2b (`בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ`).

## OBSERVATION 8

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 5–9

**Claim:** In both the Ugaritic Baal Cycle (KTU 1.4 VI 18–38) and the biblical accounts of building the Jerusalem Temple (1 Kgs 5–7; 2 Chr 2; Ezra 3:7), cedars of Lebanon and Sirion (`אֲרָזִים מִן־הַלְּבָנוֹן`, `lbnn // šryn`), forest timber (`יַעַר`), stone-hewing (`חֹצֵב`), and fire (`אֵשׁ` / `לַהֲבוֹת`) are the technical materials and operations used to *construct* a royal temple (`הֵיכָל` / `hkl`). Psalm 29 assembles that entire temple-building lexicon across vv. 5–9b (`אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן`, `לְבָנוֹן וְשִׂרְיֹן`, `חֹצֵב`, `לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ`, `יְעָרוֹת`) and turns every item into an act of the storm's *un-building*—shattering the cedars, hewing flames of fire instead of quarry-stone, and stripping the forests—immediately before stepping into YHWH's already-standing `הֵיכָל` in v. 9c (`וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד`).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — The shared lexical cluster across Ugaritic palace-building, Solomonic Temple-building, and Ps 29:5–9 is exact; reading vv. 5–9b as an ironic inversion of palace-construction before `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` is a strong literary synthesis.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- In KTU 1.4 VI 18–38, Baal's palace (`hkl`) is built by fetching choice cedars (`arz`) from Lebanon and Sirion (`lbnn // šryn`) and kindling fire (`išt` / `nblat`) inside the structure for seven days until the house is cast in silver and gold (Deep Web Research, §3; Structural Overview).
- Out of only 5 occurrences of the exact consonantal form `חצב` in the entire Tanakh, three (1 Kgs 5:29; 2 Chr 2:1, 17) designate Solomon's 80,000 stone-hewers (`חֹצֵב בָּהָר`) sent to Lebanon alongside the cedar-cutters to build the Temple (`הֵיכָל`), and Ezra 3:7 pairs `לַחֹצְבִים` ("to the stone-hewers") with `עֲצֵי אֲרָזִים מִן־הַלְּבָנוֹן` ("cedar trees from Lebanon") for the Second Temple (Distributional SQL table: Rare forms `חצב`; Concordance `חצב` & `ארזים`; Shared-Vocabulary Parallels, Ezra 3:7).
- In Ps 29:5–9, `קוֹל יְהוָה` shatters `אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן` (v. 5), makes `לְבָנוֹן וְשִׂרְיֹן` skip (v. 6), "hews flames of fire" (`חֹצֵב לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ`, v. 7), and strips `יְעָרוֹת` (v. 9b, cf. 1 Kgs 7:2 `בֵּית יַעַר הַלְּבָנוֹן`), culminating in `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד` (v. 9c) (Psalm text, vv. 5–9; Verse-Level Notes, vv. 5, 7, 9).

**Payoff (what clicks):** This resolves the long-standing puzzle of why v. 7 applies a stone-mason's quarrying verb (`חֹצֵב`) to lightning (`לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ`): `חֹצֵב` belongs with `אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן` (v. 5), `לְבָנוֹן וְשִׂרְיֹן` (v. 6), and `יְעָרוֹת` (v. 9b) as the classic vocabulary of constructing a `הֵיכָל` (v. 9c), except that YHWH—unlike Baal or earthly kings—does not quarry Lebanon to build His palace; His voice shatters Lebanon's timber and quarries fire itself while His `הֵיכָל` already resounds with "Glory!"

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators note that Lebanon/Sirion cedars built Baal's palace and Solomon's Temple, and separately note that `חֹצֵב` means "hew stone," without seeing that `חֹצֵב` + `אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן` + `יְעָרוֹת` + `לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ` form the complete technical roster of ancient Near Eastern `הֵיכָל`-construction turned inside out on the threshold of `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` (v. 9c).

## OBSERVATION 10

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 6

**Claim:** In the Septuagint of v. 6 (`Καὶ λεπτυνεῖ αὐτὰς ὡς τὸν μόσχον τὸν Λίβανον, καὶ ὁ ἠγαπημένος ὡς υἱὸς μονοκερώτων`), three seemingly unrelated translational oddities—"He will pulverize (`λεπτυνεῖ`) them," "*the* calf (`τὸν μόσχον`)," and "the beloved (`ὁ ἠγαπημένος`) like a son of unicorns (`ὡς υἱὸς μονοκερώτων`)"—actually lock together through two Pentateuchal intertexts. Reading `וַיַּרְקִידֵם` (via `ד`/`ר` confusion from `דקק`, "grind fine," immediately after `וַיְשַׁבֵּר` "shattered" in v. 5) evoked the shattering and grinding fine (`שבר` + `דקק`, LXX `ἐλέπτυνεν`) of **the Golden Calf** (`הָעֵגֶל` = LXX `τὸν μόσχον`) in Exod 32:19–20 and Deut 9:21, which is why the Greek adds the definite article (`τὸν μόσχον`, "*the* calf"); and reading `שִׂרְיֹן` (via consonantal metathesis) as `יְשֻׁרוּן` (`ὁ ἠγαπημένος`, the standard LXX rendering of Yeshurun in Deut 32:15; 33:5, 26) aligned `בֶן־רְאֵמִים` (`υἱὸς μονοκερώτων`) with Moses's blessing of Yeshurun in Deut 33:17 (`קַרְנֵי רְאֵם` = LXX `κέρατα μονοκέρωτος`), turning a synonymous Canaanite mountain couplet (`לְבָנוֹן // שִׂרְיֹן`) into an antithesis between idolatrous Lebanon pulverized like *the* Calf and Beloved Israel exalted like the `רְאֵם`.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — The Greek renderings and their Pentateuchal LXX matches (`ἐλέπτυνεν ... τὸν μόσχον` in Exod 32:20/Deut 9:21; `ὁ ἠγαπημένος` and `μονοκέρωτος` in Deut 33:5, 17, 26) are exact; that these two Pentateuchal passages jointly shaped the translator's antithesis in v. 6 is the most economical explanation of all three shifts.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- LXX Ps 28:6 reads `Καὶ λεπτυνεῖ αὐτὰς ὡς τὸν μόσχον τὸν Λίβανον, καὶ ὁ ἠγαπημένος ὡς υἱὸς μονοκερώτων` (Psalm Text, v. 6; Verse-Level Notes, v. 6).
- In Exod 32:19–20 (the first entry in the `עגל` concordance) and Deut 9:21, Moses shatters (`וַיְשַׁבֵּר`, cf. Ps 29:5 `וַיְשַׁבֵּר`) the tablets upon seeing the Calf (`אֶת־הָעֵגֶל`, LXX `τὸν μόσχον`) and grinds it until it is fine (`עַד אֲשֶׁר־דָּק`, rendered in LXX Exod 32:20 and Deut 9:21 by `λεπτύνω`: `καὶ ἐλέπτυνεν` / `καὶ ἐλέπτυνα`) (Concordance `עגל`; Verse-Level Notes, v. 6; general LXX scholarship).
- In Deut 32:15 and 33:5, 26, the LXX translates `יְשֻׁרוּן` as `ὁ ἠγαπημένος` ("the beloved"), and in the same chapter (Deut 33:17, third entry in the `ראמים` concordance) blesses Joseph/Israel with `קַרְנֵי רְאֵם` (LXX `κέρατα μονοκέρωτος`, cf. Num 23:22; 24:8 `ὡς δόξא μονοκέρωτος`) (Verse-Level Notes, v. 6; Concordance `ראמים`).

**Payoff (what clicks):** What looks in English translation of the Septuagint like surreal nonsense ("He will pulverize them like the calf, Lebanon, and the beloved is like a young unicorn") turns out to be a learned midrashic translation in Greek: once `עֵגֶל` was read negatively as *the* Golden Calf ground to dust (`λεπτυνεῖ ... ὡς τὸν μόσχον`), the gloriousPentateuchal title `בֶן־רְאֵמִים` (`υἱὸς μονοκερώτων`) could no longer apply to the same idolatrous target and was reassigned to `יְשֻׁרוּן` (`ὁ ἠγαπημένος`).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators explain `λεπτυνεῖ` (`דקק` for `רקד`), `ὁ ἠγαπημένος` (`ישרון` for `שרין`), and `μονοκερώτων` (`ראמים`) as three isolated lexical curiosities in v. 6, without noticing that Exod 32:20/Deut 9:21 (`λεπτύνω` + `ὁ μόσχος`) and Deut 33:5, 17, 26 (`ὁ ἠγαπημένος` + `μονόκερως`) bind all three into a single contrast between the Golden Calf and Yeshurun.

## OBSERVATION 11

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** v. 9a–b (with vv. 5–6)

**Claim:** In the entire Tanakh, the plural of `יַעַר` ("forest") is elsewhere always masculine `יְעָרִים` (5x); only in Ps 29:9b does it take the feminine plural form `יְעָרוֹת` (`קוֹל יְהוָה יְחוֹלֵל אַיָּלוֹת וַיֶּחֱשֹׂף יְעָרוֹת`). The phonetic transcription (`yə-ḥō-LĒL 'ay-yā-LŌTH way-ye-ḥe-SŌF yə-ʿā-RŌTH`) shows why the poet coined or selected this unique feminine plural: it gives `יְעָרוֹת` the exact syllabic, consonantal-glide (`y`), and `-ōth` rhyme template of `אַיָּלוֹת` (`'ay-yā-LŌTH` // `yə-ʿā-RŌTH`), while completing a macro-level A-B // B'-A' chiasm with vv. 5–6 in which northern shattered **trees** (`אֲרָזִים`, v. 5) followed by leaping **animals** (`עֵגֶל` / `בֶן־רְאֵמִים`, v. 6) are mirrored in the south by calving **animals** (`אַיָּלוֹת`, v. 9a) followed by stripped **trees** (`יְעָרוֹת`, v. 9b).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact morphological distribution in BDB/SQL and direct phonetic and structural correspondence across vv. 5–6 and v. 9a–b.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- `יְעָרוֹת` (v. 9b) is a unique consonantal form in the Tanakh (1x); elsewhere the plural of `יַעַר` ("wood, forest") is always masculine `יְעָרִים` (5x: Josh 17:15 margin/Ezek 34:25 Qere, Isa 22:8, Jer 26:18, Mic 3:12, Ezra 2:25) (BDB `יַעַר`; Distributional SQL table: Rare forms `יערות`).
- Phonetic transcription of v. 9a–b: `yə-ḥō-LĒL 'ay-yā-LŌTH way-ye-ḥe-SŌF yə-ʿā-RŌTH` (Phonetic Transcriptions, v. 9).
- Since Bishop Robert Lowth (1753), scholars troubled by the pairing of animals (`אַיָּלוֹת`, "hinds") with trees (`יְעָרוֹת`, "forests") have either revocalized `אַיָּלוֹת` to `אֵילוֹת` ("oaks", RSV, NIV, NET, and Solomon ben Meir's gloss in Radak) or emended `יְעָרוֹת` to `יַעֲלוֹת` ("mountain goats", Ginsberg) (Deep Web Research, §§2, 7; Radak on v. 9).
- In vv. 5–6, trees (`אֲרָזִים` / `אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן`) are paired with young animals (`עֵגֶל` / `בֶן־רְאֵמִים`); in v. 9a–b, birthing animals (`אַיָּלוֹת`) are paired with trees (`יְעָרוֹת`) (Psalm text, vv. 5–6, 9).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Both famous emendations of v. 9a–b (Lowth's "oaks" and Ginsberg's "mountain goats") are refuted by the morphology of `יְעָרוֹת` itself: if the poet had not wanted to pair animals (`אַיָּלוֹת`) with forests, there would have been no reason to bend masculine `יְעָרִים` into the unique feminine plural `יְעָרוֹת` to rhyme with `אַיָּלוֹת`, and changing either word destroys the Trees → Animals (vv. 5–6) // Animals → Trees (v. 9a–b) chiasm across the storm.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Defenders of MT `אַיָּלוֹת` appeal to ancient Near Eastern birth motifs or Job 39:1, without connecting the unique feminine plural morphology of `יְעָרוֹת` (`-ōth` rhyme with `אַיָּלוֹת`) to the inverse tree-animal pairing in vv. 5–6.

## OBSERVATION 12

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** v. 9c (with vv. 1–2, 3–9a)

**Claim:** When the storm cuts abruptly to the sanctuary in v. 9c (`וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד`), the grammar and sound perform a double inversion of vv. 1–9b. First, whereas the divine beings in vv. 1–2 were addressed with 2nd-person masculine plural imperatives (`הָבוּ ... בְּנֵי אֵלִים ... הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ`), v. 9c replaces the plural assembly with a 3rd-person masculine singular collective suffix and singular participle (`כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר`, "all of it is saying") whose grammatical antecedent—reinforced by the Masoretic *atnach* on `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ`—is the `הֵיכָל` itself; second, this singular `כֻּלּוֹ` (`kul-LŌ`) is chosen over plural `כֻּלָּם` (`kul-LĀM`) so that it both rhymes internally with `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` (`ū-və-hēy-khā-LŌ kul-LŌ`) and answers the sevenfold `קוֹל` (`QŌL`, vv. 3–9a) with a phonetic near-mirror (`QŌL` → `kul-LŌ`) that endlich gives articulate speech (`אֹמֵר כָּבוֹד`) to a storm in which YHWH's `קוֹל` never spoke a quoted word.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Direct syntactic, accentual, and phonetic analysis of vv. 1–2 and 3–9.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Plural imperatives and vocative in vv. 1–2 (`הָבוּ` 3x, `בְּנֵי אֵלִים`, `הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ`) shift in v. 9c to singular `כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר` (3ms suffix + ms participle), with the Masoretic *atnach* placed on `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` (Verse-Level Notes, vv. 1–2, 9; Deep Web Research, §2).
- `קוֹל` (`QŌL`, root `קול`) occurs 7x in vv. 3–9a without any quoted divine speech; `כֻּלּוֹ` (`kul-LŌ`, root `כלל`) in v. 9c rhymes directly with `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` (`ū-və-hēy-khā-LŌ`) and chimes phonetically with `QŌL` (sharing the back stop + `l` + `ō` vowel pattern, though from distinct roots and initial consonants `ק` vs. `כ`) while introducing the psalm's sole quoted word, `כָּבוֹד` (Phonetic Transcriptions, v. 9; Verse-Level Notes, v. 9).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The syntactic oddity of `כֻּלּוֹ אֹמֵר` ("all of it says" rather than "all of them say") is simultaneously theological and acoustic: the many `בְּנֵי אֵלִים` lose their plural autonomy by merging into the singular body of YHWH's `הֵיכָל`, and only singular `כֻּלּוֹ` (`kul-LŌ`) can rhyme with `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` and echo the sevenfold `קוֹל` (`QŌL`).

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators note the `קוֹל` / `כֻּלּוֹ` soundplay and the awkwardness of 3ms `כֻּלּוֹ` separately, without connecting the choice of singular `כֻּלּוֹ` (over `כֻּלָּם`) to the rhyme with `וּבְהֵיכָלוֹ` and the grammatical absorption of the plural `בְּנֵי אֵלִים` into a single voice.

## OBSERVATION 13

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 10 (with vv. 3, 11 and Genesis 9:15)

**Claim:** While the noun `מַבּוּל` ("Flood") occurs 12 times in Genesis 6–11 and once in Psalm 29:10, the exact consonantal string `למבול` (with the prefixed preposition `לְ`) occurs **only twice in the entire Tanakh**: in Ps 29:10 (`יְהוָה לַמַּבּוּל יָשָׁב`, vocalized with the article) and in God's post-Flood covenant oath in Genesis 9:15 (`וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה עוֹד הַמַּיִם לְמַבּוּל לְשַׁחֵת כָּל־בָּשָׂר`, vocalized without the article). This unique two-occurrence consonantal link explains both why b. Zevachim 116a explicitly cites Gen 9:15 to interpret Ps 29:10 (`ה׳ לַמַּבּוּל יָשָׁב וַיֵּשֶׁב ה׳ מֶלֶךְ לְעוֹלָם`) as God's oath never again to destroy the world by flood, and why the psalm itself moves from a terrifying storm over `הַמָּיִם` (v. 3) to `לַמַּבּוּל יָשָׁב` (v. 10, sitting in royal restraint over the deluge) to `שָׁלוֹם` (v. 11).

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — Exact consonantal distribution in the Tanakh (2x) and explicit citation of Gen 9:15 in b. Zevachim 116a:23.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- The consonantal string `למבול` occurs 2x in all Tanakh: Ps 29:10 (`לַמַּבּוּל`) and Gen 9:15 (`לְמַבּוּל` in `וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה עוֹד הַמַּיִם לְמַבּוּל לְשַׁחֵת כָּל־בָּשָׂר`); the other 11 occurrences of `מַבּוּל` in Gen 6–11 lack `לְ` (Distributional SQL table: Rare forms `למבול`; Concordance `מבול`).
- In b. Zevachim 116a:23 (and Rashi and Ibn Ezra on v. 10), when the terrified kings ask Balaam whether a flood is coming (`שֶׁמָּא מַבּוּל בָּא לָעוֹלָם – ה׳ לַמַּבּוּל יָשָׁב`), Balaam answers with v. 10b (`וַיֵּשֶׁב ה׳ מֶלֶךְ לְעוֹלָם`) that God already swore after the Flood never again to bring a flood, quoting Gen 9:15 (`וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה עוֹד הַמַּיִם לְמַבּוּל`) (Rabbinic Reception, Verse 10 — Zevachim 116a:23; Rashi and Ibn Ezra on v. 10).
- Parallel judicial idiom `יָשַׁב לְ־` ("sit for [judgment/governance]", cf. Ps 9:8; 122:5) vs. `עַל־הַמָּיִם` in v. 3 (Verse-Level Notes, v. 10; Figurative Language Insights, Throne #2).

**Payoff (what clicks):** What looks in b. Zevachim 116a like a homiletical leap—reading "YHWH sat `לַמַּבּוּל`" as a reassurance that no new flood will destroy the world—is actually anchored in a precise lexical *gezerah shavah* between the only two `למבול` forms in Scripture (Ps 29:10 and Gen 9:15), and illuminates the psalm's own logic: the storm over `הַמָּיִם` (v. 3) ends in `שָׁלוֹם` (v. 11) because YHWH's eternal throne at the `מַבּוּל` (v. 10) guarantees that `הַמָּיִם` will never again become `לְמַבּוּל`.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Commentators note that `מַבּוּל` occurs only in Gen 6–11 and Ps 29:10, without noticing that the prefixed form `למבול` is shared exclusively by Ps 29:10 and the rainbow-covenant oath of Gen 9:15 (`הַמַּיִם לְמַבּוּל`), which is the exact verse b. Zevachim 116a uses to unlock v. 10.
