## Changes
*For exact before/after text, see [psalm_076_copy_edit_diff.md](psalm_076_copy_edit_diff.md).*

1. [9h] **Who Holds the Verbs**: Replaced "The poem's most famous list" with "The poem's list" and changed "It may be the only war poem whose principal casualty is a noun" to "It is a war poem whose principal casualty is a noun."  
   The original made unsupported fame and uniqueness claims. The argument works without ranking the list or claiming unknowable singularity.

2. [9c] **Who Holds the Verbs**: Softened "He borrowed it from Hosea" to "The poet may be reworking a list from Hosea."  
   The shared verb and sequence support a likely echo, but they do not prove direct borrowing as a fact.

3. [9h] **The Song of the Sea, Run Backwards**: Changed "This is why the commentators never agreed" to "This helps explain why commentators disagreed."  
   The original overstated both causation and reception. The revision keeps the point while matching the evidence actually presented.

4. [9b] **A Deliverance Without a Possessive**: Changed "they are not Israel" to "they are not named as Israel."  
   The verse does not identify the saved as Israel, but it does not exclude Israel from the category; the original conclusion outran the evidence.

5. [10, 7] **Liturgical — Full Psalm / Key verses**: Added brief glosses for "haftarah," "Shabbat Ḥol ha-Moʿed," "selichah," "piyyut," and "yotzer," and corrected "You are known" to "You have made Yourself known."  
   These liturgical terms were unexplained for a general reader, and the paraphrase of נוֹדָעְתָּ had shifted the quoted line's sense.

6. [7] **Practical Kabbalah**: Corrected "The only other occurrence of the form רִשְׁפֵי is Song 8:6" to "The only other plural use of the noun is Song 8:6."  
   Song 8:6 does not use the exact same form as Psalm 76:4; the broader statement about plural use preserves the observation accurately.

7. [9h] **Verse 2**: Changed "Most modern readers" to "Many modern readers" and "the fullest afterlife of any in the psalm" to "a long afterlife."  
   The original made broad, unsupported reception claims. The revision keeps the point without asserting an unshown consensus or superlative.

8. [10] **Verse 5**: Replaced "textual crux" with "textual problem," glossed "emend" inline, and glossed "Targum" as "the ancient Aramaic translation."  
   These technical terms were not explained and could leave a non-specialist reader behind.

9. [9b] **Verse 5**: Revised the conclusion about הַרְרֵי־טָרֶף from "It is a known collocation" to a more precise comparison with Song 4:8's pairing of dens and predators' mountains.  
   The cited evidence showed a related image, not the exact same collocation; the original claim overstated what the comparison proved.

10. [7] **Verse 6**: Corrected the claim that the phrase נָמוּ שְׁנָתָם occurs in Nahum to the narrower observation that the sleep verb נָמוּ appears there.  
   Nah 3:18 shares the verb, not the full phrase. The corrected wording preserves the anti-Assyrian link without misstating the text.

11. [9h] **Verse 6**: Changed "The nearest scene in Western art" to "A close parallel in Western art."  
   "Nearest" was an unsupported ranking claim. The analogy remains, but without an unprovable superlative.

12. [9g] **Verse 7**: Revised "horses are normally wakeful at night" to "horses, which are light sleepers."  
   The original common-experience claim was too strong and factually shaky. The revised wording preserves Malbim's point without resting it on a dubious generalization.

13. [9f, 10] **Verse 11**: Changed "the synagogue made the connection for him" to "later synagogue reading makes the connection easy to hear," and replaced "Emendations" with "Proposed corrections."  
   The original sentence implied a later liturgical custom explained an earlier Greek translator, which is chronologically impossible. The revised wording restores clear logic and uses plainer language.

14. [7] **Verse 13**: Corrected "the second-to-last word of the poem" to a simpler comparison between נוֹדָע and נוֹרָא in the closing line.  
   נוֹרָא is not the poem's second-to-last word. The revision preserves the letter-play observation without misstating the line's position.
