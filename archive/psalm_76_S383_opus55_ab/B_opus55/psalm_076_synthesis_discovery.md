## OBSERVATION 1

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (esp. vv. 2, 7, 10, 12)

**Claim:** Psalm 76 contains no first-person form anywhere: no "I," no "we," no "us," no "our." A Zion song celebrating a deliverance "in Judah" never says who was saved in terms of "us." The only people addressed directly are "you (pl.)" in v. 12 (נִדְרוּ וְשַׁלְּמוּ … אֱלֹהֵיכֶם), and the named beneficiaries are "all the lowly of the earth" (v. 10). The classical commentators fill this gap: Rashi makes the saved "Hezekiah and his company," Radak and Alshich make עַנְוֵי אֶרֶץ Israel. The poem withholds exactly the possessive they supply.

**Confidence:** ESTABLISHED — the absence can be verified in the text; the commentators' restrictions are quoted in the dossier.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- No first-person pronouns or suffixes in vv. 2–13 (psalm text).
- 2mp appears only in v. 12, in אֱלֹהֵיכֶם and the plural imperatives (psalm text).
- Ps 46:8, 12, a sister Zion song, has "יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת עִמָּנוּ מִשְׂגָּב־לָנוּ אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב." It uses the same epithet "God of Jacob" but joins it to "us." Ps 76:7 uses the epithet with no "us" (psalm text; Ps 46 quoted in the dossier's liturgical section).
- Rashi on v. 10: "לְהוֹשִׁיעַ חִזְקִיָּהוּ וְסִיעָתוֹ"; Radak on v. 10: "להושיע את ישראל… שהם ענוי ארץ" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The universalism of v. 10 is built into the grammar, not just announced in one verse. The comparison with Ps 46 shows the omission of "us" as a choice. The commentators' narrowing becomes visible as an act of supplying what the text holds back.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier treats v. 10's universalism lexically (כָּל, אֶרֶץ) but never notices that the whole psalm lacks a first-person subject, or that the commentators restore one.

## OBSERVATION 2

**Type:** PATTERN

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 4–13)

**Claim:** A table of subjects shows that every active, transitive verb of force belongs to God: שִׁבַּר, הִשְׁמַעְתָּ, קוּם, לְהוֹשִׁיעַ, תַּחְגֹּר, יִבְצֹר. The enemy's verbs are reflexive or passive (אֶשְׁתּוֹלְלוּ, נִרְדָּם), intransitive states (נָמוּ, יָרְאָה וְשָׁקָטָה), a rhetorical impossibility (מִי יַעֲמֹד), or negated (לֹא מָצְאוּ). The one transitive verb humans get before v. 11 is "did not find," and its object is their own hands. Human agency returns only after the verdict, and only in the register of worship: fury "will praise" (תּוֹדֶךָּ), worshipers "vow and pay," the surrounding peoples "bring tribute."

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the tabulation can be checked. The Hitpolel אשתוללו could be read reflexively ("made themselves spoil"), but even so it is not an attack verb.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Verb inventory vv. 4–13 (psalm text).
- אשתוללו as Aramaizing Hitpolel/reflexive (Micro v. 6; Rashi, Radak, Ibn Ezra on v. 6).
- Macro note: "No fighting is narrated, no Israelite army appears."

**Payoff (what clicks):** The "abolition of war" thesis is enacted in the conjugations. Grammatically, humans may do only three things in this poem: fail, fall silent, or give thanks.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier states the absence of human combatants as a theme but never tabulates the voice and valency of the verbs, which is where the claim gets its exactness.

## OBSERVATION 3

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3–13 (tense system) with the commentary tradition

**Claim:** The major split in the reception history, Sennacherib in 701 (Rashi, Malbim, Meiri) versus the eschatological war of Gog (Radak, Meiri's alternative), maps onto the psalm's own verb system. Perfects and wayyiqtol in vv. 3–10 give the historical reading its footing; imperfects in vv. 11–13 give the future reading its footing. Radak has to force the perfects: he glosses v. 3's וַיְהִי as "וְאָז יִהְיֶה" ("then it will be"). He also admits on v. 13 that he once read the psalm as Assyrian and changed his mind because of the preceding psalm. Meiri resolves the tension by making the whole psalm prophecy "as if it had already come about."

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the tense distribution and the commentators' moves are documented. That the split is *caused* by the tense shift is an inference, though a well-supported one.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Perfect/imperfect shift vv. 3–10 vs. 11–13 (Macro, "Tense shift").
- Radak on v. 3: "ואז יהיה בשלם בסכה"; Radak on v. 13: "כבר במכלול… פירשתי זה המזמור על מחנה אשור… אבל עתה… על גוג מגוג" (Traditional Commentaries).
- Meiri on v. 1: "ודבר בשבח האל כאלו כבר הגיע והיה" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The long-standing either/or (past miracle or future war) is shown to be licensed by the poem's grammar. The text is neither purely one nor the other, and the exegetes' hard choices show where the grammar pinches.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The macro analysis notes the tense shift and the commentaries show the split, but no source connects the two or points out Radak's forced reading of וַיְהִי.

## OBSERVATION 4

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** whole psalm (vv. 2–9) with Exod 15

**Claim:** Diffing Ps 76 against the Song of the Sea shows a shared lexicon run in reverse order. Exod 15 moves from victory, through nations frozen in terror (15:16 "יִדְּמוּ כָּאָבֶן"), to the sanctuary as goal (15:17 "מָכוֹן לְשִׁבְתְּךָ… מִקְּדָשׁ"). Ps 76 *begins* with the sanctuary (v. 3), then gives the victory (vv. 4–7), then the stilled earth (v. 9), then the nations. Beyond the known "horse and chariot" and אַדִּיר, it shares נוֹרָא (Exod 15:11 "נוֹרָא תְהִלֹּת"; Ps 76:8, 13) and a name-line ending in שְׁמוֹ (Exod 15:3 "יְהוָה שְׁמוֹ"; Ps 76:2 "גָּדוֹל שְׁמוֹ"). Pointedly, where the Song names God "אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה" (15:3), Ps 76's God breaks מִלְחָמָה itself (v. 4).

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — every phrase can be quoted. That the reversal is deliberate is interpretive.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Exod 15:1, 21 horse/rider; אדיר ↔ Exod 15:10–11 (Macro; Micro v. 5, v. 7).
- Exod 15:3 "יְהוָה אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה יְהוָה שְׁמוֹ"; 15:11 "נֶאְדָּר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ נוֹרָא תְהִלֹּת"; 15:16 "יִדְּמוּ כָּאָבֶן"; 15:17 "מָכוֹן לְשִׁבְתְּךָ" (general scholarship; stake-in-print).
- Correction: Micro cites "נִשְׁמַת אַפּוֹ (Exod 15:8)." The actual Exod 15:8 wording is "וּבְרוּחַ אַפֶּיךָ" (general knowledge). The link to אַפֶּךָ v. 8 holds, but not the quoted phrase.
- Custom of reciting Ps 76 on the seventh day of Passover (Deep Research, Liturgical Usage). The seventh day is the day the Song of the Sea is read.

**Payoff (what clicks):** The Passover custom rests on more than one phrase. Ps 76 is a Song of the Sea whose arc starts where Exod 15 ends. "Warrior-God abolishes war" becomes a deliberate revision of the "man of war" title.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier limits the Exodus link to horse/chariot, אדיר and גערה. The reversed architecture and the אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה / שִׁבַּר מִלְחָמָה inversion are not noted.

## OBSERVATION 5

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 2, 4, 6 (with Ezek 38–39; Ps 48)

**Claim:** Radak's reading of Ps 76 as the war of Gog has a lexical basis that neither he nor the dossier states. Ezek 38:23 closes the Gog oracle with "וְהִתְגַּדִּלְתִּי… וְנוֹדַעְתִּי לְעֵינֵי גּוֹיִם רַבִּים," using the same two roots as Ps 76:2 (נוֹדָע… גָּדוֹל). Ezek 39:3 strikes Gog's bow and arrows "from your hand" (cf. v. 4 bow, v. 6 hands not found). Ezek 39:9 has Israel burn "מָגֵן… בְּקֶשֶׁת וּבְחִצִּים" (cf. v. 4 מָגֵן, רִשְׁפֵי קָשֶׁת = arrows per Rashi). The opening pair ידע (Niphal) + גדל also opens Ps 48, the sister Zion song (48:2 "גָּדוֹל יְהוָה," 48:4 "נוֹדַע לְמִשְׂגָּב"). This suggests a stock diction for the nations' assault on Zion.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the parallels are quotable. They show shared diction, not proven dependence.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Radak on vv. 2, 7, 11, 13: Gog u-Magog reading (Traditional Commentaries).
- Micro v. 2 cites Ezek 38:23 for the Niphal of ידע only.
- Ezek 38:23; 39:3 "וְהִכֵּיתִי קַשְׁתְּךָ מִיַּד שְׂמֹאולֶךָ וְחִצֶּיךָ מִיַּד יְמִינְךָ אַפִּיל"; 39:9 (general scholarship).
- Ps 48:2, 4 (general scholarship).
- Rashi on v. 4: רִשְׁפֵי = arrows (Traditional Commentaries).
- Liturgical connector: Ezek 38:18–39:16 is the haftarah of Shabbat Ḥol ha-Moʿed Sukkot, and Ps 76 is a Sukkot psalm of the day (general knowledge; Deep Research).

**Payoff (what clicks):** Radak's eschatological reading is shown to be textually motivated. The Sukkot setting gains a second anchor beyond סֻכּוֹ: the festival's Gog haftarah and its psalm share bow, shield, hands and the "known/magnified" opening.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier cites Ezek 38:23 only for its verb form. It connects neither the גדל root, nor Ezek 39's bow/hand/shield, nor the haftarah to Radak's reading or to the Sukkot custom.

## OBSERVATION 6

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3, 7, 9–10, 12–13 (with Pss 9–10)

**Claim:** The vocabulary of Ps 76 corresponds point for point to the petitions and complaints of the Ps 9–10 acrostic:
- The wicked crouches "כְּאַרְיֵה בְסֻכֹּה" to seize the poor (עָנִי, 10:9); in Ps 76 God's own סֻכּוֹ is the lair (v. 3).
- God "rebuked nations" (9:6 גָּעַרְתָּ גוֹיִם) ↔ v. 7 גְּעָרָה.
- God is "known by the judgment he executed" (9:17 "נוֹדַע יְהוָה מִשְׁפָּט עָשָׂה") ↔ v. 2 נוֹדָע + v. 10 מִשְׁפָּט.
- Pairs of דין and משפט (9:5) ↔ vv. 9–10.
- "Arise" (9:20 קוּמָה) ↔ v. 10 בְּקוּם.
- "Set terror (מוֹרָה, which BDB takes as ≈ מוֹרָא) for them" (9:21) ↔ vv. 12–13.
- Zion-dweller (9:12 "יֹשֵׁב צִיּוֹן") ↔ v. 3.
- Care for the ענוים (9:19 ketiv; 10:17).

Malbim, glossing Ps 76:13, reaches straight for Ps 10:18's closing line, "בַּל יוֹסִיף עוֹד לַעֲרֹץ אֱנוֹשׁ מִן הָאָרֶץ."

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the shared cluster. CONJECTURE that Ps 76 was composed or placed as a narrated answer to that lament: the two psalms belong to different collections (Davidic vs. Asaphite).

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Ps 10:9 בְסֻכֹּה (lion) and Ps 9:6 גָּעַרְתָּ גוֹיִם (Concordance); BDB סֹךְ lists ψ 10:9.
- BDB דין cites ψ 9:5; BDB מוֹרָה ψ 9:21 as variant of מוֹרָא (Lexicon).
- Ps 9:19 ketiv (ענוים) (Concordance, נצח).
- Ps 9:12, 9:17, 9:20, 10:17, 10:18 wording (general scholarship).
- Malbim on 76:13: "ובל יוסיף עוד לערוץ אנוש מן הארץ" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The lion-lair of v. 3 gains a moral edge. Everywhere else the lion in its covert preys on the poor; here the one lair whose lion hunts the predators is the one from which God rises "to save all the lowly." Ps 76 reads as the enacted answer to "Arise… set terror for them… do not forget the lowly."

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier lists Ps 10:9 only as a lexical parallel for סֹךְ. It never assembles the full Ps 9–10 cluster, and it never notices that Malbim's gloss on v. 13 already quotes that psalm.

## OBSERVATION 7

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3, 9, 12–13

**Claim:** The psalm never says "Jerusalem." It names the city by only half of the midrashic etymology that Radak cites on this very verse: Shem called it שָׁלֵם, Abraham called it יִרְאֶה (Gen 22:14), and God fused them into יְרוּשָׁלַיִם. The other half is scattered through the poem as the ירא Leitwort. The exact consonantal string יראה even appears at v. 9 (אֶרֶץ יָרְאָה, "fear," not Abraham's "see"). Chomat Anakh makes the point explicitly on v. 3: Jerusalem is "יראה שלם," and here the יראה has been removed so that only שלם remains. Perhaps v. 12's לַמּוֹרָא, which Malbim glosses as "the *place* and source of fear" (mem-locative), also carries a sound of Moriah, the temple mount. Midrashic tradition on Gen 22:2 derives Moriah from yirʾah.

**Confidence:** CONJECTURE for any authorial design. The consonantal facts and the commentators' statements are established. The יראה overlap is consonantal only: two different roots (ראה / ירא).

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Radak on v. 3: Shem → Shalem, Abraham → Yireh, God → Yerushalayim (Traditional Commentaries).
- Chomat Anakh on v. 3: "ירושלם הוא יראה שלם וסילק היראה ונשאר שלם" (Traditional Commentaries).
- v. 9 יָרְאָה (consonants יראה); Gen 22:14 "יְהוָה יִרְאֶה" (general knowledge).
- Malbim on v. 12: "למורא למקום היראה ומקורה"; Malbim Beur Hamilot on מ-prefixed nouns as place (Traditional Commentaries).
- 2 Chr 3:1 "בְּהַר הַמּוֹרִיָּה" (general knowledge).

**Payoff (what clicks):** This gives a second, tradition-internal motive for the archaic "Salem" beyond the pun on shalom. The Leitwort of fear completes the city's missing name, so that Shalem at the start and fear/Moriah at the end together spell out Jerusalem.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier treats the Salem pun and the ירא Leitwort as separate devices. It does not connect them through the Shalem + Yireh etymology that two of its own commentators supply.

## OBSERVATION 8

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3, 5

**Claim:** The form מֵהַרְרֵי occurs only three times in the Tanakh. One occurrence, Song 4:8, sets it in exactly Ps 76's pairing: "מִמְּעֹנוֹת אֲרָיוֹת מֵהַרְרֵי נְמֵרִים," lions' dens (מְעוֹנָה) in parallel with predators' mountains. Ps 76 has מְעוֹנָתוֹ (v. 3) and then מֵהַרְרֵי טָרֶף (v. 5). This attested collocation of den and predator-mountains supports MT's "mountains of prey" against the LXX's "eternal mountains" (הררי עד). A caution: the third occurrence, Num 23:7 "מֵהַרְרֵי קֶדֶם," shows the "ancient mountains" type also exists.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the counts are computed and the parallel can be quoted. It raises the plausibility of MT but does not settle the crux.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- מהררי: 3× in Tanakh (Ps 76:5; Num 23:7; Song 4:8) (Computed Distributional Facts).
- Song 4:8 wording (Concordance, ומעונתו).
- LXX ὀρέων αἰωνίων / Vorlage הררי עד (Micro v. 5; Deep Research).
- Radak on v. 5: "ההרים ששוכנים בהם החיות הטורפים יקראו הררי טרף" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The lion reading of vv. 3–5 gains independent lexical support. Radak's gloss turns out to describe a real biblical collocation, and MT's "harder" reading looks less like a corruption.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** Song 4:8 appears in the dossier only as a stray concordance hit for מעון. Its מהררי half, which bears on the text-critical crux, goes unmentioned.

## OBSERVATION 9

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 3, 5–7 (with Nahum)

**Claim:** Nahum, the Bible's anti-Assyrian poem, concentrates the vocabulary of Ps 76's central strophe:
- גּוֹעֵר בַּיָּם (1:4) ↔ v. 7 גְּעָרָה.
- The lion's מְעוֹן with טֶרֶף, and "וּמְעֹנֹתָיו טְרֵפָה" (2:12–13) ↔ vv. 3, 5.
- 3:18 "נָמוּ רֹעֶיךָ מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר יִשְׁכְּנוּ אַדִּירֶיךָ… עַל־הֶהָרִים." This is the only other occurrence of the form נמו in the Tanakh, addressed explicitly to the king of Assyria and paired with Assyria's אַדִּירִים on "the mountains."

Ps 76 gives אַדִּיר to God (v. 5) and leaves the enemy only נָמוּ (v. 6). Nineveh's den, which Nahum mocks as gone ("אַיֵּה מְעוֹן אֲרָיוֹת"), is replaced by Zion as the real lair.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE for the cluster. Direction of dependence unknown.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- נמו 2× in Tanakh: Ps 76:6; Nah 3:18 (Computed Distributional Facts; Concordance, נום).
- Nah 1:4 גּוֹעֵר (Concordance, גערה); Nah 2:12 מְעוֹן אֲרָיוֹת (Figurative, Lion/Den); BDB טרף lists Na 2:13, 14.
- LXX superscription πρὸς τὸν Ἀσσύριον (Micro v. 1).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The Assyrian reading of the psalm (LXX, Rashi, Malbim) gains a lexical basis independent of 2 Kgs 19. The אדיר/אבירי sound-play of vv. 5–6 becomes pointed: the word that Nahum gives to Assyria's sleeping nobles, Ps 76 reclaims for God.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier cites Nah 2:12, Nah 1:4 and Nah 3:18 in three different places. It never observes that they converge, or that the one other נמו is addressed to Assyria.

## OBSERVATION 10

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** v. 4 (with 2 Kgs 19:32 / Isa 37:33)

**Claim:** Isaiah's oracle to Sennacherib promises that the king "shall not shoot an arrow *there* nor come before it with a *shield*": "וְלֹא־יוֹרֶה שָׁם חֵץ וְלֹא־יְקַדְּמֶנָּה מָגֵן." Ps 76:4 answers with the same trio of emphatic "there," arrow and shield: "שָׁמָּה שִׁבַּר רִשְׁפֵי־קָשֶׁת מָגֵן." Rashi and Meiri gloss רִשְׁפֵי as arrows. The prophetic negative ("he will not shoot there") becomes the psalm's positive ("there he broke the arrows").

**Confidence:** PROBABLE that this lexical triad underlies the Assyrian attribution in the tradition. CONJECTURE that the poet alluded to the oracle. This is an echo, not a quotation.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- 2 Kgs 19:32 and Isa 37:33 wording (Concordance, מגן).
- v. 4 שָׁמָּה… מָגֵן (psalm text).
- Rashi on v. 4: "שֶׁל סַנְחֵרִיב… חִצִּים שֶׁהַקֶּשֶׁת מְעוֹפֵף… שֶׁלֹּא מָצִינוּ אוֹיֵב נוֹפֵל בִּירוּשָׁלַיִם אֶלָּא הוּא"; Meiri: "רשפי קשת פירושו חצי קשת" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** The emphatic שָׁמָּה gets its point: it answers Isaiah's שָׁם. The LXX superscription and Rashi's certainty rest on more than the overnight deaths of 2 Kgs 19:35.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The Sennacherib link is usually argued from 2 Kgs 19:35 (the sleep of death). The shield/arrow/"there" match with 19:32 sits unremarked in the concordance.

## OBSERVATION 11

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 4, 6–7 (with Hos 2:20)

**Claim:** Ps 76:4 reproduces Hosea's covenant-of-peace list with the contiguous words "וְחֶרֶב וּמִלְחָמָה." Hos 2:20 reads "וְקֶשֶׁת וְחֶרֶב וּמִלְחָמָה אֶשְׁבּוֹר," with the same verb שבר and the same climb from bow to abstract "war." The psalm inserts מָגֵן and upgrades "bow" to "Reshephs of the bow." Hosea continues "וְהִשְׁכַּבְתִּים לָבֶטַח": disarmament lets *Israel* lie down in safety. Ps 76 continues into sleep too, but it is the *enemy's* sleep (נָמוּ שְׁנָתָם, נִרְדָּם). The same formula produces opposite sleepers.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the contiguous match and the shared verb can be quoted. The sleep inversion is an interpretive reading of adjacent material.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**

## OBSERVATION 12

**Type:** CONNECTION

**Primary verse(s):** vv. 4, 9

**Claim:** The Deuteronomistic rest formula "וְהָאָרֶץ שָׁקְטָה מִמִּלְחָמָה" (Josh 14:15; also 11:23) is split across the psalm. Its last word, מִלְחָמָה, is the climactic abstract noun that God breaks at v. 4 (sealed by Selah). Its first words, אֶרֶץ… שָׁקָטָה, return at v. 9 ("אֶרֶץ יָרְאָה וְשָׁקָטָה"). The formula's usual narrative sense is that "the land had rest from war" after Israel's conquest. Here the war was ended by God's breaking of weapons, not Israel's, and אֶרֶץ is ambiguous between "the land" and "the earth." Rashi reads v. 9 as Eretz Israel, Radak as "the lands of the nations," so the commentators divide along exactly this ambiguity.

**Confidence:** PROBABLE — the formula can be quoted. The split is a reading of two verses together, not a contiguous citation.

**Anchors (every fact this rests on, each with its footing):**
- Josh 14:15 "וְהָאָרֶץ שָׁקְטָה מִמִּלְחָמָה" (Concordance, שקטה); Josh 11:23 (general knowledge).
- Micro v. 9 on שקט as Judges' rest formula (without the ממלחמה link).
- Rashi on v. 9 "אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל… אָז שָׁקְטָה"; Radak "ארץ העמים יראה… ושקטה עוד להלחם בישראל" (Traditional Commentaries).

**Payoff (what clicks):** vv. 4 and 9 are bound as the two halves of one idiom. The land's "rest from war" is redefined as creation's hush after war itself has been broken, and the land/earth ambiguity becomes the hinge of the psalm's universalizing.

**Novelty of the linkage (why a learned reader of Psalms has not already made this connection):** The dossier connects שקטה to Judges but misses the fuller Joshua form, whose final word מִלְחָמָה is the climax of v. 4.

## OBSERVATION 14
