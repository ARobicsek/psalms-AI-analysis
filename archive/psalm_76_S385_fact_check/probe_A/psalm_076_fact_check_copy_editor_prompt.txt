6. Verse 3: "*Salem* is Jerusalem's oldest name, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, "and Melchizedek, king of Salem" (Gen 14:18)."
   Claim: Salem is Jerusalem’s oldest attested name.
   Finding: The earliest external attestations have a longer name, not simply Salem. The identification of Genesis’s Salem with Jerusalem is traditional but cannot establish that “Salem” was the city’s oldest name.
   Evidence — Kelley Coblentz Bautch, “Toward the Reconstruction of a Sacred Landscape of the Judean Highlands,” Journal of Ancient Near Eastern Religions (https://brill.com/view/journals/jane/21/1/article-p1_1.xml): "According to the Egyptian Execration Texts ...    Evidence — Robert R. Cargill, “From Shalem to Jerusalem” (https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/first-person-from-shalem-to-jerusalem/): "An Egyptian execration (curse) text, dating to around 1800 B.C.E., lists Jerusalem’s name as Ru-ša-li-mum."
   Suggested fix: *Salem* is an ancient biblical name traditionally identified with Jerusalem, the city of Melchizedek: וּמַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם, “and Melchizedek, king of Salem” (Gen 14:18).

7. Verse 3: "Malbim's word-notes add that the מָעוֹן is often "the den of fearsome beasts of prey.""

   Evidence — Malbim, Beur Hamilot on Psalm 76:3, full entry: "הסוכה ארעי והמעון קבוע, ושם מעון בא לרוב על דברים הגיונים, או מעון חיתו טרף הנוראים"
   Suggested fix: Malbim’s word-notes add that מָעוֹן is a permanent dwelling, usually used of conceptual things, but can also mean “the den of fearsome beasts of prey.”

20. Verse 12: "The likeliest explanation is that the vow formula was too fixed for the Elohistic editor to touch, the same phrase found in the law כִּי־תִדֹּר נֶדֶר לַה׳ אֱלֹקֶיךָ, "when you vow a vow to YHWH your God" (Deut 23:22)."
   Claim: The vow formula is the same phrase, word for word, in Deuteronomy and the psalm.
   Finding: They are parallel vow formulas, not the same phrase: Deuteronomy addresses one person and says ‘your God’ in the singular; the psalm commands a group and uses the plural.
   Evidence — Deuteronomy 23:22; Psalm 76:12, Masoretic texts: "כִּי־תִדֹּר נֶדֶר לַיהֹוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ; נִדְרוּ וְשַׁלְּמוּ לַיהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם"
   Suggested fix: The likeliest explanation is that the vow formula was too fixed for the Elohistic editor to touch; a parallel formula appears in the law כִּי־תִדֹּר נֶדֶר לַה׳ אֱלֹקֶיךָ, "when you vow a vow to YHWH your God" (Deut 23:22).
