**Session 381 (2026-09-10)**: **EVERY QUOTED HEBREW LINE HAS BEEN PRINTING ITS OWN FULL STOP AT THE HEAD OF THE SENTENCE** — 271 lines across 28 guides; Word's own measurement API lied about which side the mark was on, and the fix's first draft broke the prose it was written to protect
- **Trigger**: the author, on the Ps 74 DOCX — *"for quotations, the hebrew punctuation is usually at the beginning rather than the end of the sentence. please fix."*
- **THE REPORT WAS EXACT AND THE MECHANISM WAS ONE CHARACTER WALK.** `DocumentGenerator._segment_by_script` classifies text character by character and puts every non-Hebrew character in an LTR run. On a line that is *nothing but* quoted Hebrew — a poem line, a piyyut colon, a liturgical clause — that splits the sentence's own terminal `.` / `,` / `;` / `?` off into its own LTR run; Word's bidi engine then resolves the mark at the paragraph's **LTR** level and draws it at the **right edge** of the RTL island, hard against the line's **FIRST** word. Amichai's `בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת.` printed its period after `בְּעֶרֶב`, not after `שַׁבָּת`. **`_add_primarily_hebrew_line` has documented this exact hazard since it was written** — "segmenting it in an LTR paragraph would split the punctuation into its own LTR run" — but it only fires on lines carrying a **sof-pasuq**, and **not one of the affected lines has one**.
- **WORD'S OWN MEASUREMENT API LIES ABOUT BIDI ORDER, AND IT LIED IN THE REASSURING DIRECTION.** `Range.Information(wdHorizontalPositionRelativeToTextBoundary)` returns a **caret** position, not a glyph position: probed against `Documents/Psalm study guide/Psalm 74.docx` it reported the comma on the *correct* (left) side of `אֱלֹקִים` in a case the rendering proves wrong, and reported the first character of every paragraph at the left margin regardless. **Two rounds of reasoning were spent on numbers that were not measuring what they looked like.** Ground truth came instead from exporting the DOCX to PDF through Word and parsing the content stream's `Tm`/`Td`/`TJ` operators directly — pure Python + `zlib`, **$0** — where the Hebrew and Latin runs carry different font resources, so which side the mark sits on is a coordinate comparison and not a judgement call: **Hebrew run x=108.05, period drawn separately at x=155.83**. Screenshots of the rendered page were tried first and were **not decisive** — Hebrew glyph order cannot be read reliably off an image.
- **THE FIRST DRAFT WAS BROADER THAN THE BUG AND BROKE ORDINARY PROSE.** The rule "Hebrew present, no Latin letters → one RTL run" is right about a LINE and wrong about a markdown FRAGMENT: `_process_markdown_formatting` splits on emphasis first, so `the lot *(גּוֹרָל)*, and` yields the Latin-free fragment `` (גּוֹרָל), `` — and treating that as quoted Hebrew drags the **English** sentence's comma inside the RTL run, where it renders *before* the opening parenthesis. Found by instrumenting `_segment_by_script` and **building all 75 rebuildable guides for real** ($0), then diffing old-vs-new on the 13,892 unique fragments the build actually passes it. A first regression harness that fed raw markdown lines to the segmenter reported **1,674 changed lines and was simply wrong** — it swallowed the `>` and `*` markers the pipeline strips before the segmenter ever sees them.
- **Fix**: new `_is_hebrew_only_line`, taken **once per line** and handed down as `hebrew_line=` through `_add_inline_runs` / `_add_formatted_content`. `_add_paragraph_with_soft_breaks` needed its own care — it splits on emphasis **before** it splits on newlines, so its loop variable named `line` is a fragment; the verdict is taken at the top of the method instead. Three structural tests pin the ordering, and one asserts `hebrew_line=True` appears at **no** call site.
- **MIXED PROSE IS DELIBERATELY UNTOUCHED.** There the mark after a Hebrew term belongs to the **English** sentence (`address אֱלֹקִים, "God"` — 139 such spots in Ps 74 alone) and the island's right edge is exactly where an English reader expects it; those runs come out **byte-identical**.
